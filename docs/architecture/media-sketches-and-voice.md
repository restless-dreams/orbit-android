# Media, sketches and voice

- **Copies that take a while** (`map/MediaAdding.kt`, `map/MapDrops.kt`, and ideas pasted from another map with their media, `pasteIdeas`) follow their idea, not its id: `FollowedIdeas` holds the idea each is for, and the map's screen moves it, and each idea's `PictureTurns` turn, as a save says new text renamed it (`MapViewModel.follow`), so a copy lands under the id its idea has then, never on another idea that took the old one.
  An idea the map loses, in a save (but for the one it renamed), or as the map is drawn anew (undo, redo, a version brought back, a change from elsewhere), is removed: what was on its way to it is for no idea any more (`FollowedIdeas.removed` gives it an id no idea has, `PictureTurns.removed` ends its turn), so an idea renamed to that id after never gets it, and the copy says its idea is gone.
  Each goes on with an `update` or `addFiles` the page answers (`MapViewModel.change`): only then is "Added" said; an idea gone takes nothing, which is said, and the copy is taken back; a page that didn't answer leaves it for the tidying up, which deletes it once nothing points at it.
  Undo and redo take a rename back and make it again too (`UndoRenames`, kept by the very versions on the undo and redo stacks), so the presentation's path, the rehearsal and the cards go back with the idea's old text to its old id.
- **Pictures, videos and sounds full screen** (`media/`) are a window of their own over the app, so they cover the list beside the map on a tablet, take the keys and Back, and keep light system bars on the dark.
  Their bars are see-through from the app's theme (`Theme.Orbit`), as a private map's lock window's are:
  Android 15 and later make every window's so, and on 10 to 14 the theme does it without the deprecated `Window` bar colors.
  Back's predictive swipe shrinks them as it goes.
  - **Sharing** from the viewer (`ViewerSharing`) sends a clean copy, asks about a file Orbit can't clean as the very item it is, whatever page is on screen by the time it asks, and says so when no copy could be made; its questions live with the viewer, not the bar that hides with a tap.
  - **Pictures** are [Telephoto](https://github.com/saket/telephoto)'s zoomable images on the app's Coil: big ones are read a part at a time (subsampling), so they stay sharp zoomed in without filling memory, and its flick closes the viewer.
    The app builds Coil's image loader itself (`OrbitApplication.newImageLoader`): coil-gif's `AnimatedImageDecoder` plays animated GIFs and WebPs with Android's own `ImageDecoder`, at the size they're drawn, so a big GIF takes no more memory than the screen; Telephoto draws them whole, since an animation can't be read a part at a time.
    Before it, `StillAnimations` (`media/AnimatedPictures.kt`) decodes their first frame while Android's animations are off, unless a request asks to play them, as the viewer's play button does; that frame isn't kept in Coil's memory, so turning animations back on plays them at once.
    The map's page, which draws thumbnails too, gets an animation's first frame instead of its file (`page/FirstFrames.kt`, a PNG made once in the cache for each version of the file): a planet that moved would keep the page drawing, and the battery with it, for as long as it's on screen.
    The audience's screen, the room's browsers and web pages get the file itself.
    The gallery is the map's pictures, videos and sounds in the order the page reports its nodes, the outline's (`galleryOf`).
  - **Video files** play in Media3's ExoPlayer, with Media3's Compose surface and Material controls.
    The player is made when a video opens and released when it closes, so maps carry nothing of it.
    Release builds turn Media3's own logging off as Orbit starts (`OrbitApplication.onCreate`), whichever of its players plays first: its errors can name a map's folder or a video's site.
    The view that prints a map keeps the page's console out of the log too, as the map's page does (`QuietChrome`).
  - **Sounds** play in one ExoPlayer for the open map (`media/SoundPlayback.kt`), made when a sound first plays and released with the map's screen, so the small player over the map, the details, the editor, the viewer and the presentation all show the one sound playing, and it goes on as people fly around.
    Starting another sound stops the one before first, so one that can't play never leaves the last one playing under its "can't be played".
    The player is behind `SoundPlayer`, which the tests play with a stand-in.
    It holds the phone's audio focus, and pauses when another app takes it, when headphones come out, and when Orbit leaves the screen.
- **Sketches** (`sketch/`, `map/Sketching.kt`, `map/SketchOverlay.kt`) are drawn on the planet itself.
  The page brings the planet up close with Orbit's `spotlight`, in the room the sketch's toolbar leaves: the camera flies, the map dims under its style's sheet backdrop with the planet's circle cut out, and the page takes no input.
  It says where the circle rests (`MapMessage.Spotlight`, with the color it dims with, which the screen dims the bars with too), and again as the room changes, and the screen lays its canvas exactly over that circle, the planet's own color for paper.
  The description that Done brings up is over the whole window (`SketchDescription`), above the keyboard, rather than in the map's part of it: on a phone on its side the keyboard leaves less room than the map has under the top bar, so it lies over the dimmed bars, in one row there.
  Done keeps a stroke still being drawn as it is (`SketchSession.finishInk`, which closing calls too before it asks, and a first touch down calls for a stroke let go unseen), writes the drawing as a new SVG in the map's folder (`MapRepository.addSketch`, `sketch-` and the moment), has the page load it (`pictureShown`: one it can't, or a page that stopped, keeps it off the idea, and the canvas stays to try again), sets it as the idea's picture in one `update` the page answers (`MapViewModel.change`), waits for the page to say the planet shows it, and only then lifts the canvas and lets the page fly back (`endSpotlight`), so the planet never shows without its sketch.
  An `update` the page doesn't say it took (an idea gone, a page stopped or too late) keeps the canvas up too, its drawing whole, so the canvas never closes on a sketch kept nowhere.
  Orbit holds the planet in place under the canvas through that edit.
  A picture that landed on the idea while it was sketched on (a copy chosen before that took a while: its `PictureTurns` turn moved, or the idea's picture isn't the one the sketch opened over) is asked about as Done is tapped (`SketchUi.confirming`), and replaced only once the person says so, as a picture of another kind is.
  A page that stops, or doesn't load, takes the planet with it, not the drawing (`Sketching.pageStopped`): the `SketchSession` waits, with what the description says, its canvas and tools gone while the map's place says the sketch comes back (`SketchUi.held`), and Back asks before strokes go, as on the planet.
  Load again brings the planet up close again on the new page once it has drawn the map (`pageReady`), the drawing back on it; a page too slow to hold it yet says `end` at once, and while its idea is there the drawing waits and is brought up again every half second, ten seconds at most (`bringBackLater`), Back asking before its strokes go meanwhile; an idea gone meanwhile ends the sketch, saying so.
  The waiting drawing lives only in the map screen's memory, as a sketch under way always has: Android stopping Orbit's own process meanwhile loses it, though the notice says it comes back.
  - **The canvas** draws the strokes kept as a layer of their own, and the stroke being drawn over them on each move, from every sample the touch made since the last (`pointerInteropFilter`), dispatched as they come (`requestUnbufferedDispatch`), and a frame or two ahead with AndroidX's motion prediction (`MotionEventPredictor`).
    `SketchSession` turns touches into strokes, and is plain state the JVM tests drive: the palm rejection (a finger draws nothing while a stylus is near, or for `STYLUS_GRACE` after; a stroke a finger or palm was drawing goes when the stylus comes down; `FLAG_CANCELED` takes a stroke back), the whole-stroke eraser, and undo.
    A stroke's outline (`StrokeOutline`) follows its line at each point's width, from a stylus's pressure and lean or a finger's speed, round at its ends and where it turns too sharply for its sides to follow, a run of its own from there; the runs turn the same way, so the nonzero rule fills them as one.
  - **The SVG is the sketch** (`SketchSvg`): its round paper, and each stroke as its filled outline, a smooth curve through the outline's corners in tenths of a unit, clipped to the paper.
    Nothing else is kept, so nothing has to survive beside the drawing: every element and attribute is one the outgoing cleaner keeps, so a sketch goes out as the same drawing (the cleaner writes it again, with an XML declaration at its top) and reopens on another device, and the eraser and undo work on the outlines as they were read.
    A web page (what browsers in the room are served too), Orbit's own Print and PDF from the Export panel (`WebPage.write` with `print`), and a TV take it as the PNG it draws instead (`page/WebPage.kt`, `SketchPictures.png`, reading no more of a file named as a sketch than `SketchSvg.LARGEST`, and drawing a page's at half or a quarter of its 1024 pixels only when it was drawn on after the page counted it and would come to more, `sketchPng`); only the zip and Show (through `OutgoingCopies`) carry the SVG, since Orbit puts no SVG inside a page as a data:
    URL.
    The class `orbit-sketch` marks Orbit's own: a later version that draws sketches otherwise names them otherwise, and this one shows them as pictures.
    Only on the drawing's own `<svg>` element, after nothing but an XML declaration and comments (`SketchSvg.startsAsSketch`): a photo whose text happens to hold those words is a photo.
    AndroidX Ink was weighed for the canvas: its native library would add about 5 MB to every APK that carries all of Android's processors, and its strokes are meshes with input to replay, not a drawing to keep.
  - **The app's own views** draw sketches through `media/SketchPictures.kt`, a Coil decoder for Orbit's sketches alone (Coil reads no other SVG), as the canvas draws them; the details and the editor show them whole rather than cropped.
    It claims a file only when it starts as a sketch does, so any other picture goes on to Coil's own decoders.
  - **Earlier versions** show a sketch as any picture: it's an `image` like any other, and `.svg` is among the kinds every release from 0.3.0 keeps and carries in zips, sending, backups and the folder.
    Orbit 0.4.0's own views don't draw SVGs, so its details and full screen show the picture's problem, as for any SVG, while its map shows the sketch.
- **Talking ideas onto a map** (`voice/Listening.kt`, `map/Talk.kt`) listens through the phone's `SpeechRecognizer`, one made for each talk and destroyed as the talk ends, so a speech service that answers late, or never, gives nothing to a talk that's over and keeps no microphone; it's behind `Recognizer`, which the tests drive with a stand-in.
  What's said before each pause joins the map as one `AddNodes` change, one at a time, so each save is known as the talk's (`SpokenIdeas`): only the first keeps the map for undo, and the talk's Undo is offered once its last ideas are saved, taking back the talk while it's still the last change.
  The microphone is one thing's at a time: the recorder, a presentation, a study session and the map's lock each end a talk as they start (`MapViewModel.talkable`), and a talk can't begin while another finishes its last idea.
  The talk's screen going ends a talk going on as those do (`Talk.close`, which cancels it before letting the speech service go), so the map still hears it's over (`MapViewModel.stopTalking`) and offers its Undo.
- **Voice memos** are `MediaRecorder`'s (`media/VoiceRecorder.kt`):
  AAC, one channel, 48 kHz at 96 kbps, into a take in the cache that joins the map once it's saved (`MapRepository.addRecording`).
  Its limit, the room the map has left, is kept by the recorder's own clock:
  `setMaxFileSize` would reserve room for an index as big as the limit allows, 300 KB of nothing in every memo.
  The microphone is asked for only as a memo starts, and held only while it records.
  A take starts, or goes on, only with the phone's sound (transient exclusive audio focus): one denied it, during a call, records nothing and says why, and a take paused or done stays as it was.
  A recorder that stops partway, on an error of its own or as it pauses or goes on (an `IllegalStateException`, or the plain `RuntimeException` Android's recorder throws for its other errors), is stopped and let go, which ends the take's file, and the take recorded so far is done, with `audio_recording_failed` said (`VoiceRecorder.stopped`): only Discard, or recording over it, deletes it, and only one too short to hear goes at once, as at Finish (`SHORTEST_TAKE_MILLISECONDS`), since a take's file holds its header from the start.
  The take plays back in the phone's `MediaPlayer` (`map/TakePlayer.kt`), with transient audio focus while it plays: it pauses when that's lost, ducking included, waits while a call holds it, and pauses as headphones come out.
  A take outlasts a screen made again, as for a new language or font size (`rememberVoiceRecorder`): its file's name, time and waveform are saved state, and the screen that goes lets the microphone go, which leaves the take whole in its file, back as a finished take.
  A sound from the phone is known by its first bytes before its type (`soundKind`), since Android's own types call an `.m4a` "audio/mpeg".
  - **YouTube and Vimeo** play in their own embedded players, in a web view apart from the map's: no channel to the app, no files or content, JavaScript only because the players need it, no third-party cookies, and nothing loads in it but the players' frames (a content policy says so too); other pages open in a tab.
    YouTube's player refuses to play ("Error 153") for a page that doesn't say where it is, so the player's page is loaded with `loadDataWithBaseURL` as the app's id, `https://com.hyuchia.orbit`, as YouTube asks of apps, and its frame's requests carry that Referer.
    Vimeo plays its videos protected, so its player may use the phone's protected-content system, and nothing else a browser offers.
- **Pictures and videos to the audience** go as commands (`AudienceCommand.Media`, `Playback`, `Seek` and `CloseMedia` in `page/Protocol.kt`), which the audience's page turns into Orbit's media viewer (`openMedia` with `startAt`, `playMedia`, `pauseMedia`, `seekMedia`, `unmuteMedia`).
  The phone reckons where a video is from what it sends (`present/ShownMedia.kt`); a second screen's and a TV's word on their video (`mediaState`) corrects it, and a browser that joins halfway opens the video where the phone reckons it is.
  A video at its end, played there or paused right there, starts over as it plays: the phone sends `Seek` to 0 before `Playback`, so every screen starts it over alike, and the audience's page reckons it the same way (`playback` in `web/audience.ts`), for the sound a browser's "Tap for sound" turns on.
  - **Sounds stay on the phone:** they play from the presenter's phone, so they never go to the audience's screens.
  - **The room in browsers is the map's, not its screen's** (`BrowserRoom`, which `MapViewModel` keeps, with the presenter's choice of what goes as it is, `presentingAsTheyAre`): a presentation's screen made again, for a new language or text size, finds it as it was, its address, code and members; it stops as the presentation ends, or with the map.
    Its browsers say over the map that they're finding the phone again while their connection is down (`.audience__status`).
  - **A page that stops during a presentation** shows its problem over the presentation, with Load again (`PageProblemNotice`), and the page loaded again presents at the idea on screen (`load` sends `Present`, `View` and `Focus`).
    A second screen's page that stops or doesn't load is the audience's trouble (`Audience.trouble`, `ScreenTrouble`), which the presenter's bar shows with Load it again; so is a message Cast says didn't reach the TV, with Send it again (`CastAudience`, which hears each `sendMessage`'s result).
    Either loads the presentation there again (`PresentationControl.loadScreenAgain`: a new page in the `Presentation`, or the hello and language to the TV again), then catches it up as a screen that just arrived; the phone's own Load again does it too, as the app's pages share their process and stop together.
  - **The map changing while it's presented** (a save typed as the presentation started, a change from elsewhere, the map drawn again from its file) reaches every audience: each page message with the map's state recomputes the presentation's order from the path (`MapViewModel.presentationFollowsMap`, `PresentationControl.mapChanged`), its renamed ideas followed first (`renamed`, from `follow`), and an audience shown other text than the map's now gets the map again at the idea on screen.
  - **A video moved before it knows its length** opens again from there (`seek` in `web/audience.ts`):
    Orbit's player would otherwise put it back at the time it opened at once its length is known.
  - **Videos seek** because both servers answer for part of a file.
    The phone's web server answers a `Range` request with 206 and `Content-Range`, reading only that part (`nearby/ByteRanges.kt`).
    In the WebView, maps' files answer the same way (`page/MapFiles.kt`), holding the whole file:
    WebView skips to the part a request asks for by itself, in any stream an app answers with.
