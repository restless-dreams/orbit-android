# Between devices

- **Maps between devices are sealed** (`nearby/OfferEncryption.kt`), with standard algorithms from the JDK and Android:
  - A device taking maps makes a P-256 key pair each launch, and gives the public key in its advertisement (`key`, with the `version` of offers it takes) and at `GET /key`.
  - The sender makes a fresh pair, agrees a secret with ECDH, and derives its keys with HKDF-SHA256, salted with both public keys.
    What's sealed travels in AES-256-GCM chunks of 64 KiB, each with its own nonce, authenticating how many chunks there are and where each goes.
    Only the sender's public key and the version travel in the clear.
  - An offer starts with a hello (`POST /offer`): the sender's name and its commitment to its half of the check code, sealed.
    The receiver answers with its own half, the sender reveals its half (`POST /offer/<id>`), and both show the code, which comes from the agreed keys and both halves: no one in between can pick keys until the codes match.
  - The map and its pictures go (`POST /offer/<id>/map`), sealed with a key of their own, only once the person sending says the other device shows the same code.
    So a device that answers in another's place on the Wi-Fi gets nothing of the map.
    An offer holds its place under the limits from its hello on, and the receiver refuses a map it isn't waiting for, or has no room for, before reading any of it: room for it once, as it opens straight from the connection and unpacks into the files its offer waits in (`readBundle`, looking at the room as it goes), besides what the maps arriving with it will take.
    Nothing of it counts until all of it has opened, its last chunk checked (`readToEnd`); a map cut short on its way keeps its offer, to come whole while it has time.
    Its map has to start coming within `CONFIRMING_LIFETIME_MILLISECONDS` of the code showing: an upload under way then may finish, and one cut short after that ends the offer.
    A map that doesn't become an offer, whatever stops it, leaves no file, and its offer ends; one the storage runs out of room for as it opens stops there, and so does one declined here or cancelled there as it arrives, at its next piece (`WhileOffered`).
    Closing the send sheet while the map goes closes its connection at once (`deliver`).
  - An offer takes one answer (`NearbyHost.claim`):
    Open, Keep both, Decline or its time running out, whichever comes first, and a second tap does nothing (`Accepted.AnsweredAlready`).
    A map taken in is taken in on the host's scope, so a screen made again meanwhile never leaves it half answered.
    A map that arrived waits for its answer `ANSWER_LIFETIME_MILLISECONDS`, three minutes, as long as every release's sender waits for it, and is then declined; a sender waits as long as any release keeps a map waiting (`ANSWER_WAIT_MILLISECONDS`: Orbit 0.5.0 and earlier keep one ten minutes), so it hears the other device decline it then, and never says no answer came while the map can still be opened there.
    A Decline tapped on a map on its way as it arrives declines the offer it became, unless another answer came first (`decline(Incoming)`), so the sender never hears "declined" of a map that can still be opened.
  - A sender's waits are measured in time, however long each request in them takes (`SendTimes`, `waitForState`): the answer `ANSWER_WAIT_MILLISECONDS` from the map's arrival, the codes `CONFIRMING_LIFETIME_MILLISECONDS` from showing.
    Each ask of where an offer is has ten seconds in all, from connecting to the last of its answer, the key, the hello and the half of the code a minute each, and the word that the map won't come five seconds, as sending stops.
    A request out of time, or as sending is cancelled, closes its connection there and then (`request`, `closedOnCancel`), so a device that stops answering, or answers a byte at a time, holds up neither a wait nor the sheet closing.
  - Where an offer is (`GET /offer/<id>`: confirming, waiting, opened or declined) comes with the receiver's proof, an HMAC with a key of the offer's own (`stateProof`), so a sender whose codes matched believes "opened" or "declined" only from that device.
    The receiver says it proves its answers in the first eight bytes of its half of the check code (`provesStates`): the code both people compare covers them, so no one in between can take the mark off without the codes differing, as TLS 1.3 marks its server's random against downgrades.
    Orbit 0.3.0 and 0.4.0 send a half all random and no proof, and a sender takes their answers at their word, as before; they read past the proof.
    Answered offers are said for ten minutes, the newest 256 (`OfferStates`).
  - Version 2 is the one spoken.
    Orbit 0.2.0 and earlier (version 1) sent the map first: neither side offers to the other, and each says which device to update.
- **Versions between the parts that live apart.**
  Each pair says which version it speaks, and handles the other being older or newer:

  | Between | What each side says | When one is older | When one is newer |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | The phone and a TV's receiver (`present/ScreenVersions.kt`, `web/receiver-protocol.ts`) | The phone says hello first, with its protocol (`AUDIENCE_PROTOCOL`, 2, which `ProtocolTest` holds to the pages' `RECEIVER_PROTOCOL`) and its version. The receiver answers with its protocol, the newest map format its Orbit reads, and its Orbit's commit. Apps 0.1.1 and 0.2.0 say nothing (protocol 1), and a receiver that doesn't answer within 3 seconds counts as protocol 1 with format 1. | A receiver older than a command doesn't get it (`AudienceCommand.protocol`). For a map written for a newer format than its Orbit reads, the presenter hears that parts may look different on the TV, which still shows it as well as it can. Below `OLDEST_RECEIVER_PROTOCOL`, the presenter hears that the TV can't show this app's presentations yet. | The receiver keeps following every older protocol's commands, so an older app never notices. |
  | Two phones sending a map (`nearby/`) | The offers' `version`, in the advertisement and at `GET /key`, and `X-Orbit-Version` on each offer. The device taking maps answers `GET /key` and a hello with the newest map format it reads (`mapFormat`), the kinds of file it keeps (`mediaTypes`) and the most a map sent to it may be, sealed (`mapSize`); Orbit 0.3.0 says none of them, 0.4.0 all but the last, and both read past them. Its half of the check code says whether it proves what it says about an offer (`provesStates`); 0.3.0 and 0.4.0 don't. | Another version of offers: neither sends to the other. The sender says which device to update, and the device taking maps refuses an older offer before reading any of it. The same version, an older Orbit: with the code, the sender says what won't open there (`olderThere`): the files of kinds it doesn't keep, which stay here (0.3.0 has no sounds, and keeps only a voice memo's `.mp4`), and a map this device edits that's written for a newer format than the other device reads, which that one only shows. What it says of the offer, unproven, is taken at its word. A map bigger than it takes, 64 MiB for 0.3.0 and 0.4.0, isn't offered past its hello: the sender says to update Orbit there. | The same, the other way round. |
  | The phone and browsers presenting or opening a map (`nearby/BrowserPresenting.kt`) | Nothing: the phone serves their pages itself, so they're always its own version. | | |
  | The app and its files (`data/StorageLayout.kt`) | `maps/.layout.json`: the layout (`LAYOUT_VERSION`, 2) and the oldest app that may change files in it (`LAYOUT_COMPATIBLE`, 1). | Moved to this layout step by step, a copy of every map's text kept first. | Read but left unchanged, unless this app is at least the oldest one allowed to change them. |
  | The app and a map's text (Orbit's format) | `schemaVersion`, in the frontmatter or at the top of a YAML map; none means 1. | Upgraded as it's read, and written as the current version at the next save. | Opened read-only, with a note. Nothing in it is lost, and attributes Orbit doesn't know are always kept. |
  | The app and `meta.json` | Fields by name. | | Fields a newer version wrote stay when this one changes the file. |
  | A map's zip: Share, Open with, a map sent to a device (`data/MapBundles.kt`) | `orbit.json`: the zip's version (`bundle`, `BUNDLE_FORMAT`, 1), the oldest that reads it right (`compatible`, 1) and the app that made it (`app`). Zips from before 0.4.0 say none of it. | Read as it is: what it doesn't say reads as none. Orbit 0.4.0 and earlier refuse a zip that unpacks to more than 96 MiB, or holds a file over 64 MiB (`BUNDLE_SIZE_UP_TO_0_4_0`, `ZIP_ENTRY_LIMIT_UP_TO_0_4_0`): where such a zip is made, Export and the send sheet say to update Orbit wherever it's opened (`pastOlderZips`). | Read field by field: fields this version doesn't know are ignored, a field given another shape is lost alone, and files of kinds it doesn't know stay out, said. A zip whose `compatible` is above `BUNDLE_FORMAT` is refused, saying to update Orbit. |
  | A zip of every map (Export all) | `orbit-export.json`, the zip's first entry: its version (`export`, `EXPORT_FORMAT`, 1), `compatible`, `app`, the layout and the map format. Zips from before 0.4.0 have none. | Read as it is. | As for a map's zip, refused before any map comes out of it. |
  | Devices through the synced folder | The maps' markdown files, as above; `meta.json` stays on each device. Each device's record in `.orbit/devices/` (`data/FolderDevices.kt`): its Orbit, the newest map format it reads, the kinds of file it carries and how big a map's files may be there. Each device's answers in study mode, a record per map in `.orbit/study/<id>/` (`data/FolderStudy.kt`), with its `format` (`STUDY_FORMAT`, 1). Releases before records never read that folder, and releases before study records keep study on each device. | As for a map's text. Settings says what the older device can't open, and Home says it once for files the folder holds: update it. A study record from an older version is read as it is. | As for a map's text. Settings and Home say the same, from the other side: update this one. A study record or a device's record from a newer version is read for the fields this one knows, and this device's own keeps the rest, unless its `compatible` says this version would misread it: then it's left out. |
  | Android's backup | The files as they were, the layout record included. | As for the app's files. | As for the app's files. |
  | The app and GitHub's releases (the update check, `data/Updates.kt`) | `releases/latest`: its tag, whether it's a draft or a pre-release, and its page. | | A draft, a pre-release, a tag that isn't a version (with or without `v`), or a page that isn't HTTPS is ignored. |

  The receiver is the one part every version of the app shares.
  So it only ever adds to what it understands, and a new receiver is published before an app that needs it ships (RELEASE.md).
  Anyone on the network can join a TV's Cast session, so it takes nothing on trust: it joins a command's parts in whatever order they come, forgets those a phone left unfinished as it says hello again (as it does sending the presentation again after a message didn't reach the TV), and drops a part that isn't one, a command of more than `MOST_PARTS` parts or `MOST_COMMAND_LENGTH` characters, and parts that don't make JSON; a map to show that can't be followed leaves the map on screen as it was (`show` in `web/audience.ts` reads what it needs first).
