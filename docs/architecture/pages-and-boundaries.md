# Pages, and what crosses the app's edge

- **Pages, ready before they're needed.**
  A WebView with Orbit's page is made once the app is idle, so a map draws the moment it opens.
  The map's view model keeps its page through rotations and trips to other screens.
  When the map closes, the page goes back to the pool, once it has said its last word (what was being typed on it) and converted what was typed in the source editor a moment before (`SourceEditing.flush`), YAML included; the map's last text is written after, on the app's scope, since the view model's own work stops as it's cleared, keeping the version before it for the reason a save would have.
  Whether it's written goes by the map as it is now (`readOnlyNow`), never a version on show, which a newer Orbit may have made.
  A screen that opened the map again meanwhile may have read it before those writes landed, so it catches up with them once they have (`MapRepository.lastWritten`), as with a change from elsewhere; one that read them finds the file as it last wrote or read it, which is never taken for one, and a read that one of its own saves landed during is read again (`readSettled`).
  When the last text found the map changed meanwhile, as by that screen saving first, it goes into the history, and that screen says so (`keptAside`).
  The view model works with its page through `MapPage` (`page/MapPage.kt`), which `OrbitPage` is, and with the rest of the app through `MapApp` (`map/MapApp.kt`), which `AppContainer` is: a JVM test makes it with maps and settings on files, and a page of its own that says what `page-protocol.json`'s examples say (`MapViewModelTest`).
- **What leaves the phone says no more than it shows.**
  A map's own files, the ones its text points at where an address starts (`media/…` in a picture, a link, an idea's attributes or a YAML map, and never inside a web address: `MediaNames.references`), but those it names only in code, which a note shows as words (`sharedNames`, `MediaNames.withoutCode`, with code found as Orbit's reader finds it, `MapCode`, rule for rule: the map read without a byte-order mark at its start or the indentation every line with text shares, as the parser takes them away first; in a note, a paragraph read from left to right as the note reader's one pattern reads it, a run of backticks closing at the first run as long, across the paragraph's lines, unless a picture, a link, a web address in angle brackets, or bold, struck or emphasized words start first; in a title, a run to the next exactly as long, before anything else; and a fenced block, opened at most three spaces past where its level starts, as the parser reads it level by level, to its closing line or the level's end, a quote's fences read without their `>`; the map keeps those, and its folder, backups and Export all carry them as ever), go to other people as `OutgoingMedia` makes them, in shared zips and maps sent to a device (`writeZip`), web pages and prints (`WebPage.write`, with what goes inside in `PageMedia`), SVGs (`OutgoingMedia.drawing`), the browsers a presentation is shown in (`OutgoingCopies`, a clean copy made once and sent in parts), a picture, video or sound shared from the viewer (`sharePicture`, `shareVideoOrSound`), and a video opened in another app from its player (`openVideoInAnotherApp`, a clean copy made in the exports by `openMapExport`, after the room check of its `ExportRoom`, and opened as the map's lock allows right before).
  JPEG, PNG and WebP go as `SharedPictures.withoutMetadata` makes them, a JPEG's JFIF segment written again without its thumbnail (`jfifWithoutThumbnail`) and JFXX's left out, a PNG or WebP with only the chunks that draw it (`PNG_PICTURE`, `WEBP_PICTURE`), an animation's frames with only theirs (`WEBP_FRAME`), and nothing past a WebP's RIFF; a PNG with a critical chunk of a kind Orbit doesn't know can't be read through.
  So do GIFs, without comments or other apps' blocks (`media/GifsAndDrawings.kt`), and SVG drawings, with only what draws them as a picture's own sandbox (an `<img>`) draws it (`media/PassiveDrawings.kt`): read as they stream by, they keep only the SVG elements and attributes on its lists, and the XHTML a `foreignObject` lays out text with; references only to the drawing's own parts (`#…`) and pictures inside it, made clean as any picture; and styles, in a sheet or an attribute, without `@import`, a `url()` outside the drawing, an escape or anything that runs.
  So scripts, event handlers, links, other apps' elements and attributes, comments and instructions, and anything a drawing opened on its own would fetch, go.
  One with a document type, or a picture inside that can't go clean, can't be read through.
  MP4 and QuickTime videos, and the audio kept in MP4, are patched where they are, so every frame and offset stays: their `udta`, `meta` and `meco` boxes and XMP's `uuid`, in the movie, its tracks, their media (`mdia`, `minf`) and its fragments (`moof`, `traf`), and any `uuid` in the movie itself (a camera's own, as Canon's), become `free`, and so do `prft` and the `free` and `skip` boxes already there, which may hold what an editor freed; the dates in their movie, track and media headers become zero, what follows a handler's type and a video's compressor name and QuickTime's vendor in its sample descriptions become zeros, and anything after the last whole box (a phone's own trailer) is left out.
  HEIF and AVIF pictures have their EXIF, XMP and other metadata items (`mime`, whatever its name and content type, and `uri `), wherever `iloc` puts them, written over with zeros, every `infe` entry's name made empty, what follows it (a content type and encoding, a URI's type) moved up behind it and zeros after, so the entry keeps its size (`IsoBoxes.itemNames`), its `meta` box's `hdlr` name zeros, as a track's is, and in `iprp`'s `ipco` a `udes` description's language, name, description and tags zeros, and the `crtt`, `mdft` and `itai` times zero (`propertiesSaid`), a picture whose items are in more than 65,536 places together (`ITEM_PLACES_MOST`) not read through, and an image sequence's movie, as an animated AVIF's, cleaned as a movie's is (its tracks of pictures, `pict` and `auxv`, as a video's); every other top-level box becomes `free`, but for those a picture is made of (`PICTURE_BOXES`) and any one of its items is in, and the boxes after the last it keeps are left out: so the video a motion photo keeps (`mpvd`), Samsung's trailer (`sefd`), any `uuid` (XMP, content credentials, a camera's own) and what free space held never leave: a map shows such a picture's still alone, and a live photo's motion is a file of its own beside it.
  Sounds and WebM are patched the same way, each read through by its own reader, and the tags after what it reads (ID3v1, APE, Lyrics3, `SoundTags`) left out:
  MP3s and ADTS frame by frame (`MpegFrames`), an ID3v2 tag before or between their frames emptied; FLAC's blocks other than STREAMINFO and SEEKTABLE made PADDING, its frames one right after another, each whole by its checksums and nothing but zeros between them, read in one pass (`onlyFrames`), its last frame found whole by its checksums (`FlacBlocks`); Ogg page by page, each stream's comment header emptied in place and its pages' checksums written again (`OggPages`); WAV's chunks other than those that make the sound made `JUNK`, and the byte of padding after a chunk of an odd size a zero, unless it's the next chunk's first, as when a writer left it out (`WaveChunks`); WebM and Matroska element by element, tags, chapters, attachments, titles, dates and names made `Void`, the apps that made it named with nothing, checksums written again, and segments and clusters of no size read to their end (`MatroskaElements`).
  A sound cut short at its end goes as far as its last whole piece, and what the cut falls in stays out:
  `MpegFrames` and `OggPages` stop before the frame or page the file ends in, or before tags after them that it ends in, read from where each starts (`SoundTags.cutShortFrom`), an Ogg once every stream's headers are past and its last page's checksum is right; `FlacBlocks` finds the last whole frame from the end, where its CRC-16 comes to zero before a frame or tags cut short; `WaveChunks` stops at the last whole sample (`nBlockAlign`), or before a chunk cut short after the samples, and writes RIFF's and the samples' sizes, or RF64's in `ds64`, to match; `MatroskaElements` stops before the block the cut falls in, or before the last cluster when none of its blocks is whole, writes the segment's and the cluster's sizes to match (libwebm, Android's own reader, skips a cluster that says it goes on past the file), makes a cut cluster's CRC-32 Void, and the index's entries for what was cut away (ExoPlayer seeks to the cues the index names, and fails when they aren't there), and so are cues before the clusters for what was cut away, a `CueTrackPositions` whose `CueClusterPosition`, or `CueRelativePosition` in its cluster, is past the new end, a `CuePoint` with none left, and the `Cues` with none left, the index's entry for them with it (`cuesKept`).
  A cut anywhere else, as in a sound's headers or a WebM's cues or tags, still can't be read through, and nor can a movie, or a HEIF or AVIF picture, whose frames or items aren't all before the end of its last whole box, as a voice memo cut short in its `mdat`, after a whole one or not: every `stco` and `co64` chunk offset (`IsoBoxes.chunksBefore`), every fragment's `trun` data, from its `tfhd` base or the `moof`, wherever its `mdat` is (`IsoBoxes.fragmentBefore`), and every item's place in `iloc` has to be there and be told (one that runs past the file can't be).
  BMPs keep their headers, colors, pixels and embedded profile, and the rest is zeros or left out (`Bitmaps`).
  Videos keep every track, a camera's GPS track included, as the privacy policy says, and what an encoder writes into a stream stays with it; a file Orbit can't read through, or of no kind it knows, doesn't go, unless the person sharing chose to share it as it is.
  `MapRepository.uncleaned` finds those files (`Uncleaned`), each with the `FileStamp` it had as Orbit found it can't clean it; each way out asks once, as it starts at the phone, through `rememberAsTheyAre` (the export panel, Home's share sheet, the send sheet and its computer share, presenting in browsers or on a TV), and hands the answers down as `AsTheyAreFiles` (the stamps asked about and chosen, never names) to `writeZip`, `WebPage.write`, `OutgoingCopies`, the TV's `inlinePictures` and `OutgoingMedia.drawing`, whose pictures name no file, so each is known by what it holds: one chosen goes as it is, and one left out, or no one was asked about, doesn't, whatever else was chosen.
  An answer is about that very file: `OutgoingMedia.of` gives `OutgoingFile.AsIs` only for a file whose stamp now is one chosen, and a file Orbit can't clean whose stamp no one was asked about throws `FileChanged` in a way out that can ask again (`AsTheyAreFiles.stops`), which deletes what it made and asks again with `AsTheyAre.askAgain`, its question's first line saying so (`AskedAgain`), once; a way out that lasts and can't ask (`lasting`: the presentation's browsers and TV, the computer share) leaves it on this device.
  What's read is checked at the last moment: every `OutgoingFile` read from a file carries its stamp, and its `open` checks it against the open descriptor (`FileStamp.isOpenAt`, `Os.fstat` on Android), never the path, before its first byte and again after its last, so a plan made for one file never writes another's bytes, and what was written of a file that changed meanwhile is thrown away with the output it went into, every one of them made in the app's own storage before it leaves.
  The presentation's browsers get a file chosen to go as it is as a copy in `outgoing/as-they-are` (`OutgoingCopies`), made the first time one asks, in room the phone has to spare, and deleted as the presentation ends or stops (`forgetAsTheyAre`), as the map locks: a folder for each time they were forgotten, so a copy still being made then is deleted once it's made, and a private map's are kept with `PrivateCopies` too; `PrivateCopies` deletes any left as Orbit starts (`servedAsTheyAre`).
  Every copy is made once at a time (`making`), however many browsers ask for it at once: the others wait for it.
  Every way out finds a map's files through `mapMediaFile`: a name Orbit gives one, and the file itself in the map's own media folder, through no link, whether the file, the media folder or the map's folder is one (the app's storage above may sit behind one); the map's page (`MapFiles`) reads its pictures the same way.
  The viewer asks in its own dialog (`Shared.NotClean`, `ShareAsItIsDialog`, `shareAsItIs`), and so do the video player (`OpeningElsewhere`, `openVideoAsItIs`) and an idea's file (`FileOpening`), each about the very file it found it can't clean, by its stamp: one replaced since goes clean if it can, else is asked about again (`Shared.Changed`).
  The servers never ask anyone: with no choice made, those files stay out.
  The map's own files never change, and Export all, a backup, and the folder maps are kept in take them exactly as they are.
  Every way out says what goes with the map first (`MediaSummary`, in `ui/MediaWords`'s words).
- **The servers listen only in the room** (`nearby/LocalServer.kt`, the sockets, with its requests and answers in `HttpMessages.kt`, how they're read and written in `HttpWire.kt`, event streams in `EventStream.kt`, and the pages' content policy and every answer's safety headers in `PagePolicy.kt`; `nearby/Addresses.kt`): on the addresses of the Wi-Fi, a hotspot, a cable or USB tethering (`roomAddresses`), following them as the phone joins and leaves networks.
  They answer only devices in the room's ranges, never another app on the phone itself through the Wi-Fi's own address, and send their pictures and videos with a content policy that keeps an SVG's script, say, from ever running.
  A request at a room's code from another site's page (`fromAnotherSite`: `Sec-Fetch-Site` says `cross-site` or `same-site`, or, from a browser before it, `Origin` isn't the address asked) makes no session, counts and clears no wrong code or PIN, and gets the same answer whatever the code, right, wrong or ended (`fromElsewhere`): for a room's page, join.html's way on, which opens the same address again from the page itself, by itself once in a tab and after that with a tap, so the room then answers it as it answers anyone who typed it; for anything else, a refusal.
  What the person opens themselves and everything a room's page asks for say `none` or `same-origin`, and a request that says neither, or only `Origin: null`, from an older browser or a tool, is taken as the person's own, as it can't be told from someone typing the address.
  The join form keeps referrers to the phone's own address (`Referrer-Policy: same-origin`, where every other answer sends none), so a browser before `Sec-Fetch-Site` posts it with its real `Origin`.
  A request's head has 15 seconds to arrive, and its body 15 seconds and 30 more for each megabyte it says it holds (`ServerTimings`), so a device sending a byte at a time can't hold a connection.
  Nothing big is made in memory: a body past 256 KiB goes to a file, but a map sent here, which its handler opens as it arrives, straight from the connection (`Admission.Read.streamed`, `BodyInput`, within the time a body of its length has; one cut short throws `BodyCutShortException`), and a shared map's page and its zip are each made in a file once for each version of the map, only once there's room for all of it (`SharedMap.page`, `SharedZip`), and every browser that asks is sent that file, the page with its own content policy (`Response.pageFile`); the one before stays for answers on their way, and they go as the share ends.
  Each start of a server has a job its handlers' work runs under, cancelled as it stops (`LocalServer.Running`): a big answer still being made, a page, a zip or a map arriving, stops with it (`WhileActive`), and leaves nothing behind.
- **What pages may load.**
  The pages come with a content security policy: their own scripts and nothing else to run, pictures and videos from the web over HTTPS, and YouTube's and Vimeo's players framed (`PAGE_POLICY` in `page/OrbitPage.kt`).
  A shared web page carries a policy of its own wherever it's opened, a file, a website or a browser on the Wi-Fi: only its own script runs, by its hash, and what a map holds shows, from inside the page or over HTTPS, but never runs (`sharedPagePolicy` in `page/WebPage.kt`), so an address that slips past Orbit's guard still can't.
  Nothing goes over the web in the clear: the policy has WebView ask for anything at an `http` address over `https` (`upgrade-insecure-requests`; without it, WebView refuses such a picture), and the app's own views do the same (`pictureAddress`), and load a map's pictures only from the web, from inside the map's text or from its folder, as the page does (`isWebPicture`, for the details, the editor and notes drawn natively); a video at one plays in the browser, as links open there.
  Of the maps' files, a page gets only the pictures and videos in the media folder of the map it shows, or of one it's drawing a picture of, sent so they never run as a page (`page/MapFiles.kt`): a map can't point it at another map's text or pictures.
  A picture's job, or quick capture's, which draws the map it adds ideas to out of sight, reads its map's at an address of its own, by a key only it is given, while it lasts (`jobBaseUrl`, `OrbitPage.jobGrants`), so two pictures drawn at once, or one drawn while the page shows another map, never read each other's maps; the page puts into a picture only what's at that address, or on the web (`picturesOwn` in `web/bridge.ts`); private maps locking call off the pictures being drawn of them (`PagePool.revokeJobs`); and a file is its map's only when it's in that map's own media folder, wherever a link in its path leads, as a folder prepared outside Orbit may have one.
  An animated picture goes to the map's page as its first frame, and a HEIC or HEIF one, which Chromium doesn't decode, as a JPEG made from it, once for each version of the file (`page/FirstFrames.kt`), which web pages and prints carry in its place too, and SVG and PNG pictures of the map, made on a map's page, take; the audience's screen and the room's browsers get that JPEG as well (`MapFiles`'s `animations`, `BrowserPresenting`'s `drawn`), though an animation as it is; the file itself stays as it is.
  One the cache can't keep, full or unwritable, goes to the map's page from memory, made again each time it's asked for (`FirstFrames.standIn`), and a web page or print carries the picture as it is; nothing a page asks for can stop the app: what can't be read is missing (`MapFiles.answer`).
  Pictures from the phone are made JPEGs as they come in, so only ones that came through the folder or a zip need it.
  An SVG export takes in only what answers as a picture.
- **An idea's file opens in another app** as a copy in the exports, never the map's own file (`map/IdeaFileViews.kt`, `FileOpening`): only one in the map's media folder, wherever its address leads (`MapViewModel.ideaFile`); clean when Orbit can clean it, as a picture held as a file, and otherwise only once the person chose it, asked in a dialog that says what may go with it.
  A copy of a private map's file is kept by `PrivateCopies`, deleted as private maps lock, and its folder is marked, so one left when Orbit stopped goes as it starts again.
  So is a private map's video opened in another app from its player: `openMapExport` keeps every copy it opens of a private map's (`openExport`).
- **What Orbit hands other apps.**
  Its file provider reaches the cache's exports and all of the maps' folder, as earlier versions opened a video Orbit couldn't clean in the phone's video app as the map's own file; the video app now always gets a copy from the exports, clean or, once the person chose, as it is.
  Every address Orbit gives out comes from `sharedFileUri` (`ui/Sharing.kt`), which hands out only an export or a file in a map's media folder (`sharesFile`), wherever links in its path lead: never a map's text, versions or details, whatever a later change asks of it.
  `openVideoElsewhere` opens only addresses on the web, never a map's file.
- **What other apps can hand Orbit.**
  Files and text another app opens or shares with Orbit (`VIEW` and `SEND` in the manifest) become maps, as Open with and Share are meant to.
  Files of any kind shared to "Attach to an Orbit idea", an alias of the main activity (`AttachFiles`), join the idea in focus on the map that's open, unless it's locked, or a new map named after the first (`Importer.newMapWithFiles`); only other apps' content addresses, as imports.
  Android offers the alias for any share, text alone included: that joins the idea in focus as ideas, pasted there (`MapViewModel.addSharedText`), or makes a new map as text shared to Orbit does (`AppRequest.AttachText`).
  The types and extensions maps come as are listed once, in `data/MapFileTypes.kt` (other apps' formats carry their own, `OtherFormat`), for the manifest's filters, the file pickers, drops and imports; `MapFileTypesTest` holds the manifest to it.
  So an app on the phone could add maps no one asked for, but Orbit comes to the front with each one, which goes to the trash like any other map, and nothing goes back:
  Orbit only reads what it's handed, and answers no app with anything.
