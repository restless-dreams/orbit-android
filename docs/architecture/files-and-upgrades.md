# Files and upgrades

- **Maps are folders.**
  Each holds:
  - `map.md`, the map in orbit-md;
  - `media/`, the pictures added to it, kept at most 2048 pixels on their longer side, as JPEG or PNG (an animated GIF or WebP as it came, up to 250 MB, since redrawing it would keep its first frame alone: `data/Animations.kt` reads its frames without decoding them), the videos added from the phone as they came (MP4, WebM or QuickTime, by their type), or made smaller on the phone as they came in, an MP4 (`data/SmallerVideos.kt` plans it, `media/VideoMaker.kt` makes it with Media3's Transformer, `MapMediaIntake.addMadeVideo` puts it in place once it's whole), each with a frame from it beside it as its poster, and its sounds: voice memos recorded in Orbit (`memo-…`) and sounds from the phone as they came (`sound-…`).
    Memos are AAC in an `.mp4`, and an M4A is kept as the `.mp4` it is, because every release carries `.mp4` files in its zips, backups, folder and sending; the other sounds keep their own extension (`data/MapSounds.kt`), which earlier releases leave out of those.
    A live photo is kept as two plain files: its picture, as any picture is kept, and its motion beside it as an MP4 (`picture-…-motion.mp4`), which its picture's `image.motion` points at (`data/LivePhotos.kt` reads the motion out of an Android phone's picture, `data/LivePairs.kt` joins an iPhone's still and movie, and `data/MotionVideos.kt` makes the movie an MP4 without touching its video, with `data/QuickTimeMovies.kt` for what Android's reader can't do: open an older iPhone's movie, which starts without an `ftyp`, and read the uncompressed sound QuickTime describes its own ways); and its ideas' files, of any kind, as they came (`file-` and the name each came with, made safe: `ideaFileName`), which their ideas' `files` entries point at (`data/IdeaFiles.kt`).
    Since they're of any kind, a file is a map's because its text names it in `files` (`fileNamesIn`, read from a markdown map's attributes and a YAML map's keys as `soundNamesIn` reads sounds): a map carries those and the pictures, videos and sounds of the kinds this version keeps (`carriedNames`), in its room, zips, sending, backups and the folder; one of a kind it doesn't know that the text doesn't name stays out of what comes in; and Orbit's own unused `file-` names go as unused pictures do.
    They never reach a map's page (`page/MapFiles.kt` serves only pictures and videos), a room's browsers or a web page, which see the map without them (`withoutFiles`);
  - `meta.json`, whether it's pinned or private, when it was opened, where you left it, its lasting identity, its file in the folder maps are kept in, and its cards in study mode: this device's answers (`study`), and the other devices' that the folder brings (`studyElsewhere`), a card going as the latest of them left it, and which of the ideas with answers aren't cards now (`studyNotCards`), which Home's count of cards due leaves out;
  - beside the maps, `history/<map>/` holds its earlier versions: one as each visit begins, one for every stretch of editing and one before each big change, thinned out as they age, up to the newest 200 (`versionsToKeep`), and `<time>.original.md` for a file that came in from elsewhere as a new map, as it came, never thinned (a newer version of a map that arrives later is an ordinary version).
    `reasons.json` beside them says why each was kept, by its file's name (`VersionReason`): what the history shows under each version's time, with what changed from the one before it, worked out as its row comes on screen (`VersionChanges`, `map/VersionSummaries.kt`);
  - `trash/<map>-<time>/` holds a deleted map, with its history, for 30 days;
  - `maps/.layout.json` says which layout the files are in:
    `StorageLayout` runs each step from an older one (a copy of the maps' text first, in `backups/`), and leaves a newer one alone.

  The settings are a DataStore, `files/datastore/settings.preferences_pb`.

  A map's pictures, videos and sounds come to at most `MAP_MEDIA_LIMIT`, a gigabyte, each file to `MEDIA_FILE_LIMIT`, 250 MB (Orbit 0.4.0 and earlier: 90 MB and 50 MB, `…_UP_TO_0_4_0`).
  What comes in counts against that room from the moment its copy is let start (`MapStore.arriving`, which counts and takes it at once), at the size it says it is, then at its files' own, until a text the map has points at them (`IncomingMedia`, settled as the map's text is written and read, and let go once no screen shows the map, or as soon as a copy won't join it after all, as a drop the map no longer takes, or one of several stopped partway, whose copies in by then join nothing: `MapRepository.letGo`, which leaves its files for the tidying up; a copy stopped as its files came in, past the moment it would notice, lets go of them itself, as whoever asked for them never hears of them: `MapMediaIntake.intake`, `IncomingMedia.Arrival.letGo`): files that come in together, a batch chosen at once, a drop, or a video still copying while a file joins, each count the others, so they never take a map past its room.
  A map travels between devices as a zip of its text and the pictures, videos, sounds and ideas' files it points at, with `orbit.json` carrying its identity and place.
  Files kept only for its earlier versions stay on the device.
  Big files are written, copied and synced a piece at a time, never whole in memory: a map's zip unpacks into files while there's room (`readBundle`, `ROOM_LEFT_FREE`, looked at before the first byte and after each megabyte), refused before any of it is read when there's no room for about its size (`noRoomForBundle`), leaving nothing behind whatever stops it, and a declined one lets go of its files at once (`discard`).
  So are exports: a web page holds the map's pictures, up to `PAGE_PICTURES_LIMIT` (100 MiB, more than a map held before), and its sounds and live photos' motions, up to 25 MiB each, in Base64, about 200 MB at most, and `WebPage.write` writes it as it goes, the page's HTML, then its data's JSON with each picture as its place in the text comes, then each sound and motion, each of them copied whole to a file of its own first (`PageMedia.spool`, in the cache's `page-parts`, where what a page that never finished left goes an hour later), so one whose file changes meanwhile stays out rather than leave half of itself.
  Pictures are cleaned the same way wherever they go in a zip or a page: read from their file a window at a time (`PictureInFile`) and written from the parts of it they keep (`SharedPictures.plan`, `OutgoingFile.Pieced`), so no file, however big, is ever whole in memory; only an SVG drawing, whose text is read as a whole, is cleaned in memory.
  A page or a zip says the most it may come to before any of it is written, and isn't started on a storage without room for it and some to spare (`requireRoom`, the `ExportRoom` an export is written to).
  A page counts each picture as many times as the map names it, as much of it as goes, worked out once for each (cleaned, drawn again as a JPEG, or as it is: `OutgoingFile.size`; a sketch as the PNG it draws), and none comes to more as it's written: one that changed since and grew stays out, and a sketch drawn on since goes smaller to fit (`pictureSizes`, `sketchPng`).
  Export makes each file into the cache (`makeExport`, a folder of its own among the exports) and saves or shares it from there, a full storage told apart from a map's file that went (`OutputFailed`); a print's page is a file too, which its web view reads as it loads (`MapPrinter.pageFile`).
  A TV the map is cast to still gets it as one string, its pictures shrunk to its screen and the first 48 MiB of them only (`inlinePictures`'s `most`), each read only once it fits.
  How a file goes is worked out once while it's the same file, by its path, size and time to the nanosecond, which file it is on its storage, and on Linux when its inode last changed, which no app can set back (`FileStamp`, `OutgoingMedia.judge`'s plans, worked out from the open file while its descriptor says it's that one), so the export panel and the send sheet read no sound twice, and a file written again in place at the same size and time, as a sync app may leave it, is worked out again, and the room gets a new copy of it (`OutgoingCopies`); an SVG past 32 MiB, cleaned in memory, isn't read through, and goes only as it is, when chosen.

  Edits are saved as they happen, one write after another, each only while the file is still the text it was made from (`MapRepository.write`), and only when it was made on the text the screen has: each `Load` and `Restore` carries the screen's revision, which the page's `saved` echoes, so a save made before a change from elsewhere reached the page goes into the history instead (`isStale`).
  Saves wait in `SaveQueue`: the screen's newest text, which takes the place of an older one still waiting since it holds all that one did, and each text set aside, in turn, which nothing takes the place of; a closing map keeps what's still set aside.
  The source editor's typing carries the change of the map the editor's text showed (`SourceEditing`), and is saved only while that's the newest: typing on the text before a change goes into the history too.
  So does an idea being typed on the map as a change from elsewhere reaches the page (a `Restore` with a new revision), where the change would close its field: a map the page can only show, or the idea typed into, or under, no longer where it was, under the same ideas.
  The page saves it first, at the revision it was typed on and while the map can still be changed, and the app sets that save aside (`takesTyping` in `web/bridge.ts`); a change that leaves the idea where it was keeps the field open on it, as Orbit does, and what's typed is saved on the change.
  The app's own `Restore`s (undo, redo, a version restored) keep the revision, and the page saves nothing first: a save then would be taken for an edit made after them.
  So the app closes an open field itself before undo, redo and the map drawn from Edit as text, and waits for its save, as the page answers a check only after everything it said before it (`afterTypingOnTheMap`): the typing is a change of its own, which Undo takes back, and a page that doesn't answer in time gets no undo or redo, which its late save would land over.
  A `lock` (the map deleted, or the storage a newer Orbit's) has the page save what's typed first too, while the map still takes it: a save that finds the map gone to the trash goes into the history the map took there, deleted since the screen opened (`keepAsideInTrash`), and one the storage won't take stays on the screen as unsaved, tried again, as with a full storage.
  A write never leaves half a file (`writeAtomically`: written beside, under a name of its own for each write, synced to disk, then renamed, and the rename synced too; partial files a crash left are cleared at the next start).
  A picture added to a map that's gone to the trash meanwhile doesn't bring its folder back, and a live photo's motion added again gets a name of its own rather than going over the one earlier versions point at.
  Each file coming into a map's media, from the phone, a paste, the recorder, a sketch or the folder maps are kept in (`addMediaFile`, and `renameMedia`'s copy under a name of its own), gets a name no file there has and no other copy on its way in holds, chosen and held in one step until its copy is in (`IncomingMedia.Names`), so two in one millisecond, or two files of one name, never get the same one; the folder's sync passes over held names as it numbers one (`hasMediaNamed`).
  A new file then takes its name only when no file has it just before it's put in place (`writeNew`, a move that doesn't replace), so one put there meanwhile from outside Orbit stays, and the copy fails instead.
  Folders of new maps are filled while hidden and then named, so a crash never leaves half a map, their details first, so a private one is private from its first file on.

  Android's backup goes through `OrbitBackupAgent`, within the rules in `res/xml`: it keeps each backup inside the quota its transport gives (`FullBackupDataOutput.getQuota`), the maps' text and the settings first, then their pictures, sounds, videos and ideas' files, each smallest first (`backupFiles`), and takes the history only when moving to a new phone.
  Private maps go only into a backup that's encrypted end to end or goes to a new phone (`takesPrivateMaps`, from the transport's flags): any other leaves out their folders and history, and takes every other map.
  Whether a map is private is looked at again right before each of its entries is written (`backUp`), its folder going right before its first file since the folder's name is the map's, so a map made private while the backup is made stays out from then on, and one whose details can't be read counts as private; a map still being made, in its hidden folder, never goes.
  It carries how many it left out, a count only (`notePrivateMapsLeftOut`, written beside the maps as the backup's last file and deleted once it's made), which a restore moves to where Home reads it (`keepRestoredPrivateMapsNote`, from `onRestoreFinished`), so a restored phone says once how they come back (`PrivateRestoreCard`): from a synced folder as ordinary maps, or from an Export all, still private.
  Restoring is Android's own, but each file is written beside its place and takes it only once all of it came (`restoreFile`), the folder's deletions too, whose bytes Orbit reads itself, since Android restores a file the rules name on its own only in its very place (`restoreWhole`); and each file's time, which Android sets in seconds where it takes milliseconds, is put right (`onRestoreFile`).
- **Every version opens what the earlier ones left, and keeps what later ones wrote.**
  A change to what the app keeps follows these rules:
  - **The files' layout** (`StorageLayout`): a change that moves or renames files is a new step in `LAYOUT_STEPS` and raises `LAYOUT_VERSION`.
    A step must be safe to run again after a crash midway, and gets a test that starts from the older layout's files (`MapRepositoryTest`); one that can't be done whole, as a lone map file that won't move into its folder, throws once it has done what it could, so the record isn't advanced and the next launch runs it again.
    `LAYOUT_COMPATIBLE` is the oldest layout whose app may still change files laid out this way: raise it too when an older app would harm the new layout by writing to it, and that app then only reads the maps.
    The record's `version` and `compatible` stay whole numbers, and fields are only added to it: a record that's there but can't be read leaves the maps only read too.
  - **`meta.json`** (`MapMetadata`): a new field is optional, with a default that means what files without it mean.
    A field is never renamed or given another shape: add a new one instead.
    What this version doesn't know or can't read stays in the file when it writes it, at every depth: a field a newer version added inside `sync` too (`MetadataFile`, `keepUnknownFields`), and the folder's notes keep the kinds a newer version added the same way, so going back to an older version and forward again loses nothing.
    Such a field stays as it was while an older version changes the fields beside it, so it mustn't count on them staying as it left them.
    Details fail closed: a `private` this version can't read, and a `meta.json` it can't read at all, read as a private map's (`MetadataFile.unreadable`), the former going back as it came while the map stays private; a damaged `meta.json` is kept aside (`meta.json.unreadable-<time>`) as it's first written over, and one that can't be read for now is never written over (`readMetadataToChange`), nor are the folder's deletions (`readTombstones`), whose records are read one by one, a `private` one can't read counting as private, and written again as they were, with what a newer version added (`writeTombstones`).
    Deletions that can't be read at all, damaged, are no more taken for none than ones out of reach for now: a sync does nothing until they can be read, and says why (`data_folder_deletions_unreadable`), rather than bring a deleted private map's file back as a map like any other.
    One record among them that can't be read whole (its `file` reshaped, say) stops nothing: what of it can be read is kept, as a private map's deletion, and as an untold one without its `file`, and the fields it couldn't read are written back as they were, unless this version has changed one since, as a sync writes when its file went (`salvagedTombstone`, `changedTombstone`).
    Details that can't say whether a map has a file in the folder maps are kept in, nor which (`folderFileUnknown`: `meta.json` can't be read, or its `sync` can't), leave a deletion of the map's text and whether it was private for the folder the maps are kept in then (`SyncRecord.untold`: that folder, an empty `file`), and none while they're kept in none; earlier versions read it as a deletion whose file is gone, and forget it at their first sync.
    A sync of that folder then never takes a file that may be that map's for a map like any other (see "The folder maps are kept in").
    A map's identity (`uuid`) is its own: two maps that share one, as Export all could leave them before 0.4.0, are told apart as the list is read, the one made later (or, with the same or no time, the later folder by name) getting an identity of its own, and only its `meta.json` is written.
  - **Settings** (`SettingsRepository`): a new key has a default, a key is never renamed and keeps its type, and a choice is kept by its name, so one this version doesn't know reads as the default, as does a value of another type.
    `update` writes only the keys whose value changes, so a choice this version can't read stays as the newer version left it.
  - **Backups** come back onto any version (`restoreAnyVersion`), an older one too, which these rules make safe.
  - **The maps' text** is Orbit's format, which has versions of its own (Orbit's `docs/format.md`, "Versions"): a map written for a newer one opens read-only.
  - **The app's own kinds of idea** that count (`goal` and `progress`): their fields are only ever added, meaning the same.
    A change older apps would misread raises the kind's version, written as Orbit writes a type's (`goal@2`), and those apps take such an idea for one like any other: not counted, without its ring or its steps (`GOAL_VERSION` and `PROGRESS_VERSION`, in `web/goal.ts`, `web/look.ts` and `page/Protocol.kt`).
    Another kind picked in the editor takes off the version, which was the old kind's (`kindChanges`): the idea becomes the kind as this version writes it.
    The kind its type names already, picked again, changes nothing, so an idea written for a newer version of it keeps that version and its fields as they were written, and the editor shows that kind as chosen (`kindNamed`).
  - **The folder maps are kept in is shared by every version that syncs it.**
    Each device keeps a record of itself there (`.orbit/devices/<id>.json`, `FolderDevice`): its Orbit, the newest map format it reads (`MAP_FORMAT`, which `OrbitBuildTest` holds to the pages' Orbit as `orbit.json` names it), the kinds of file it carries (`carriedMediaTypes`, and the kinds of the ideas' files the folder holds, so a version from before ideas' files says it can't open them; `ideaFiles` says it keeps those of every kind), and how big a map's files may be there (`mediaFileLimit`, `mapMediaLimit`; a record without them is 0.4.0's, which keeps 50 MB and 90 MB, `keepsFile`, `keepsMapMedia`).
    A sync that finds a device keeping less than this one lists, by device, the folder's maps it keeps only part of (`FolderRoster.partlyKept`, `FolderSyncState.partly`), which Settings names beside it.
    A field is only ever added, a device keeps what a newer Orbit wrote in its own record, and it never writes or deletes another's; nor does it write its own over one a newer Orbit marks as one it would misread (`compatible`), as after going back to an older version, or over one it can't read for now (`FolderDeviceRecords.update`).
    The record carries `format` (`DEVICE_FORMAT`, 1) and `compatible`, the oldest format that reads it right: a change older versions would misread raises `compatible`, and they leave the record out.
    Its id (`ThisDevice`) stays out of backups, and an install that finds one another install made, as a cloning backup tool copies every file, makes its own, so two installs never share a record.
    A map a sync writes for a newer format than another device in the folder reads (`FolderRoster.leftBehind`, checked as each map is written) goes in the folder's notes (`FolderNotes.newer`), and Settings says which maps that device can only show; `FolderNotice.Older` with `showsOnly` says, on Home and in Settings, which device it is.
    The version that first writes a newer format than 1 relies on that check before upgrading a map in a shared folder.
    Every release lists only the folder's top-level `.md` files whose names don't start with a dot (`DocumentFolder.files()`), so `.orbit/` stays invisible to them: anything a newer version keeps in the folder for itself goes there.
  - **Study goes through the folder in records each device keeps of its own** (`.orbit/study/<id>/<map's file>.json`, `StudyRecord`, `data/FolderStudy.kt`): its answers to the map's cards, by idea id, each with the moment it was given (`StudyCard.at`), and the map's earlier files (`was`), so a map renamed on one device keeps the answers the others still keep under its old name.
    A device writes and deletes only its own records and reads everyone's, so devices studying at once never write the same file, and a card goes as its latest answer on any of them left it (`mergedProgress`).
    A record a sync can't read, or another device's it can't make out, is read again by the next one; until then, what that device studied stays as it was here, and a device that can't read its own records doesn't write them.
    One of this device's own that holds JSON this version can't make out is left as it is, as a newer Orbit's is, neither written over nor deleted until a sync can; one that isn't JSON at all, empty or the start of a record, as a write cut short leaves it, by this version or an earlier one, is written again whole.
    A record carries `format` (`STUDY_FORMAT`, 1) and `compatible`, the oldest format that reads it right: a newer one keeps every field an older one reads, meaning the same, so it's read for what it says that this version knows, and a change older versions would misread raises `compatible`, so they leave the record out, as if it weren't there (`StudyRead.Incompatible`), unlike one they can't make out at all, whose device's answers stay until a later sync reads it (`StudyRead.Garbled`); and a device keeps what a newer Orbit added to its own records, and to each card (`StudyCard.extras`, until this version answers the card).
    A record of its own that a newer Orbit marks as one this version would misread it neither writes over nor deletes, the map's answers staying on this device meanwhile, so going back to an older version and forward again loses none of the newer one's.
  - **What crosses to other devices carries a version:** an offer's in its `X-Orbit-Version` header and in `GET /key`'s answer.
    A change to how offers work raises it, and a device refuses an offer in a version it doesn't speak before reading any of it, saying which device to update.
    Within a version, a device taking maps also says what of a map it opens, and the most a map sent to it may be, in its answers to `GET /key` and to a hello (`mapFormat`, `mediaTypes` and `mapSize`, `DeviceReads`): the answers only ever gain fields, and a sender reads one that isn't there as Orbit 0.3.0 had it (`DeviceReads.RELEASE_0_3_0`; 0.4.0 says all but `mapSize`, and takes 64 MiB, `OFFER_LIMIT_UP_TO_0_4_0`).
    A map bigger than the other device takes goes no further than its hello, before the sender reveals its half of the code, so nothing shows there, and the sender says to update Orbit there (`SendState.TooBigThere`).
    The TV receiver works the other way round: it lives apart from the app, so it keeps following every older protocol's commands, and the phone sends it only the commands its protocol knows ("Versions between the parts that live apart", below).
  - **Zips say what made them:** a map's zip, in `orbit.json` beside the map's identity and place (`bundle`, `BUNDLE_FORMAT`; `compatible`; `app`), and a zip of every map, in `orbit-export.json`, its first entry (`export`, `EXPORT_FORMAT`; `compatible`; `app`; the layout and the map format).
    Their fields are only added, and read one by one, so one a newer version reshapes is lost alone, never the map's identity.
    A change older apps would misread raises `compatible`, and they refuse the zip, saying to update Orbit.
    Files of kinds a version doesn't know stay out of what it takes in, and the import says how many.
    Releases before 0.4.0 ignore the new fields, and skip `orbit-export.json`.
  - **The maps' history:** every release lists only the files named like versions (`VERSION_NAME`: `<time>.md` and `<time>.original.md`), thins out only `<time>.md` files, and takes the whole folder to the trash and back (`move`: renamed, or else copied a file at a time, each whole, and deleted only once every file is there; a copy a crash cut short, its first bytes alone, is finished from the whole one, and a file that holds something else keeps the folder where it was).
    Deleting a map moves its history into the map's folder first (`.history`, as the trash keeps it), so the one rename that takes the map to the trash takes its history too: one that can't move keeps the map where it was, and one Orbit stopped with in between goes back at the next start (`moveHistoryOutOfMaps`, since 0.4.0), as one a map back from the trash couldn't move does.
    While a history waits in a map's folder, nothing takes it for the map's own files: Export all leaves it out, a backup takes it only to a new phone, as it does the history, and `removeUnusedPictures` keeps every file.
    Deleting a map for good deletes a history an earlier version left outside the trash (`history/<map>`) too, unless a map here, or another in the trash, has that id.
    So what a newer version keeps about the versions goes beside them under another name, as `reasons.json` does.
    Its entries are by each version's file, a `why` this version doesn't know reads as none, entries and fields it doesn't know stay when it writes the file, and only entries whose version is gone (one an older release thinned out, say) are dropped.
    It's written as a version is kept, never as a map opens, and Export all carries it, which older releases' imports skip.
  - **Crash reports** keep the names every release gave them, `crash-<number>.txt` beside `seen`, so each release reads the others' in order: the number is when the crash was, or, when the clock says that's no later than the latest report's or the one Home showed last, just after it (`CrashLog`), so no report takes another's name or hides behind one.
  - **Each release's files are tests.**
    `app/src/test/resources/releases/` holds what every release left on a phone, made by its own code, and `ReleaseDataTest` opens each one with this version: every map, picture, video, version, trashed map and setting has to be there, opening them must write nothing, and they take changes without losing what they had; from 0.5.0, their reminders come at their moment, and the records the install kept in the folder maps are kept in (its device record and study records, in `folder/.orbit/`) read as another device's.
    `web/releases.test.ts` reads their maps, versions and trash with the pages' Orbit, as the map's page does: none is locked or misread, each is saved as it was written, and their kinds of idea and goals are the app's.
    A release adds its own (`RELEASE.md`).
- **The folder maps are kept in** is reached through Android's Storage Access Framework (`ACTION_OPEN_DOCUMENT_TREE` and `DocumentsContract`), so it can be any folder the system's picker offers.
  Each map remembers its file and how both sides were when they last matched: the text's hash, and the file's time and size, and when a sync first missed the file.
  So a sync tells which side changed, and a file just as it was isn't read again.
  `planSync` plans each sync as plain data, which the tests cover, and `FolderSync` carries it out.
  Files are read in whatever encoding they were written (`decodeText`: a UTF-8 file with a stray byte keeps its characters, and only that byte reads as Windows-1252), so writing them back never loses a character.
  A folder file that stops partway through a character, or isn't the size it was listed at (an empty one too: only a size the folder's app can't tell is unknown), is still arriving (`StillArriving`): it waits, and is taken as it is only once it has stayed so for `SETTLING`.
  Before writing over a file or deleting one, a sync checks it still holds the text it compared (`stillAsCompared`, from `Run.compared`): its time and size as listed (`SyncFolder.describe`) and, where the folder's app gives no time, or before any deletion, the text itself, read again.
  One changed since waits for the next sync, which keeps both, or brings a deleted map's changed file back as a map.
  Android's storage access can't write or delete a document only while it's unchanged, so a change that lands between that check and the write or deletion right after it is the one still lost: a window as narrow as one call to the folder's app, which nothing on Android can close (`FolderSyncRacesTest` runs the two races before it, on `FolderApp`, a folder's app that lands another device's edit at the very moment).
  After writing, a read-back that's someone else's text isn't written over again.
  A file over `MAP_TEXT_LIMIT` is never read, nor more of one than that whose size the app can't tell or listed smaller (`TooLargeForAMap`; the devices' records are read no further either), and the notes name it (`FolderNotes.large`).
  Its name is among the notes about private maps when it's a private map's file, here or deleted here, a sync app's copy of one, or no map's while a private map's file is gone from where it was, which it may be, renamed.
  A step that fails, whatever the folder's app throws but a refusal (`SecurityException`, which says the folder is out of reach), counts as a map not in step and the others go on; a sync that stops partway keeps the notes of what it did so far (`Run.notes`), and `FolderSyncState.failures` counts syncs in a row with a problem, which Home shows from the second (`FolderProblemCard`).
  A listing its app marks as failed (`EXTRA_ERROR`) is never taken for the folder's contents.
  After its steps, a sync fetches the pictures, videos and sounds that maps kept in the folder point at but don't have (`fetchMissingMedia`), as one that reached the folder after its map's text; a map is looked at again only when its text changes (`contentKey`) or while something it points at is missing, and the folder's media is listed only then.
  A media file that isn't its listed size (an empty one too) is still arriving and isn't copied.
  One too big for its map stays only in the folder until the folder's file changes or the map has more room (`refused`, with both as they were), and one that goes on failing to arrive for `MEDIA_PATIENCE` is a problem the sync reports, counting the maps missing them (`unfetched`).
