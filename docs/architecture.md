- **Maps are folders.** Each holds:
  - `map.md`, the map in orbit-md;
  - `media/`, the pictures added to it, kept at most 2048 pixels on their longer side, as JPEG or PNG (an animated GIF or WebP as it came, up to 250 MB, since redrawing it would keep its first frame alone: `data/Animations.kt` reads its frames without decoding them), the videos added from the phone as they came (MP4, WebM or QuickTime, by their type), or made smaller on the phone as they came in, an MP4 (`data/SmallerVideos.kt` plans it, `media/VideoMaker.kt` makes it with Media3's Transformer, `MapMediaIntake.addMadeVideo` puts it in place once it's whole), each with a frame from it beside it as its poster, and its sounds: voice memos recorded in Orbit (`memo-…`) and sounds from the phone as they came (`sound-…`). Memos are AAC in an `.mp4`, and an M4A is kept as the `.mp4` it is, because every release carries `.mp4` files in its zips, backups, folder and sending; the other sounds keep their own extension (`data/MapSounds.kt`), which earlier releases leave out of those. A live photo is kept as two plain files: its picture, as any picture is kept, and its motion beside it as an MP4 (`picture-…-motion.mp4`), which its picture's `image.motion` points at (`data/LivePhotos.kt` reads the motion out of an Android phone's picture, `data/LivePairs.kt` joins an iPhone's still and movie, and `data/MotionVideos.kt` makes the movie an MP4 without touching its video, with `data/QuickTimeMovies.kt` for what Android's reader can't do: open an older iPhone's movie, which starts without an `ftyp`, and read the uncompressed sound QuickTime describes its own ways); and its ideas' files, of any kind, as they came (`file-` and the name each came with, made safe: `ideaFileName`), which their ideas' `files` entries point at (`data/IdeaFiles.kt`). Since they're of any kind, a file is a map's because its text names it in `files` (`fileNamesIn`, read from a markdown map's attributes and a YAML map's keys as `soundNamesIn` reads sounds): a map carries those and the pictures, videos and sounds of the kinds this version keeps (`carriedNames`), in its room, zips, sending, backups and the folder; one of a kind it doesn't know that the text doesn't name stays out of what comes in; and Orbit's own unused `file-` names go as unused pictures do. They never reach a map's page (`page/MapFiles.kt` serves only pictures and videos), a room's browsers or a web page, which see the map without them (`withoutFiles`);
  - `meta.json`, whether it's pinned or private, when it was opened, where you left it, its lasting identity, its file in the folder maps are kept in, and its cards in study mode: this device's answers (`study`), and the other devices' that the folder brings (`studyElsewhere`), a card going as the latest of them left it, and which of the ideas with answers aren't cards now (`studyNotCards`), which Home's count of cards due leaves out;
  - beside the maps, `history/<map>/` holds its earlier versions: one as each visit begins, one for every stretch of editing and one before each big change, thinned out as they age, up to the newest 200 (`versionsToKeep`), and `<time>.original.md` for a file that came in from elsewhere as a new map, as it came, never thinned (a newer version of a map that arrives later is an ordinary version). `reasons.json` beside them says why each was kept, by its file's name (`VersionReason`): what the history shows under each version's time, with what changed from the one before it, worked out as its row comes on screen (`VersionChanges`, `map/VersionSummaries.kt`);
  - `trash/<map>-<time>/` holds a deleted map, with its history, for 30 days;
  - `maps/.layout.json` says which layout the files are in: `StorageLayout` runs each step from an older one (a copy of the maps' text first, in `backups/`), and leaves a newer one alone.

  The settings are a DataStore, `files/datastore/settings.preferences_pb`.

  A map's pictures, videos and sounds come to at most `MAP_MEDIA_LIMIT`, a gigabyte, each file to `MEDIA_FILE_LIMIT`, 250 MB (Orbit 0.4.0 and earlier: 90 MB and 50 MB, `…_UP_TO_0_4_0`). A map travels between devices as a zip of its text and the pictures, videos and sounds it points at, with `orbit.json` carrying its identity and place. Files kept only for its earlier versions stay on the device. Big files are written, copied and synced a piece at a time, never whole in memory: a map's zip unpacks into files while there's room (`readBundle`, `ROOM_LEFT_FREE`, looked at before the first byte and after each megabyte), refused before any of it is read when there's no room for about its size (`noRoomForBundle`), leaving nothing behind whatever stops it, and a declined one lets go of its files at once (`discard`). So are exports: a web page holds the map's pictures, up to `PAGE_PICTURES_LIMIT` (100 MiB, more than a map held before), and its sounds and live photos' motions, up to 25 MiB each, in Base64, about 200 MB at most, and `WebPage.write` writes it as it goes, the page's HTML, then its data's JSON with each picture as its place in the text comes, then each sound and motion, each of them copied whole to a file of its own first (`PageMedia.spool`, in the cache's `page-parts`, where what a page that never finished left goes an hour later), so one whose file changes meanwhile stays out rather than leave half of itself. Pictures are cleaned the same way wherever they go in a zip or a page: read from their file a window at a time (`PictureInFile`) and written from the parts of it they keep (`SharedPictures.plan`, `OutgoingFile.Pieced`), so no file, however big, is ever whole in memory; only an SVG drawing, whose text is read as a whole, is cleaned in memory. A page or a zip says the most it may come to before any of it is written, and isn't started on a storage without room for it and some to spare (`requireRoom`, the `ExportRoom` an export is written to). Export makes each file into the cache (`makeExport`, a folder of its own among the exports) and saves or shares it from there, a full storage told apart from a map's file that went (`OutputFailed`); a print's page is a file too, which its web view reads as it loads (`MapPrinter.pageFile`). A TV the map is cast to still gets it as one string, its pictures shrunk to its screen and the first 48 MiB of them only (`inlinePictures`'s `most`), each read only once it fits. How a file goes is worked out once while it's the same file, by its path, size and time (`OutgoingMedia.of`'s plans), so the export panel and the send sheet read no sound twice; an SVG past 32 MiB, cleaned in memory, isn't read through, and goes only as it is, when chosen.

  Edits are saved as they happen, one write after another, each only while the file is still the text it was made from (`MapRepository.write`), and only when it was made on the text the screen has: each `Load` and `Restore` carries the screen's revision, which the page's `saved` echoes, so a save made before a change from elsewhere reached the page goes into the history instead (`isStale`). Saves wait in `SaveQueue`: the screen's newest text, which takes the place of an older one still waiting since it holds all that one did, and each text set aside, in turn, which nothing takes the place of; a closing map keeps what's still set aside. The source editor's typing carries the change of the map the editor's text showed (`SourceEditing`), and is saved only while that's the newest: typing on the text before a change goes into the history too. A write never leaves half a file (`writeAtomically`: written beside, under a name of its own for each write, synced to disk, then renamed, and the rename synced too; partial files a crash left are cleared at the next start). A picture added to a map that's gone to the trash meanwhile doesn't bring its folder back, stills are named so two in one millisecond don't meet, and a live photo's motion added again gets a name of its own rather than going over the one earlier versions point at. Folders of new maps are filled while hidden and then named, so a crash never leaves half a map, their details first, so a private one is private from its first file on.

  Android's backup goes through `OrbitBackupAgent`, within the rules in `res/xml`: it keeps each backup inside the quota its transport gives (`FullBackupDataOutput.getQuota`), the maps' text and the settings first, then their pictures, sounds and videos, each smallest first (`backupFiles`), and takes the history only when moving to a new phone. Private maps go only into a backup that's encrypted end to end or goes to a new phone (`takesPrivateMaps`, from the transport's flags): any other leaves out their folders and history, and takes every other map. Whether a map is private is looked at again right before each of its entries is written (`backUp`), its folder going right before its first file since the folder's name is the map's, so a map made private while the backup is made stays out from then on, and one whose details can't be read counts as private; a map still being made, in its hidden folder, never goes. It carries how many it left out, a count only (`notePrivateMapsLeftOut`, written beside the maps as the backup's last file and deleted once it's made), which a restore moves to where Home reads it (`keepRestoredPrivateMapsNote`, from `onRestoreFinished`), so a restored phone says once how they come back (`PrivateRestoreCard`): from a synced folder as ordinary maps, or from an Export all, still private. Restoring is Android's own, but each file is written beside its place and takes it only once all of it came (`restoreFile`), the folder's deletions too, whose bytes Orbit reads itself, since Android restores a file the rules name on its own only in its very place (`restoreWhole`); and each file's time, which Android sets in seconds where it takes milliseconds, is put right (`onRestoreFile`).
- **Every version opens what the earlier ones left, and keeps what later ones wrote.** A change to what the app keeps follows these rules:
  - **The files' layout** (`StorageLayout`): a change that moves or renames files is a new step in `LAYOUT_STEPS` and raises `LAYOUT_VERSION`. A step must be safe to run again after a crash midway, and gets a test that starts from the older layout's files (`MapRepositoryTest`). `LAYOUT_COMPATIBLE` is the oldest layout whose app may still change files laid out this way: raise it too when an older app would harm the new layout by writing to it, and that app then only reads the maps. The record's `version` and `compatible` stay whole numbers, and fields are only added to it: a record that's there but can't be read leaves the maps only read too.
  - **`meta.json`** (`MapMetadata`): a new field is optional, with a default that means what files without it mean. A field is never renamed or given another shape: add a new one instead. What this version doesn't know or can't read stays in the file when it writes it, at every depth: a field a newer version added inside `sync` too (`MetadataFile`, `keepUnknownFields`), and the folder's notes keep the kinds a newer version added the same way, so going back to an older version and forward again loses nothing. Such a field stays as it was while an older version changes the fields beside it, so it mustn't count on them staying as it left them. Details fail closed: a `private` this version can't read, and a `meta.json` it can't read at all, read as a private map's (`MetadataFile.unreadable`), the former going back as it came while the map stays private; a damaged `meta.json` is kept aside (`meta.json.unreadable-<time>`) as it's first written over, and one that can't be read for now is never written over (`readMetadataToChange`), nor are the folder's deletions (`readTombstones`), whose records are read one by one, a `private` one can't read counting as private. Deletions that can't be read at all, damaged, are no more taken for none than ones out of reach for now: a sync does nothing until they can be read, and says why (`data_folder_deletions_unreadable`), rather than bring a deleted private map's file back as a map like any other. A map's identity (`uuid`) is its own: two maps that share one, as Export all could leave them before 0.4.0, are told apart as the list is read, the one made later (or, with the same or no time, the later folder by name) getting an identity of its own, and only its `meta.json` is written.
  - **Settings** (`SettingsRepository`): a new key has a default, a key is never renamed and keeps its type, and a choice is kept by its name, so one this version doesn't know reads as the default, as does a value of another type. `update` writes only the keys whose value changes, so a choice this version can't read stays as the newer version left it.
  - **Backups** come back onto any version (`restoreAnyVersion`), an older one too, which these rules make safe.
  - **The maps' text** is Orbit's format, which has versions of its own (Orbit's `docs/format.md`, "Versions"): a map written for a newer one opens read-only.
  - **The app's own kinds of idea** that count (`goal` and `progress`): their fields are only ever added, meaning the same. A change older apps would misread raises the kind's version, written as Orbit writes a type's (`goal@2`), and those apps take such an idea for one like any other: not counted, without its ring or its steps (`GOAL_VERSION` and `PROGRESS_VERSION`, in `web/goal.ts`, `web/look.ts` and `page/Protocol.kt`).
  - **The folder maps are kept in is shared by every version that syncs it.** Each device keeps a record of itself there (`.orbit/devices/<id>.json`, `FolderDevice`): its Orbit, the newest map format it reads (`MAP_FORMAT`, which `OrbitBuildTest` holds to the pages' Orbit as `orbit.json` names it), the kinds of file it carries (`carriedMediaTypes`, and the kinds of the ideas' files the folder holds, so a version from before ideas' files says it can't open them; `ideaFiles` says it keeps those of every kind), and how big a map's files may be there (`mediaFileLimit`, `mapMediaLimit`; a record without them is 0.4.0's, which keeps 50 MB and 90 MB, `keepsFile`, `keepsMapMedia`). A sync that finds a device keeping less than this one lists, by device, the folder's maps it keeps only part of (`FolderRoster.partlyKept`, `FolderSyncState.partly`), which Settings names beside it. A field is only ever added, a device keeps what a newer Orbit wrote in its own record, and it never writes or deletes another's; nor does it write its own over one a newer Orbit marks as one it would misread (`compatible`), as after going back to an older version, or over one it can't read for now (`FolderDeviceRecords.update`). The record carries `format` (`DEVICE_FORMAT`, 1) and `compatible`, the oldest format that reads it right: a change older versions would misread raises `compatible`, and they leave the record out. Its id (`ThisDevice`) stays out of backups, and an install that finds one another install made, as a cloning backup tool copies every file, makes its own, so two installs never share a record. A map a sync writes for a newer format than another device in the folder reads (`FolderRoster.leftBehind`, checked as each map is written) goes in the folder's notes (`FolderNotes.newer`), and Settings says which maps that device can only show; `FolderNotice.Older` with `showsOnly` says, on Home and in Settings, which device it is. The version that first writes a newer format than 1 relies on that check before upgrading a map in a shared folder. Every release lists only the folder's top-level `.md` files whose names don't start with a dot (`DocumentFolder.files()`), so `.orbit/` stays invisible to them: anything a newer version keeps in the folder for itself goes there.
  - **Study goes through the folder in records each device keeps of its own** (`.orbit/study/<id>/<map's file>.json`, `StudyRecord`, `data/FolderStudy.kt`): its answers to the map's cards, by idea id, each with the moment it was given (`StudyCard.at`), and the map's earlier files (`was`), so a map renamed on one device keeps the answers the others still keep under its old name. A device writes and deletes only its own records and reads everyone's, so devices studying at once never write the same file, and a card goes as its latest answer on any of them left it (`mergedProgress`). A record a sync can't read, or another device's it can't make out, is read again by the next one; until then, what that device studied stays as it was here, and a device that can't read its own records doesn't write them. A record carries `format` (`STUDY_FORMAT`, 1) and `compatible`, the oldest format that reads it right: a newer one keeps every field an older one reads, meaning the same, so it's read for what it says that this version knows, and a change older versions would misread raises `compatible`, so they leave the record out, as if it weren't there (`StudyRead.Incompatible`), unlike one they can't make out at all, whose device's answers stay until a later sync reads it (`StudyRead.Garbled`); and a device keeps what a newer Orbit added to its own records, and to each card (`StudyCard.extras`, until this version answers the card). A record of its own that a newer Orbit marks as one this version would misread it neither writes over nor deletes, the map's answers staying on this device meanwhile, so going back to an older version and forward again loses none of the newer one's.
  - **What crosses to other devices carries a version:** an offer's in its `X-Orbit-Version` header and in `GET /key`'s answer. A change to how offers work raises it, and a device refuses an offer in a version it doesn't speak before reading any of it, saying which device to update. Within a version, a device taking maps also says what of a map it opens, and the most a map sent to it may be, in its answers to `GET /key` and to a hello (`mapFormat`, `mediaTypes` and `mapSize`, `DeviceReads`): the answers only ever gain fields, and a sender reads one that isn't there as Orbit 0.3.0 had it (`DeviceReads.RELEASE_0_3_0`; 0.4.0 says all but `mapSize`, and takes 64 MiB, `OFFER_LIMIT_UP_TO_0_4_0`). A map bigger than the other device takes goes no further than its hello, before the sender reveals its half of the code, so nothing shows there, and the sender says to update Orbit there (`SendState.TooBigThere`). The TV receiver works the other way round: it lives apart from the app, so it keeps following every older protocol's commands, and the phone sends it only the commands its protocol knows ("Versions between the parts that live apart", below).
  - **Zips say what made them:** a map's zip, in `orbit.json` beside the map's identity and place (`bundle`, `BUNDLE_FORMAT`; `compatible`; `app`), and a zip of every map, in `orbit-export.json`, its first entry (`export`, `EXPORT_FORMAT`; `compatible`; `app`; the layout and the map format). Their fields are only added, and read one by one, so one a newer version reshapes is lost alone, never the map's identity. A change older apps would misread raises `compatible`, and they refuse the zip, saying to update Orbit. Files of kinds a version doesn't know stay out of what it takes in, and the import says how many. Releases before 0.4.0 ignore the new fields, and skip `orbit-export.json`.
  - **The maps' history:** every release lists only the files named like versions (`VERSION_NAME`: `<time>.md` and `<time>.original.md`), thins out only `<time>.md` files, and takes the whole folder to the trash and back. So what a newer version keeps about the versions goes beside them under another name, as `reasons.json` does. Its entries are by each version's file, a `why` this version doesn't know reads as none, entries and fields it doesn't know stay when it writes the file, and only entries whose version is gone (one an older release thinned out, say) are dropped. It's written as a version is kept, never as a map opens, and Export all carries it, which older releases' imports skip.
  - **Each release's files are tests.** `app/src/test/resources/releases/` holds what every release left on a phone, made by its own code, and `ReleaseDataTest` opens each one with this version: every map, picture, video, version, trashed map and setting has to be there, opening them must write nothing, and they take changes without losing what they had. `web/releases.test.ts` reads their maps, versions and trash with the pages' Orbit, as the map's page does: none is locked or misread, each is saved as it was written, and their kinds of idea and goals are the app's. A release adds its own (`RELEASE.md`).
- **The folder maps are kept in** is reached through Android's Storage Access Framework (`ACTION_OPEN_DOCUMENT_TREE` and `DocumentsContract`), so it can be any folder the system's picker offers. Each map remembers its file and how both sides were when they last matched: the text's hash, and the file's time and size, and when a sync first missed the file. So a sync tells which side changed, and a file just as it was isn't read again. `planSync` plans each sync as plain data, which the tests cover, and `FolderSync` carries it out. Files are read in whatever encoding they were written (`decodeText`: a UTF-8 file with a stray byte keeps its characters, and only that byte reads as Windows-1252), so writing them back never loses a character. After writing, a read-back that's someone else's text isn't written over again. A media file that isn't its listed size (an empty one too) is still arriving and isn't copied. The folder's sync writes into a locked private map without asking, on purpose: it's the owner's own sync, running in the background, and asking would stall it until they unlocked; the map stays private and locked, and the notes don't name it. A private map being presented stops as it locks (`PresentationLocking`): `stopPresentation` sends the audiences `End` at once, even with the screen off, so browsers hear it ended and a TV or second screen goes back to waiting; a TV hears it ahead of whatever was still on its way, such as a map whose pictures were being shrunk, which then never goes (`CastAudience`), and stays connected for the next presentation. They close as the map's screen next draws, and until then the room's server gives out none of the map's pictures or videos, which it serves only while a map is shown, one still being made ready as the map locked included. Unlocked, presenting again is offered. Quick capture and the widget's picker keep themselves out of Recents while they name unlocked private maps, as the main window does (`keepPrivateMapsOutOfRecents`).

Then Recents gets no picture: `setRecentsScreenshotEnabled(false)` on Android 13 and later, and `FLAG_SECURE` before, the only way there, which also stops screenshots and screen casting, so only while something private is on screen. - **Widgets are Jetpack Glance,** with a layout for each of six sizes; the launcher shows the size closest to the widget's among those that fit. Their pictures are the home screen's thumbnails, ready before a widget draws so it never opens on a placeholder. Widgets draw again once changes to the maps settle, at once as a map becomes private (`widgetRedraws`), so its widget shows the lock right away, when the home screen turns light or dark, when the text size changes, and when the wallpaper's colors are turned on or off. Their colors are the app's (`widgetColors`, from `orbitColorScheme`), light and dark for the launcher to pick; before Android 12, which leaves a widget's rounding and progress bar to the launcher, the frame is a tinted rounded shape and the bar a picture of it. Glance runs on WorkManager, whose own permissions join Orbit's in the built app: `RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED` (which reminders ask for too) to schedule the widgets' updates again after a restart, `WAKE_LOCK` so an update isn't cut short by the phone sleeping, and `FOREGROUND_SERVICE` for urgent work on Android 11 and earlier (expedited work runs as a foreground service there). They stay: without them, widgets would stop updating after a restart, or on older phones.
- **Maps between devices are sealed** (`nearby/OfferEncryption.kt`), with standard algorithms from the JDK and Android:
  - A device taking maps makes a P-256 key pair each launch, and gives the public key in its advertisement (`key`, with the `version` of offers it takes) and at `GET /key`.
  - The sender makes a fresh pair, agrees a secret with ECDH, and derives its keys with HKDF-SHA256, salted with both public keys. What's sealed travels in AES-256-GCM chunks of 64 KiB, each with its own nonce, authenticating how many chunks there are and where each goes. Only the sender's public key and the version travel in the clear.
  - An offer starts with a hello (`POST /offer`): the sender's name and its commitment to its half of the check code, sealed. The receiver answers with its own half, the sender reveals its half (`POST /offer/<id>`), and both show the code, which comes from the agreed keys and both halves: no one in between can pick keys until the codes match.
  - The map and its pictures go (`POST /offer/<id>/map`), sealed with a key of their own, only once the person sending says the other device shows the same code. So a device that answers in another's place on the Wi-Fi gets nothing of the map. An offer holds its place under the limits from its hello on, and the receiver refuses a map it isn't waiting for, or has no room for, before reading any of it: room for it once, as it opens straight from the connection and unpacks into the files its offer waits in (`readBundle`, looking at the room as it goes), besides what the maps arriving with it will take. Nothing of it counts until all of it has opened, its last chunk checked (`readToEnd`); a map cut short on its way keeps its offer, to come whole while it has time. Its map has to start coming within `CONFIRMING_LIFETIME_MILLISECONDS` of the code showing: an upload under way then may finish, and one cut short after that ends the offer. A map that doesn't become an offer, whatever stops it, leaves no file, and its offer ends; one the storage runs out of room for as it opens stops there.
  - Where an offer is (`GET /offer/<id>`: confirming, waiting, opened or declined) comes with the receiver's proof, an HMAC with a key of the offer's own (`stateProof`), so a sender whose codes matched believes "opened" or "declined" only from that device. The receiver says it proves its answers in the first eight bytes of its half of the check code (`provesStates`): the code both people compare covers them, so no one in between can take the mark off without the codes differing, as TLS 1.3 marks its server's random against downgrades. Orbit 0.3.0 and 0.4.0 send a half all random and no proof, and a sender takes their answers at their word, as before; they read past the proof. Answered offers are said for ten minutes, the newest 256 (`OfferStates`).
  - Version 2 is the one spoken. Orbit 0.2.0 and earlier (version 1) sent the map first: neither side offers to the other, and each says which device to update.

  The receiver is the one part every version of the app shares. So it only ever adds to what it understands, and a new receiver is published before an app that needs it ships (RELEASE.md). Anyone on the network can join a TV's Cast session, so it takes nothing on trust: it joins a command's parts in whatever order they come, and drops a part that isn't one, a command of more than `MOST_PARTS` parts or `MOST_COMMAND_LENGTH` characters, and parts that don't make JSON.
- **Pictures, videos and sounds full screen** (`media/`) are a window of their own over the app, so they cover the list beside the map on a tablet, take the keys and Back, and keep light system bars on the dark. Their bars are see-through from the app's theme (`Theme.Orbit`), as a private map's lock window's are: Android 15 and later make every window's so, and on 10 to 14 the theme does it without the deprecated `Window` bar colors. Back's predictive swipe shrinks them as it goes.
  - **Pictures** are [Telephoto](https://github.com/saket/telephoto)'s zoomable images on the app's Coil: big ones are read a part at a time (subsampling), so they stay sharp zoomed in without filling memory, and its flick closes the viewer. The app builds Coil's image loader itself (`OrbitApplication.newImageLoader`): coil-gif's `AnimatedImageDecoder` plays animated GIFs and WebPs with Android's own `ImageDecoder`, at the size they're drawn, so a big GIF takes no more memory than the screen; Telephoto draws them whole, since an animation can't be read a part at a time. Before it, `StillAnimations` (`media/AnimatedPictures.kt`) decodes their first frame while Android's animations are off, unless a request asks to play them, as the viewer's play button does; that frame isn't kept in Coil's memory, so turning animations back on plays them at once. The map's page, which draws thumbnails too, gets an animation's first frame instead of its file (`page/FirstFrames.kt`, a PNG made once in the cache for each version of the file): a planet that moved would keep the page drawing, and the battery with it, for as long as it's on screen. The audience's screen, the room's browsers and web pages get the file itself. The gallery is the map's pictures, videos and sounds in the order the page reports its nodes, the outline's (`galleryOf`).
  - **Video files** play in Media3's ExoPlayer, with Media3's Compose surface and Material controls. The player is made when a video opens and released when it closes, so maps carry nothing of it. Release builds turn Media3's own logging off as Orbit starts (`OrbitApplication.onCreate`), whichever of its players plays first: its errors can name a map's folder or a video's site. The view that prints a map keeps the page's console out of the log too, as the map's page does (`QuietChrome`).
  - **Sounds** play in one ExoPlayer for the open map (`media/SoundPlayback.kt`), made when a sound first plays and released with the map's screen, so the small player over the map, the details, the editor, the viewer and the presentation all show the one sound playing, and it goes on as people fly around. Starting another sound stops the one before first, so one that can't play never leaves the last one playing under its "can't be played". The player is behind `SoundPlayer`, which the tests play with a stand-in. It holds the phone's audio focus, and pauses when another app takes it, when headphones come out, and when Orbit leaves the screen.
- **Sketches** (`sketch/`, `map/Sketching.kt`, `map/SketchOverlay.kt`) are drawn on the planet itself. The page brings the planet up close with Orbit's `spotlight`, in the room the sketch's toolbar leaves: the camera flies, the map dims under its style's sheet backdrop with the planet's circle cut out, and the page takes no input. It says where the circle rests (`MapMessage.Spotlight`, with the color it dims with, which the screen dims the bars with too), and again as the room changes, and the screen lays its canvas exactly over that circle, the planet's own color for paper. The description that Done brings up is over the whole window (`SketchDescription`), above the keyboard, rather than in the map's part of it: on a phone on its side the keyboard leaves less room than the map has under the top bar, so it lies over the dimmed bars, in one row there. Done writes the drawing as a new SVG in the map's folder (`MapRepository.addSketch`, `sketch-` and the moment), sets it as the idea's picture in one `update`, waits for the page to say the planet shows it (`pictureShown`), and only then lifts the canvas and lets the page fly back (`endSpotlight`), so the planet never shows without its sketch. Orbit holds the planet in place under the canvas through that edit.
  - **The canvas** draws the strokes kept as a layer of their own, and the stroke being drawn over them on each move, from every sample the touch made since the last (`pointerInteropFilter`), dispatched as they come (`requestUnbufferedDispatch`), and a frame or two ahead with AndroidX's motion prediction (`MotionEventPredictor`). `SketchSession` turns touches into strokes, and is plain state the JVM tests drive: the palm rejection (a finger draws nothing while a stylus is near, or for `STYLUS_GRACE` after; a stroke a finger or palm was drawing goes when the stylus comes down; `FLAG_CANCELED` takes a stroke back), the whole-stroke eraser, and undo. A stroke's outline (`StrokeOutline`) follows its line at each point's width, from a stylus's pressure and lean or a finger's speed, round at its ends and where it turns too sharply for its sides to follow, a run of its own from there; the runs turn the same way, so the nonzero rule fills them as one.
  - **The SVG is the sketch** (`SketchSvg`): its round paper, and each stroke as its filled outline, a smooth curve through the outline's corners in tenths of a unit, clipped to the paper. Nothing else is kept, so nothing has to survive beside the drawing: every element and attribute is one the outgoing cleaner keeps, so a sketch goes out as the same drawing (the cleaner writes it again, with an XML declaration at its top) and reopens on another device, and the eraser and undo work on the outlines as they were read. The class `orbit-sketch` marks Orbit's own: a later version that draws sketches otherwise names them otherwise, and this one shows them as pictures. AndroidX Ink was weighed for the canvas: its native library would add about 5 MB to every APK that carries all of Android's processors, and its strokes are meshes with input to replay, not a drawing to keep.
  - **The app's own views** draw sketches through `media/SketchPictures.kt`, a Coil decoder for Orbit's sketches alone (Coil reads no other SVG), as the canvas draws them; the details and the editor show them whole rather than cropped.
  - **Earlier versions** show a sketch as any picture: it's an `image` like any other, and `.svg` is among the kinds every release from 0.3.0 keeps and carries in zips, sending, backups and the folder. Orbit 0.4.0's own views don't draw SVGs, so its details and full screen show the picture's problem, as for any SVG, while its map shows the sketch.
- **Talking ideas onto a map** (`voice/Listening.kt`, `map/Talk.kt`) listens through the phone's `SpeechRecognizer`, one made for each talk and destroyed as the talk ends, so a speech service that answers late, or never, gives nothing to a talk that's over and keeps no microphone; it's behind `Recognizer`, which the tests drive with a stand-in. What's said before each pause joins the map as one `AddNodes` change, one at a time, so each save is known as the talk's (`SpokenIdeas`): only the first keeps the map for undo, and the talk's Undo is offered once its last ideas are saved, taking back the talk while it's still the last change. The microphone is one thing's at a time: the recorder, a presentation, a study session and the map's lock each end a talk as they start (`MapViewModel.talkable`), and a talk can't begin while another finishes its last idea.
- **Voice memos** are `MediaRecorder`'s (`media/VoiceRecorder.kt`): AAC, one channel, 48 kHz at 96 kbps, into a take in the cache that joins the map once it's saved (`MapRepository.addRecording`). Its limit, the room the map has left, is kept by the recorder's own clock: `setMaxFileSize` would reserve room for an index as big as the limit allows, 300 KB of nothing in every memo. The microphone is asked for only as a memo starts, and held only while it records. The take plays back in the phone's `MediaPlayer` (`map/TakePlayer.kt`), with transient audio focus while it plays: it pauses when that's lost, ducking included, waits while a call holds it, and pauses as headphones come out. A take outlasts a screen made again, as for a new language or font size (`rememberVoiceRecorder`): its file's name, time and waveform are saved state, and the screen that goes lets the microphone go, which leaves the take whole in its file, back as a finished take. A sound from the phone is known by its first bytes before its type (`soundKind`), since Android's own types call an `.m4a` "audio/mpeg".
  - **YouTube and Vimeo** play in their own embedded players, in a web view apart from the map's: no channel to the app, no files or content, JavaScript only because the players need it, no third-party cookies, and nothing loads in it but the players' frames (a content policy says so too); other pages open in a tab. YouTube's player refuses to play ("Error 153") for a page that doesn't say where it is, so the player's page is loaded with `loadDataWithBaseURL` as the app's id, `https://com.hyuchia.orbit`, as YouTube asks of apps, and its frame's requests carry that Referer. Vimeo plays its videos protected, so its player may use the phone's protected-content system, and nothing else a browser offers.
- **Pictures and videos to the audience** go as commands (`AudienceCommand.Media`, `Playback`, `Seek` and `CloseMedia` in `page/Protocol.kt`), which the audience's page turns into Orbit's media viewer (`openMedia` with `startAt`, `playMedia`, `pauseMedia`, `seekMedia`, `unmuteMedia`). The phone reckons where a video is from what it sends (`present/ShownMedia.kt`); a second screen's and a TV's word on their video (`mediaState`) corrects it, and a browser that joins halfway opens the video where the phone reckons it is.
  - **Sounds stay on the phone:** they play from the presenter's phone, so they never go to the audience's screens.
  - **Videos seek** because both servers answer for part of a file. The phone's web server answers a `Range` request with 206 and `Content-Range`, reading only that part (`nearby/ByteRanges.kt`). In the WebView, maps' files answer the same way (`page/MapFiles.kt`), holding the whole file: WebView skips to the part a request asks for by itself, in any stream an app answers with.
- **App and page talk in JSON.**
  - The page says hello on a `WebMessageListener` channel limited to its own origin and its main frame, and talks to the app on it (`MapMessage` in `page/Protocol.kt`).
  - The app sends its commands back on the same channel, to the frame that said hello (`MapCommand`): the page reads them as JSON, so nothing the app sends, text from other apps included, is ever run as code.
  - Jobs such as pictures and conversions carry a number that their answer returns. A job is let go once it's answered, given up on after a minute, or no longer wanted by whoever asked, as a thumbnail that scrolled away; one the page already has, the app then calls off (`cancel`): the page aborts its pictures' loading and stops at its next step, before it reads or draws the picture on a canvas, and answers nothing (`working` in `web/bridge.ts`).
  - Commands sent before the page says hello wait for it, only as long as they matter (`WaitingCommands`): a page that didn't load takes none, and a map loaded or unloaded makes the commands about the one before it moot.
  - After every change the page sends the whole map: each node with its attributes, its custom ones included, and the color it's drawn with (Orbit's `getNodeColor`).
  - A map's numbers reach the app as numbers its types hold. Orbit takes any number for how many levels a map shows, an idea's priority and the version of a kind of idea (`maxDepth: 1.5`, `priority: 1e12`, `goal@99999999999`), and a message with one the app's `Int` can't hold would be lost whole: the map never ready, or a save with its edit. So the page sends each as the whole number nearest what it means, within an `Int`'s range (`web/whole-numbers.ts`: 1.5 levels show what 2 do, and a version past the range is still newer than any the app knows), and the app reads any number there (`MapWholeNumberSerializer`). The map's text keeps them as they were written.
  - Both sides are held to one set of examples: `web/protocol-examples.ts` has one of each command and message, held to the pages' own types with a key for every kind, and the pages' build writes them into `app/src/test/resources/page-protocol.json`. `ProtocolTest` checks that the app writes each command exactly so, reads each message with all it says, and knows the same kinds. A message the app still can't read is an error in the log of every build, by its kind alone.
  - The app changes a goal's numbers by merging them into the idea's custom attributes, and steps it with `addToGoal`, which adds to the number the page has, so quick steps all count.
  - A box of a note's checklist checked in the details goes as `checkNote`, the idea and the box's line in its note, and the page checks it in the note it has (Orbit's `setNoteCheckbox`), so quick presses all count and the file changes on that line alone: Orbit writes a note's changed lines in place when it can. One no longer on its line, as after the note changed, is `declined`. Until a save shows the box so, the app shows it as it was pressed (`MapUi.noteChecks`), and that save's message offers Undo while it's still the last change.
  - An earlier version from the history is drawn with `preview`, and the page takes no change and saves nothing until the map as it is now comes back (`web/changing.ts`): an editable `preview` of it, a `restore` or a `load`. The app brings it back itself before any change it sends (`showNow`), and closes the history before what takes the map over (`closeHistory`), so a change always lands on the map as it is now. While a version is on show, the app keeps the map's state as it is now, and takes only whether it can be changed from what the page says.
  - A picture's, video's, sound's or page's caption, description or live photo's motion goes to the page alone (`mediaChange`, `motionChange`), and the page merges it into the node's own value (`web/media.ts`): every other field stays as it is, those Orbit reads and those it keeps for a newer version or another app, which it carries through edits of the same file. Only another file is a whole new value.
- **Dates remind like a calendar's** (`reminders/`). An idea's date is Orbit's `due` attribute, in the map's text, so every device with the map reminds of it. The app reads one as Orbit does (`IdeaDate`, Orbit's `parseDue`): a day, or a day and a time, in local time with no time zone, so a reminder is at that time wherever the phone is, and a date alone at 9:00. The editor and the long-press menu set it with Material's pickers, through the page's `update` (`due`, `null` to take it off), and the details and the outline show it, overdue once past unless the idea is done, as the planet's chip does.
  - **What reminds:** `Reminders` reads the maps' dates with the light reading (`MapText.dates`), never a page, and again only for a map whose text changed (`contentKey`), wherever it changed: the app follows the repository's maps (`keepRemindersCurrent`), so edits, the folder, maps sent or imported and restored versions all count. Done ideas don't remind; maps in the trash, or that can't be read, aren't read. What was read is kept in `reminders.json` beside the maps, with when each reminder was first seen and the moment reminders were last handled up to, so an alarm, a restart, a new time or time zone, or an update rings without reading a map (`ReminderReceiver`). A reminder comes once: one whose moment passed while the phone was off comes as it starts again, but one first seen after its moment (a date set in the past, a map arriving with past dates) never does. Turned off in Settings, none come and no alarm is set; turned on again, only those still to come do.
  - **When:** without exact alarms, which Android 12 and later keep for alarm clocks and calendars, two alarms for the next reminder: `setWindow`, within ten minutes of its moment, the shortest window Android gives, and `setAndAllowWhileIdle` for a phone dozing then, which holds windowed alarms back. Whichever rings first shows it; the other finds it shown. WorkManager would wait for a dozing phone's maintenance windows, hours apart.
  - **What a notification says:** the idea and its map, and only "A reminder" on a lock screen that hides what notifications say (`setPublicVersion`). A private map's says only "A reminder from a private map", anywhere, whether it's locked or not, since the notification outlives the unlocking. A tap opens the map as Orbit's own intent (`OpenMark`) with the idea (`IdeaToShow`: its name, date and place), which the map's view model finds among the page's ideas (`ideaAmong`) and points at, as search does, once it's read; behind `PrivateMapGate`, a locked private map asks for the screen lock first, as its view model is made only then. Notifications are asked for on Android 13 and later as the first date is set, once, saying why (`NotificationsAsk`); Settings offers them again while they're off.
  - **Times on the map** follow the phone's 12- or 24-hour clock: the pages' language carries its hour cycle (`withHourCycle`, `-u-hc-h23`), which Orbit's `Intl` follows.
- **Study mode never writes the map** (`study/`). A session's cards are the ideas under where it began with ideas or a note under them (`cardsUnder`). `StudyControl` asks the page to hide the ideas under every one of them, with Orbit's `hideChildren`, so revealing one card never gives away another's, then flies to each card and reveals it with `revealChildren`: that's only how the page draws the map, so nothing is edited, saved or added to the history, and nothing reaches the folder maps are kept in or other devices. Each answer is kept at once in the map's `meta.json` (`MapMetadata.study`): a card's Leitner box, the day it's due and the day it was first studied, by idea id, following an idea's new id as the path does (`renameIdea`), and dropped for ideas the map no longer has as a session begins, once the map was read whole (`readOnly` is null: a map Orbit couldn't read all of, or a newer Orbit made, may show only some of its ideas, or give them other ids, and the page reports a map in the trash, or kept by a newer Orbit, in place of either). When new text gives an idea a new id, its card goes on under it as its latest answer on any device left it, kept as this device's own, since the other devices see only the new text, and find the card in this device's record in the folder. The page says an idea's new id in its `saved` (`renamed`), for text the app's editor sent (`updating`) and for text typed on the map, as the field for typing there closes on the idea (`web/renaming.ts`). Days are counted in the phone's time zone (`LocalDate.toEpochDay`). Each answer, and each one taken back, is kept as a change to that one card (`StudyChange`), written in the order they were given against this device's answers as `meta.json` has them then, under the maps' lock (`MapRepository.changeStudy`), never as a copy of them all: so one that lands after the other devices' answers were folded in (the folder was left, `keepStudyHere`, or the map came to another folder, `takeStudyElsewhere`), or after a session began again, loses nothing. They're written on the app's scope, so a map that closes still writes the ones on their way (`StudyControl.close`), and a session that begins again (Keep going, Study anyway) counts the ones not written yet. Undo gives the card back as this device's own answer before it, or, once the other devices' answers were folded into this device's, as they all left it; an answer the folder brought in later stays. A card the map no longer has as a card, as it changed elsewhere while it's studied (a session takes no edits), leaves the session at once and takes no answer (`Study.without`). A box under the first or past the last, as another version may write, counts as the nearest, and stays as it was written until the card is answered (`answered`). Home counts the cards due among the map's cards only: as the map's page reads it whole, the ideas with answers that aren't cards (gone, or with no ideas or note under them) are noted in `meta.json` (`studyNotCards`, by `noteCards`), and their answers stay for when they're cards again; Home's day moves on at midnight, as the phone's date, time or time zone changes, and as Orbit comes back on screen (`rememberStudyDay`). A page that loads the map again (its process stopped, say) gets the hidden ideas and the card back (`pageLoaded`). Behind a private map's lock (`LocalMapLocked`, from `PrivateMapGate`), a session holds still: it takes no keys or focus, keeps the screen on no longer and says nothing to TalkBack, and it's just where it was once unlocked. A studied map takes no drops (`takesDrops`), and its ideas don't drag out.
- **Orbit's own features, used as they're meant to be:**
  - `editable` with `longPressDelay` set to Android's long-press timeout;
  - `focus` to reopen a map where you left it;
  - `hideChildren` and `revealChildren` for study mode's cards;
  - node types with `render` hooks for people, progress rings and goals. Orbit draws again only the nodes an edit touched, so the app draws its rings again in place after each edit, for the Progress idea above a goal, moving to the new share;
  - `nodeTypeFor`, which draws ideas with to-dos or goals under them as Progress ideas without writing the kind into the map, on the map, the audience's screens and web pages alike;
  - `showActionButtons: false`, with the idea's buttons in the native toolbar instead, and `onAction` taking over the detail sheet for the native one;
  - `insets`, so the map fits around the toolbar, the rail, sheets and captions;
  - `baseUrl` serving each map's pictures from its folder;
  - themes and palettes from the frontmatter, and `serializeOrbitMarkdown` and `serializeOrbitYAML` for files.
- **Keys go where they belong:**
  - The map's page gets the keys first, and the app's shortcuts take what it leaves (`WebViewClient.onUnhandledKeyEvent`). So typing in an idea keeps Ctrl+Z for its text.
  - Shortcuts no text field uses, such as Ctrl+F, the views and Ctrl+/, are caught before anything else.
  - The page keeps its own focus when the view takes the keyboard's (`setNeedInitialFocus(false)`). The map takes the keyboard's focus back whenever nothing covers it, even after the layout moves it.
- **Native feel, on purpose:**
  - **The page is transparent:** Compose paints behind it, so there's never a white flash.
  - **Notes are drawn natively** (`ui/MarkdownNote`), with the markdown renderer's own pieces for text, lists and pictures, and the note's for tables and checklists: a table is a layout of its own (`ui/NoteTable`), each column as wide as its cells on one line up to a cap, scrolling sideways when it's wider than the note, and a list with checkboxes (`ui/NoteChecklist`) makes each item one row that toggles, as a Material checkbox with its words. Neither reads alignment lines as it measures, so both lay out in a pane's lookahead pass on a tablet. The editor's preview checks a box in the note being written (`withNoteBox`), saved with it.
  - **Messages over sheets:** while a panel is a sheet over the map, the map's messages show over it, in a window of their own at the screen's foot (`LocalSheetMessages`), as the map's own place for them is under the sheet.
  - **No web tells:** no text selection, callouts or tap highlights.
  - **Haptics:** Orbit's spin haptics play native clock ticks, and long presses and new ideas get their own.
  - **Back:** predictive back on every screen.
  - **One of several choices** is Material 3 Expressive's connected buttons when there are a few (`ui/Choices`, `ConnectedChoice`), which become a list of radio buttons when their words don't fit on one line, and chips when there are many (`ChoiceChips`). TalkBack hears both as radio buttons in a group. Material's filter chips are checkboxes to it, so `ChoiceChips` sets the radio role outside the chip's own, which Compose applies last.
  - **The keyboard:** it comes up when a menu opens the field for typing, and the map makes room for it at once.
  - **Times and dates** follow the phone's 12- or 24-hour clock, its time zone and its language (`ui/TimeWords`), the clock and the zone as they change while they're on screen (`rememberTimeWords` hears Android say so): Android's pattern for the fields wanted (`DateFormat.getBestDateTimePattern`), since java.time's localized styles ignore the clock setting. A pattern ICU had to pad (`├…┤`), or one without the fields asked for, gives way to a plain one, and a calendar or hour cycle in the language's tag changes nothing.
