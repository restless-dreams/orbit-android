- **Maps are folders.** Each holds:
  - `map.md`, the map in orbit-md;
  - `media/`, the pictures added to it, kept at most 2048 pixels on their longer side, as JPEG or PNG (an animated GIF or WebP as it came, up to 50 MB, since redrawing it would keep its first frame alone: `data/Animations.kt` reads its frames without decoding them), the videos added from the phone as they came (MP4, WebM or QuickTime, by their type), each with a frame from it beside it as its poster, and its sounds: voice memos recorded in Orbit (`memo-…`) and sounds from the phone as they came (`sound-…`). Memos are AAC in an `.mp4`, and an M4A is kept as the `.mp4` it is, because every release carries `.mp4` files in its zips, backups, folder and sending; the other sounds keep their own extension (`data/MapSounds.kt`), which earlier releases leave out of those. A live photo is kept as two plain files: its picture, as any picture is kept, and its motion beside it as an MP4 (`picture-…-motion.mp4`), which its picture's `image.motion` points at (`data/LivePhotos.kt` reads the motion out of an Android phone's picture, `data/LivePairs.kt` joins an iPhone's still and movie, and `data/MotionVideos.kt` makes the movie an MP4 without touching its video, with `data/QuickTimeMovies.kt` for what Android's reader can't do: open an older iPhone's movie, which starts without an `ftyp`, and read the uncompressed sound QuickTime describes its own ways);
  - `meta.json`, whether it's pinned or private, when it was opened, where you left it, its lasting identity, its file in the folder maps are kept in, and its cards in study mode: this device's answers (`study`), and the other devices' that the folder brings (`studyElsewhere`), a card going as the latest of them left it;
  - beside the maps, `history/<map>/` holds its earlier versions: one as each visit begins, one for every stretch of editing and one before each big change, thinned out as they age, up to the newest 200 (`versionsToKeep`), and `<time>.original.md` for a file that came in from elsewhere as a new map, as it came, never thinned (a newer version of a map that arrives later is an ordinary version). `reasons.json` beside them says why each was kept, by its file's name (`VersionReason`): what the history shows under each version's time, with what changed from the one before it, worked out as its row comes on screen (`VersionChanges`, `map/VersionSummaries.kt`);
  - `trash/<map>-<time>/` holds a deleted map, with its history, for 30 days;
  - `maps/.layout.json` says which layout the files are in: `StorageLayout` runs each step from an older one (a copy of the maps' text first, in `backups/`), and leaves a newer one alone.

  The settings are a DataStore, `files/datastore/settings.preferences_pb`.

  A map travels between devices as a zip of its text and the pictures, videos and sounds it points at, with `orbit.json` carrying its identity and place. Files kept only for its earlier versions stay on the device. Big files are written, copied and synced a piece at a time, never whole in memory.

  Edits are saved as they happen, one write after another, each only while the file is still the text it was made from (`MapRepository.write`), and only when it was made on the text the screen has: each `Load` and `Restore` carries the screen's revision, which the page's `saved` echoes, so a save made before a change from elsewhere reached the page goes into the history instead (`isStale`). A write never leaves half a file (`writeAtomically`: written beside, under a name of its own for each write, synced to disk, then renamed, and the rename synced too; partial files a crash left are cleared at the next start). A picture added to a map that's gone to the trash meanwhile doesn't bring its folder back, stills are named so two in one millisecond don't meet, and a live photo's motion added again gets a name of its own rather than going over the one earlier versions point at. Folders of new maps are filled while hidden and then named, so a crash never leaves half a map, their details first, so a private one is private from its first file on.

  Android's backup goes through `OrbitBackupAgent`, within the rules in `res/xml`: it keeps each backup inside the quota its transport gives (`FullBackupDataOutput.getQuota`), the maps' text and the settings first, then their pictures, sounds and videos, each smallest first (`backupFiles`), and takes the history only when moving to a new phone. Private maps go only into a backup that's encrypted end to end or goes to a new phone (`takesPrivateMaps`, from the transport's flags): any other leaves out their folders and history, and takes every other map. Whether a map is private is looked at again right before each of its entries is written (`backUp`), its folder going right before its first file since the folder's name is the map's, so a map made private while the backup is made stays out from then on, and one whose details can't be read counts as private; a map still being made, in its hidden folder, never goes. It carries how many it left out, a count only (`notePrivateMapsLeftOut`, written beside the maps as the backup's last file and deleted once it's made), which a restore moves to where Home reads it (`keepRestoredPrivateMapsNote`, from `onRestoreFinished`), so a restored phone says once how they come back (`PrivateRestoreCard`): from a synced folder as ordinary maps, or from an Export all, still private. Restoring is Android's own, but for each file's time, which Android sets in seconds where it takes milliseconds (`onRestoreFile`).
- **Every version opens what the earlier ones left, and keeps what later ones wrote.** A change to what the app keeps follows these rules:
  - **The files' layout** (`StorageLayout`): a change that moves or renames files is a new step in `LAYOUT_STEPS` and raises `LAYOUT_VERSION`. A step must be safe to run again after a crash midway, and gets a test that starts from the older layout's files (`MapRepositoryTest`). `LAYOUT_COMPATIBLE` is the oldest layout whose app may still change files laid out this way: raise it too when an older app would harm the new layout by writing to it, and that app then only reads the maps. The record's `version` and `compatible` stay whole numbers, and fields are only added to it: a record that's there but can't be read leaves the maps only read too.
  - **`meta.json`** (`MapMetadata`): a new field is optional, with a default that means what files without it mean. A field is never renamed or given another shape: add a new one instead. What this version doesn't know or can't read stays in the file when it writes it, at every depth: a field a newer version added inside `sync` too (`MetadataFile`, `keepUnknownFields`), and the folder's notes keep the kinds a newer version added the same way, so going back to an older version and forward again loses nothing. Such a field stays as it was while an older version changes the fields beside it, so it mustn't count on them staying as it left them. Details fail closed: a `private` this version can't read, and a `meta.json` it can't read at all, read as a private map's (`MetadataFile.unreadable`), the former going back as it came while the map stays private; a damaged `meta.json` is kept aside (`meta.json.unreadable-<time>`) as it's first written over, and one that can't be read for now is never written over (`readMetadataToChange`), nor are the folder's deletions (`readTombstones`), whose records are read one by one, a `private` one can't read counting as private. A map's identity (`uuid`) is its own: two maps that share one, as Export all could leave them before 0.4.0, are told apart as the list is read, the one made later (or, with the same or no time, the later folder by name) getting an identity of its own, and only its `meta.json` is written.
  - **Settings** (`SettingsRepository`): a new key has a default, a key is never renamed and keeps its type, and a choice is kept by its name, so one this version doesn't know reads as the default, as does a value of another type. `update` writes only the keys whose value changes, so a choice this version can't read stays as the newer version left it.
  - **Backups** come back onto any version (`restoreAnyVersion`), an older one too, which these rules make safe.
  - **The maps' text** is Orbit's format, which has versions of its own (Orbit's `docs/format.md`, "Versions"): a map written for a newer one opens read-only.
  - **The app's own kinds of idea** that count (`goal` and `progress`): their fields are only ever added, meaning the same. A change older apps would misread raises the kind's version, written as Orbit writes a type's (`goal@2`), and those apps take such an idea for one like any other: not counted, without its ring or its steps (`GOAL_VERSION` and `PROGRESS_VERSION`, in `web/goal.ts`, `web/look.ts` and `page/Protocol.kt`).
  - **The folder maps are kept in is shared by every version that syncs it.** Each device keeps a record of itself there (`.orbit/devices/<id>.json`, `FolderDevice`): its Orbit, the newest map format it reads (`MAP_FORMAT`, which `OrbitBuildTest` holds to the pages' Orbit as `orbit.json` names it) and the kinds of file it carries (`carriedMediaTypes`). A field is only ever added, a device keeps what a newer Orbit wrote in its own record, and it never writes or deletes another's. The record carries `format` (`DEVICE_FORMAT`, 1) and `compatible`, the oldest format that reads it right: a change older versions would misread raises `compatible`, and they leave the record out. Its id (`ThisDevice`) stays out of backups, and an install that finds one another install made, as a cloning backup tool copies every file, makes its own, so two installs never share a record. A map a sync writes for a newer format than another device in the folder reads (`FolderRoster.leftBehind`, checked as each map is written) goes in the folder's notes (`FolderNotes.newer`), and Settings says which maps that device can only show; `FolderNotice.Older` with `showsOnly` says, on Home and in Settings, which device it is. The version that first writes a newer format than 1 relies on that check before upgrading a map in a shared folder. Every release lists only the folder's top-level `.md` files whose names don't start with a dot (`DocumentFolder.files()`), so `.orbit/` stays invisible to them: anything a newer version keeps in the folder for itself goes there.
  - **Study goes through the folder in records each device keeps of its own** (`.orbit/study/<id>/<map's file>.json`, `StudyRecord`, `data/FolderStudy.kt`): its answers to the map's cards, by idea id, each with the moment it was given (`StudyCard.at`), and the map's earlier files (`was`), so a map renamed on one device keeps the answers the others still keep under its old name. A device writes and deletes only its own records and reads everyone's, so devices studying at once never write the same file, and a card goes as its latest answer on any of them left it (`mergedProgress`). A record a sync can't read, or another device's it can't make out, is read again by the next one; until then, what that device studied stays as it was here, and a device that can't read its own records doesn't write them. A record carries `format` (`STUDY_FORMAT`, 1) and `compatible`, the oldest format that reads it right: a newer one keeps every field an older one reads, meaning the same, so it's read for what it says that this version knows, and a change older versions would misread raises `compatible`, so they leave the record out, as if it weren't there (`StudyRead.Incompatible`), unlike one they can't make out at all, whose device's answers stay until a later sync reads it (`StudyRead.Garbled`); and a device keeps what a newer Orbit added to its own records, and to each card (`StudyCard.extras`, until this version answers the card).
  - **What crosses to other devices carries a version:** an offer's in its `X-Orbit-Version` header and in `GET /key`'s answer. A change to how offers work raises it, and a device refuses an offer in a version it doesn't speak before reading any of it, saying which device to update. Within a version, a device taking maps also says what of a map it opens, in its answers to `GET /key` and to a hello (`mapFormat` and `mediaTypes`, `DeviceReads`): the answers only ever gain fields, and a sender reads one that isn't there as Orbit 0.3.0 had it (`DeviceReads.RELEASE_0_3_0`). The TV receiver works the other way round: it lives apart from the app, so it keeps following every older protocol's commands, and the phone sends it only the commands its protocol knows ("Versions between the parts that live apart", below).
  - **Zips say what made them:** a map's zip, in `orbit.json` beside the map's identity and place (`bundle`, `BUNDLE_FORMAT`; `compatible`; `app`), and a zip of every map, in `orbit-export.json`, its first entry (`export`, `EXPORT_FORMAT`; `compatible`; `app`; the layout and the map format). Their fields are only added, and read one by one, so one a newer version reshapes is lost alone, never the map's identity. A change older apps would misread raises `compatible`, and they refuse the zip, saying to update Orbit. Files of kinds a version doesn't know stay out of what it takes in, and the import says how many. Releases before 0.4.0 ignore the new fields, and skip `orbit-export.json`.
  - **The maps' history:** every release lists only the files named like versions (`VERSION_NAME`: `<time>.md` and `<time>.original.md`), thins out only `<time>.md` files, and takes the whole folder to the trash and back. So what a newer version keeps about the versions goes beside them under another name, as `reasons.json` does. Its entries are by each version's file, a `why` this version doesn't know reads as none, entries and fields it doesn't know stay when it writes the file, and only entries whose version is gone (one an older release thinned out, say) are dropped. It's written as a version is kept, never as a map opens, and Export all carries it, which older releases' imports skip.
  - **Each release's files are tests.** `app/src/test/resources/releases/` holds what every release left on a phone, made by its own code, and `ReleaseDataTest` opens each one with this version: every map, picture, video, version, trashed map and setting has to be there, opening them must write nothing, and they take changes without losing what they had. `web/releases.test.ts` reads their maps, versions and trash with the pages' Orbit, as the map's page does: none is locked or misread, each is saved as it was written, and their kinds of idea and goals are the app's. A release adds its own (`RELEASE.md`).
- **The folder maps are kept in** is reached through Android's Storage Access Framework (`ACTION_OPEN_DOCUMENT_TREE` and `DocumentsContract`), so it can be any folder the system's picker offers. Each map remembers its file and how both sides were when they last matched: the text's hash, and the file's time and size, and when a sync first missed the file. So a sync tells which side changed, and a file just as it was isn't read again. `planSync` plans each sync as plain data, which the tests cover, and `FolderSync` carries it out. Files are read in whatever encoding they were written (`decodeText`: a UTF-8 file with a stray byte keeps its characters, and only that byte reads as Windows-1252), so writing them back never loses a character. A folder file that stops partway through a character, or isn't the size it was listed at, is still arriving (`StillArriving`): it waits, and is taken as it is only once it has stayed so for `SETTLING`. Before writing over a file, a sync checks it's still as listed (`SyncFolder.describe`), and after writing, a read-back that's someone else's text isn't written over again. A file over `MAP_TEXT_LIMIT` is never read, and the notes name it (`FolderNotes.large`). A step that fails, whatever the folder's app throws but a refusal (`SecurityException`, which says the folder is out of reach), counts as a map not in step and the others go on; a sync that stops partway keeps the notes of what it did so far (`Run.notes`), and `FolderSyncState.failures` counts syncs in a row with a problem, which Home shows from the second (`FolderProblemCard`). A listing its app marks as failed (`EXTRA_ERROR`) is never taken for the folder's contents. After its steps, a sync fetches the pictures, videos and sounds that maps kept in the folder point at but don't have (`fetchMissingMedia`), as one that reached the folder after its map's text; a map is looked at again only when its text changes (`contentKey`) or while something it points at is missing, and the folder's media is listed only then. A media file that isn't its listed size is still arriving and isn't copied. The folder's one `media/` is shared by every map: when it holds another file under a picture's name (what they hold says so, as a name and a size alone can't: compared once, and again only once either file changes), the picture gets a free name (`MapRepository.renameMedia`, which a map's screen catches up with) before it goes there. Keeping both names the copy through Orbit's own editor (`MapTextEditor`, the spare page's), so a YAML map stays whole, and keeps one Orbit can only show byte for byte under a copy's file name; it records the folder's text as matched as soon as the copy exists, so a sync that fails after it writes the map's text next time instead of keeping both again. A file a write made that holds only the start of its map is deleted rather than left for a later sync to take as a map of its own, and so is a document `DocumentFolder` made whose write failed; a folder's app that only takes "w", which may keep the end of a longer file, has that end cut off (`Os.ftruncate`), or the write fails. A folder with none of the maps' files is written again only once a sync `MISSING_CONFIRMATION` after the first agrees, so a restored phone doesn't write its backup's text into a folder its sync app is still filling; and records carry the install that made them (`SyncRecord.install`: `FolderDevice` ids are never backed up, and a record from before gets this install's at the next sync), so another install's record (a backup's) waits `FOREIGN_CONFIRMATION` for its file, and is taken over once its file matches. More than `MOST_REMOVALS` maps following their files out in one sync are held (`FolderSyncState.held`) for Home to ask (`FolderRemovalsCard`); a map the folder trashes is marked in its details (`MapMetadata.removedByFolder`) and stays in the trash past `TRASH_DAYS` until the note is read, when its days start over (`seeFolderRemovals`). A file that holds only the start of its map's text is a write cut short only if this device was writing that text (`SyncRecord.writing`, set before each write and cleared once the file is read back whole); any other shorter file is someone's change, as deleting a map's last ideas leaves it, and its map takes it once it has stayed as it is for `SETTLING`, so a file still arriving isn't taken (`FolderSyncShorterTest`, with two devices sharing a folder).
- **Pages, ready before they're needed.** A WebView with Orbit's page is made once the app is idle, so a map draws the moment it opens. The map's view model keeps its page through rotations and trips to other screens. When the map closes, the page goes back to the pool, once it has said its last word (what was being typed on it) and converted what was typed in the source editor a moment before (`SourceEditing.flush`), YAML included; the map's last text is written after, on the app's scope, since the view model's own work stops as it's cleared. The view model works with its page through `MapPage` (`page/MapPage.kt`), which `OrbitPage` is, and with the rest of the app through `MapApp` (`map/MapApp.kt`), which `AppContainer` is: a JVM test makes it with maps and settings on files, and a page of its own that says what `page-protocol.json`'s examples say (`MapViewModelTest`).
- **What leaves the phone says no more than it shows.** A map's own files, the ones its text points at where an address starts (`media/…` in a picture, a link, an idea's attributes or a YAML map, and never inside a web address: `MediaNames.references`), go to other people as `OutgoingMedia` makes them, in shared zips and maps sent to a device (`writeZip`), web pages and prints (`inlinePictures`, and a page's sounds in `soundsInside`), SVGs (`OutgoingMedia.drawing`), the browsers a presentation is shown in (`OutgoingCopies`, a clean copy made once and sent in parts), and a picture, video or sound shared from the viewer (`sharePicture`, `shareVideoOrSound`). JPEG, PNG and WebP go as `SharedPictures.withoutMetadata` makes them, and so do GIFs, without comments or other apps' blocks, and SVG drawings, with only what draws them (`media/GifsAndDrawings.kt`). MP4 and QuickTime videos, and the audio kept in MP4, are patched where they are, so every frame and offset stays: their `udta`, `meta` and `meco` boxes and XMP's `uuid`, in the movie, its tracks, their media (`mdia`, `minf`) and its fragments (`moof`, `traf`), and any `uuid` in the movie itself (a camera's own, as Canon's), become `free`, and so do `prft` and the `free` and `skip` boxes already there, which may hold what an editor freed; the dates in their movie, track and media headers become zero, what follows a handler's type and a video's compressor name and QuickTime's vendor in its sample descriptions become zeros, and anything after the last whole box (a phone's own trailer) is left out. HEIF and AVIF pictures have their EXIF and XMP items, wherever `iloc` puts them, written over with zeros, and the video a motion photo keeps in its `mpvd` box is cleaned as any video, where it is. Sounds and WebM are patched the same way, each read through by its own reader, and the tags after what it reads (ID3v1, APE, Lyrics3, `SoundTags`) left out: MP3s and ADTS frame by frame (`MpegFrames`), an ID3v2 tag before or between their frames emptied; FLAC's blocks other than STREAMINFO and SEEKTABLE made PADDING, its last frame found whole by its checksums (`FlacBlocks`); Ogg page by page, each stream's comment header emptied in place and its pages' checksums written again (`OggPages`); WAV's chunks other than those that make the sound made `JUNK` (`WaveChunks`); WebM and Matroska element by element, tags, chapters, attachments, titles, dates and names made `Void`, the apps that made it named with nothing, checksums written again, and segments and clusters of no size read to their end (`MatroskaElements`). A sound cut short at its end goes as far as its last whole piece, and what the cut falls in stays out: `MpegFrames` and `OggPages` stop before the frame or page the file ends in, or before tags after them that it ends in, read from where each starts (`SoundTags.cutShortFrom`), an Ogg once every stream's headers are past and its last page's checksum is right; `FlacBlocks` finds the last whole frame from the end, where its CRC-16 comes to zero before a frame or tags cut short; `WaveChunks` stops at the last whole sample (`nBlockAlign`), or before a chunk cut short after the samples, and writes RIFF's and the samples' sizes, or RF64's in `ds64`, to match; `MatroskaElements` stops before the block the cut falls in, or before the last cluster when none of its blocks is whole, writes the segment's and the cluster's sizes to match (libwebm, Android's own reader, skips a cluster that says it goes on past the file), makes a cut cluster's CRC-32 Void, and the index's entries for what was cut away (ExoPlayer seeks to the cues the index names, and fails when they aren't there). A cut anywhere else, as in a sound's headers or a WebM's cues or tags, still can't be read through, and nor can a movie without its whole `mdat`, as a voice memo cut short. BMPs keep their headers, colors, pixels and embedded profile, and the rest is zeros or left out (`Bitmaps`). Videos keep every track, a camera's GPS track included, as the privacy policy says, and what an encoder writes into a stream stays with it; a file Orbit can't read through, or of no kind it knows, doesn't go, unless the person sharing chose to share it as it is. `MapRepository.uncleaned` finds those files (`Uncleaned`); each way out asks once, as it starts at the phone, through `rememberAsTheyAre` (the export panel, Home's share sheet, the send sheet and its computer share, presenting in browsers or on a TV), and hands the names chosen down as `asTheyAre` to `writeZip`, `WebPage.build`, `OutgoingMedia.drawing`, `OutgoingCopies` and the TV's `inlinePictures`. The viewer asks in its own dialog (`Shared.NotClean`, `ShareAsItIsDialog`, `shareAsItIs`). The servers never ask anyone: with no choice made, those files stay out. The map's own files never change, and Export all, a backup, and the folder maps are kept in take them exactly as they are. Every way out says what goes with the map first (`MediaSummary`, in `ui/MediaWords`'s words).
- **The servers listen only in the room** (`nearby/LocalServer.kt`, the sockets, with its requests and answers in `HttpMessages.kt`, how they're read and written in `HttpWire.kt`, event streams in `EventStream.kt`, and the pages' content policy and every answer's safety headers in `PagePolicy.kt`; `nearby/Addresses.kt`): on the addresses of the Wi-Fi, a hotspot, a cable or USB tethering (`roomAddresses`), following them as the phone joins and leaves networks. They answer only devices in the room's ranges, never another app on the phone itself through the Wi-Fi's own address, and send their pictures and videos with a content policy that keeps an SVG's script, say, from ever running. A request's head has 15 seconds to arrive, and its body 15 seconds and 30 more for each megabyte it says it holds (`ServerTimings`), so a device sending a byte at a time can't hold a connection. Nothing big is made in memory: a body past 256 KiB goes to a file, and a shared map's zip is made in a file for each download and deleted once it's sent.
- **What pages may load.** The pages come with a content security policy: their own scripts and nothing else to run, pictures and videos from the web over HTTPS, and YouTube's and Vimeo's players framed (`PAGE_POLICY` in `page/OrbitPage.kt`). A shared web page carries a policy of its own wherever it's opened, a file, a website or a browser on the Wi-Fi: only its own script runs, by its hash, and what a map holds shows, from inside the page or over HTTPS, but never runs (`sharedPagePolicy` in `page/WebPage.kt`), so an address that slips past Orbit's guard still can't. Nothing goes over the web in the clear: the policy has WebView ask for anything at an `http` address over `https` (`upgrade-insecure-requests`; without it, WebView refuses such a picture), and the app's own views do the same (`pictureAddress`), and load a map's pictures only from the web, from inside the map's text or from its folder, as the page does (`isWebPicture`, for the details, the editor and notes drawn natively); a video at one plays in the browser, as links open there. Of the maps' files, a page gets only the pictures and videos in the media folder of the map it shows, or of one it's drawing a picture of, sent so they never run as a page (`page/MapFiles.kt`): a map can't point it at another map's text or pictures. An animated picture goes to the map's page as its first frame, and a HEIC or HEIF one, which Chromium doesn't decode, as a JPEG made from it, once for each version of the file (`page/FirstFrames.kt`); the file itself stays as it is. Pictures from the phone are made JPEGs as they come in, so only ones that came through the folder or a zip need it. An SVG export takes in only what answers as a picture.
- **What Orbit hands other apps.** Its file provider reaches the cache's exports and all of the maps' folder, since a map's video plays in the phone's video app. But every address Orbit gives out comes from `sharedFileUri` (`ui/Sharing.kt`), which hands out only an export or a file in a map's media folder (`sharesFile`), wherever links in its path lead: never a map's text, versions or details, whatever a later change asks of it.
- **What other apps can hand Orbit.** Files and text another app opens or shares with Orbit (`VIEW` and `SEND` in the manifest) become maps, as Open with and Share are meant to. The types and extensions maps come as are listed once, in `data/MapFileTypes.kt` (other apps' formats carry their own, `OtherFormat`), for the manifest's filters, the file pickers, drops and imports; `MapFileTypesTest` holds the manifest to it. So an app on the phone could add maps no one asked for, but Orbit comes to the front with each one, which goes to the trash like any other map, and nothing goes back: Orbit only reads what it's handed, and answers no app with anything.
- **Drag and drop** (`map/MapDrops.kt`, `home/HomeDrops.kt`, `data/Dropped.kt`, `map/IdeaDrag.kt`):
  - **Onto a map:** the map's WebView has a drag listener (`MapDropListener`), ahead of the page's own, which only takes text into an idea being typed. The page lights up the idea under the finger with Orbit's `getNodeAt` and `setDropTarget`, as its own drags light theirs (`dropOver`, `dropTarget`), and says where a drop lands (`dropAt`).
  - **What a drop adds** goes through what pasting uses (`paste`), or, for several pictures and videos, `addNodes`: one change, one Undo.
  - **Home** takes map files and text through Compose's `dragAndDropTarget`. Compose hands a drop to the last target a drag was over, which a quick drag can leave on the list as it goes on to a map beside it, so the list lights up by where the drag is, and takes only a drop let go over it.
  - **Files** are read with the permissions the drag lends the activity (`requestDragAndDropPermissions`), held until they're in, and only from other apps' content addresses (`importable`), as imports are. Whatever the other app's provider throws back, or Android lending its files, fails only the file it was about, with the app's usual message (`fromProvider` in `data/Dropped.kt`, and the importer's own catches): a permission taken back, one it can't lend, an address it no longer knows, a broken cursor. A text file is read whole, up to a map's text on the list of maps (`MAP_TEXT_LIMIT`) and a list's on a map, and decoded at once, so it's never cut, nor its encoding told from part of it (`readTextFile`).
  - **Several files** that are other versions of maps here are asked about one after another, as one is (`OtherVersions` in `OrbitApp.kt`, through `ImportResult.SeveralFiles`), the private ones behind the screen lock as ever, and what the rest brought is said once they're answered.
  - **Out of Orbit:** an idea lifted on the map and dragged out of the window (`dragOut`) becomes a system drag: Android's `startDragAndDrop`, from the page's view, with a synthetic cancel so the page lets go of the press. Rows of the outline start one too.
  - **Back in:** an idea of the map dropped on it moves. Android gives a drag's local state only to the window it started in, and the outline's sheet is a window of its own, so the idea's map and id also travel in the drag's item, with a flag in its description saying it's one of Orbit's.
  - **Behind a private map's lock** the page takes part in no drag. A map that locks while open stays under the lock's cover (`PrivateMapGate`), and its page is made invisible there, which Android leaves out of drags altogether: neither the map nor a field typed into takes a drop, and nothing lights up. An idea on its way out is called back with `cancelDragAndDrop` from the window it started in. One from the outline's sheet on a phone outlives the sheet, which steps aside as the drag starts, so that one goes on. A file with another version of a locked private map asks for the screen lock before it updates it.
- **The spare page works off screen.** Thumbnails, style previews and exports are Orbit's `renderToSVGString`, turned into WebP or PNG on a canvas in the page. Ideas caught without opening their map are Orbit's `addNode` on a map no one sees, nested as pasting nests them (`web/paste.ts`), so the text comes back the way Orbit writes it. Home's renames, tags and copies are made there too (`editMap`, `web/map-edits.ts`, through `data/MapEdits.kt`): Orbit reads the map, markdown or YAML, makes the change as the map's screen would, and writes it back, only the changed lines in markdown; a map Orbit can only show (`readingOf`) is left as it is, and the app says why. Home's light reading of maps (`MapText.outline`) reads a YAML map's title, tags and ideas as Orbit does. Text indented as an outline that makes a new map is read there too (`mapFromText`), so a shared list keeps its branches; a markdown file never goes there, and is kept as written (`Importer.newMap`). Maps from other apps (OPML, FreeMind and Freeplane, XMind) are Orbit's `importMap` there (`web/other-apps.ts`): `data/OtherApps.kt` tells them from Orbit's own files by name, type and content, and the importer adds each map with its pictures and says, in the app's words, what Orbit kept another way or couldn't keep. An OPML or `.mm` file is kept in the map's history as it came in, and read the same way when it's shown or restored. OPML export is Orbit's `exportOPML`. PDFs and prints are the map's web page laid out for paper (Orbit's `renderPrintLayout`, in `web/print.ts`) in a web view of their own that no one sees, which Android's print framework turns into pages (`page/MapPrinter.kt`).
- **Private maps are locked in Orbit, not encrypted** (`privacy/`). Being private is `private` in the map's `meta.json`; the map's text never says so, so files, folders and other devices see a map like any other. Details that can't be read count as private (see the rules for `meta.json`): such a map is locked like any other, everywhere maps are listed, backed up or imported, and its owner unlocks it and can make it not private.
  - **Covers go where `private` goes.** The emoji and name a private map shows instead of its own while it's locked are `cover` in the same `meta.json` (`LockedCover`, which keeps what a newer version adds to it): backups and Export all carry it with `private`, and the folder, sending to a device and shared zips leave both out. A copy that stays private keeps it, saying it's a copy as its title does (`coverOfCopy`). Without a cover, a locked map still looks its own: `lockedLook` makes its color and planets from its id, never from what it holds, and `coverTitle` names it wherever "Private map" stood for its title.
  - **The prompt is Android's own,** the platform's `BiometricPrompt`, which works in any activity on Android 10 and later with no library: a fingerprint or face (`BIOMETRIC_WEAK`) or the device credential, and `setDeviceCredentialAllowed` on Android 10. AndroidX Biometric would need a `FragmentActivity` and bring AppCompat into the libre build. Orbit never sees a fingerprint, face or PIN, only whether it was the phone's owner.
  - **When they lock** is `PrivateLock`, plain state that changes at once on the main thread, so a screen coming back already shows maps locked: `ProcessLifecycleOwner` says when Orbit leaves and comes back, a receiver hears the screen turn off, and the setting says how long Orbit may be away. Orbit starts locked. While the phone asks, it isn't leaving: on Android 10 its PIN screen is an activity of its own. Without a screen lock (`KeyguardManager.isDeviceSecure`) nothing locks, since nothing could unlock it.
  - **What a lock hides:** Home's state leaves locked maps out of searches and tags (`homeState`), and their cards show only the lock; with `hideLockedPrivateMaps` it leaves them out of Home altogether, and Home's bar offers to show them (`PrivateMapsButton`) whether any are there or not, so the offer gives nothing away. Every other place that lists, names or counts maps asks `PrivateView.conceals` too, and offers the same way back (`ShowPrivateMapsButton`). A map's screen is behind `PrivateMapGate`: opened locked, its view model and page aren't made until it's unlocked, and until the maps' summaries are read (a cold start from a shortcut or widget) whether it's private isn't known, so it counts as locked (`gate`), as it does in `MapViewModel.locked`; having been on screen counts only in this run of Orbit (`PrivateMaps.run`), so a screen Android brings back after closing Orbit starts behind the lock; locked while open, a window of its own covers the map's sheets, menus and pictures, which are windows too, and an opaque cover under it keeps the first frame back from showing the map before that window draws. That holds only on the screen the map was open on (`openHere`): a screen made again while it's locked, as for a new language or font size, would open the map's sheets again above that window, so it shows the lock alone until it's unlocked. Nothing outside a map names a locked one: the folder's notes keep which of their entries are about private maps (`FolderNotes.private`, read as none from older notes), so Settings counts those (`noteEntries`); the arrival dialog leaves out the name of the private map it would update; screens whose messages or dialogs can name one close them as private maps lock (`WhenPrivateMapsLock`); a way out that makes a file of a map and opens another app or a system screen with it (the share sheet, the save screen, the print dialog) looks at the map again right before it opens, as its details and the lock are then, not as they were when it began (`shareMapExport`, `PrivateMaps.hidesNow`): one that locked while its file was made opens nothing, and the file is deleted; and a computer the map is open on gets none of a page or zip made for it as the map locked, and hears the share ended; what takes every map out as it is, Export all and choosing the folder maps are kept in, asks for the screen lock first while a private map is locked, and always while they hide (`rememberUnlockedForEveryMap`, `asksBeforeEveryMap`); and crash reports never name any map. Keyboards are asked not to learn from what's typed into a private map: its fields through `KeyboardsDontLearn`, and the map's page through `OrbitPage.incognito`, which adds `IME_FLAG_NO_PERSONALIZED_LEARNING` to the page's input connection. Under the cover, the map's semantics are cleared and its page is invisible (`MapViewModel.locked`), so screen readers, the keyboard and drags can't reach it; the map registers no shortcuts there, and its view model opens no panel and presents, studies, undoes or redoes nothing while it's locked; and `ArrivalDialog` asks for the screen lock before Update writes into a locked private map. A map the sync adds is private from the start when it's a private map's (`MapRepository.add`'s `isPrivate`, written before the map appears): the folder's version kept beside one, a sync app's conflict copy of its file (`conflictOriginal`), or its file back after it was deleted here (the deletion's record keeps `private`). The folder's sync writes into a locked private map without asking, on purpose: it's the owner's own sync, running in the background, and asking would stall it until they unlocked; the map stays private and locked, and the notes don't name it. A private map being presented stops as it locks (`PresentationLocking`): `stopPresentation` sends the audiences `End` at once, even with the screen off, so browsers hear it ended and a TV or second screen goes back to waiting; a TV hears it ahead of whatever was still on its way, such as a map whose pictures were being shrunk, which then never goes (`CastAudience`), and stays connected for the next presentation. They close as the map's screen next draws, and until then the room's server gives out none of the map's pictures or videos, which it serves only while a map is shown, one still being made ready as the map locked included. Unlocked, presenting again is offered. Quick capture and the widget's picker keep themselves out of Recents while they name unlocked private maps, as the main window does (`keepPrivateMapsOutOfRecents`).
  - **Recents:** screens that show something private say so while they do (`PrivateOnScreen`). Then Recents gets no picture: `setRecentsScreenshotEnabled(false)` on Android 13 and later, and `FLAG_SECURE` before, the only way there, which also stops screenshots and screen casting, so only while something private is on screen.
- **Widgets are Jetpack Glance,** with a layout for each of six sizes; the launcher shows the size closest to the widget's among those that fit. Their pictures are the home screen's thumbnails, ready before a widget draws so it never opens on a placeholder. Widgets draw again once changes to the maps settle, when the home screen turns light or dark, when the text size changes, and when the wallpaper's colors are turned on or off. Their colors are the app's (`widgetColors`, from `orbitColorScheme`), light and dark for the launcher to pick; before Android 12, which leaves a widget's rounding and progress bar to the launcher, the frame is a tinted rounded shape and the bar a picture of it. Glance runs on WorkManager, whose own permissions join Orbit's three in the built app: `RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED` to schedule the widgets' updates again after a restart, `WAKE_LOCK` so an update isn't cut short by the phone sleeping, and `FOREGROUND_SERVICE` for urgent work on Android 11 and earlier (expedited work runs as a foreground service there). They stay: without them, widgets would stop updating after a restart, or on older phones.
- **Maps between devices are sealed** (`nearby/OfferEncryption.kt`), with standard algorithms from the JDK and Android:
  - A device taking maps makes a P-256 key pair each launch, and gives the public key in its advertisement (`key`, with the `version` of offers it takes) and at `GET /key`.
  - The sender makes a fresh pair, agrees a secret with ECDH, and derives its keys with HKDF-SHA256, salted with both public keys. What's sealed travels in AES-256-GCM chunks of 64 KiB, each with its own nonce, authenticating how many chunks there are and where each goes. Only the sender's public key and the version travel in the clear.
  - An offer starts with a hello (`POST /offer`): the sender's name and its commitment to its half of the check code, sealed. The receiver answers with its own half, the sender reveals its half (`POST /offer/<id>`), and both show the code, which comes from the agreed keys and both halves: no one in between can pick keys until the codes match.
  - The map and its pictures go (`POST /offer/<id>/map`), sealed with a key of their own, only once the person sending says the other device shows the same code. So a device that answers in another's place on the Wi-Fi gets nothing of the map. An offer holds its place under the limits from its hello on, and the receiver refuses a map it isn't waiting for, or has no room for, before reading any of it.
  - Version 2 is the one spoken. Orbit 0.2.0 and earlier (version 1) sent the map first: neither side offers to the other, and each says which device to update.

  The receiver is the one part every version of the app shares. So it only ever adds to what it understands, and a new receiver is published before an app that needs it ships (RELEASE.md). Anyone on the network can join a TV's Cast session, so it takes nothing on trust: it joins a command's parts in whatever order they come, and drops a part that isn't one, a command of more than `MOST_PARTS` parts or `MOST_COMMAND_LENGTH` characters, and parts that don't make JSON.
- **Pictures, videos and sounds full screen** (`media/`) are a window of their own over the app, so they cover the list beside the map on a tablet, take the keys and Back, and keep light system bars on the dark. Their bars are see-through from the app's theme (`Theme.Orbit`), as a private map's lock window's are: Android 15 and later make every window's so, and on 10 to 14 the theme does it without the deprecated `Window` bar colors. Back's predictive swipe shrinks them as it goes.
  - **Pictures** are [Telephoto](https://github.com/saket/telephoto)'s zoomable images on the app's Coil: big ones are read a part at a time (subsampling), so they stay sharp zoomed in without filling memory, and its flick closes the viewer. The app builds Coil's image loader itself (`OrbitApplication.newImageLoader`): coil-gif's `AnimatedImageDecoder` plays animated GIFs and WebPs with Android's own `ImageDecoder`, at the size they're drawn, so a big GIF takes no more memory than the screen; Telephoto draws them whole, since an animation can't be read a part at a time. Before it, `StillAnimations` (`media/AnimatedPictures.kt`) decodes their first frame while Android's animations are off, unless a request asks to play them, as the viewer's play button does; that frame isn't kept in Coil's memory, so turning animations back on plays them at once. The map's page, which draws thumbnails too, gets an animation's first frame instead of its file (`page/FirstFrames.kt`, a PNG made once in the cache for each version of the file): a planet that moved would keep the page drawing, and the battery with it, for as long as it's on screen. The audience's screen, the room's browsers and web pages get the file itself. The gallery is the map's pictures, videos and sounds in the order the page reports its nodes, the outline's (`galleryOf`).
  - **Video files** play in Media3's ExoPlayer, with Media3's Compose surface and Material controls. The player is made when a video opens and released when it closes, so maps carry nothing of it. Release builds turn Media3's own logging off as Orbit starts (`OrbitApplication.onCreate`), whichever of its players plays first: its errors can name a map's folder or a video's site. The view that prints a map keeps the page's console out of the log too, as the map's page does (`QuietChrome`).
  - **Sounds** play in one ExoPlayer for the open map (`media/SoundPlayback.kt`), made when a sound first plays and released with the map's screen, so the small player over the map, the details, the editor, the viewer and the presentation all show the one sound playing, and it goes on as people fly around. Starting another sound stops the one before first, so one that can't play never leaves the last one playing under its "can't be played". The player is behind `SoundPlayer`, which the tests play with a stand-in. It holds the phone's audio focus, and pauses when another app takes it, when headphones come out, and when Orbit leaves the screen.
- **Voice memos** are `MediaRecorder`'s (`media/VoiceRecorder.kt`): AAC, one channel, 48 kHz at 96 kbps, into a take in the cache that joins the map once it's saved (`MapRepository.addRecording`). Its limit, the room the map has left, is kept by the recorder's own clock: `setMaxFileSize` would reserve room for an index as big as the limit allows, 300 KB of nothing in every memo. The microphone is asked for only as a memo starts, and held only while it records. A sound from the phone is known by its first bytes before its type (`soundKind`), since Android's own types call an `.m4a` "audio/mpeg".
  - **YouTube and Vimeo** play in their own embedded players, in a web view apart from the map's: no channel to the app, no files or content, JavaScript only because the players need it, no third-party cookies, and nothing loads in it but the players' frames (a content policy says so too); other pages open in a tab. YouTube's player refuses to play ("Error 153") for a page that doesn't say where it is, so the player's page is loaded with `loadDataWithBaseURL` as the app's id, `https://com.hyuchia.orbit`, as YouTube asks of apps, and its frame's requests carry that Referer. Vimeo plays its videos protected, so its player may use the phone's protected-content system, and nothing else a browser offers.
- **Pictures and videos to the audience** go as commands (`AudienceCommand.Media`, `Playback`, `Seek` and `CloseMedia` in `page/Protocol.kt`), which the audience's page turns into Orbit's media viewer (`openMedia` with `startAt`, `playMedia`, `pauseMedia`, `seekMedia`, `unmuteMedia`). The phone reckons where a video is from what it sends (`present/ShownMedia.kt`); a second screen's and a TV's word on their video (`mediaState`) corrects it, and a browser that joins halfway opens the video where the phone reckons it is.
  - **Sounds stay on the phone:** they play from the presenter's phone, so they never go to the audience's screens.
  - **Videos seek** because both servers answer for part of a file. The phone's web server answers a `Range` request with 206 and `Content-Range`, reading only that part (`nearby/ByteRanges.kt`). In the WebView, maps' files answer the same way (`page/MapFiles.kt`), holding the whole file: WebView skips to the part a request asks for by itself, in any stream an app answers with.
- **App and page talk in JSON.**
  - The page says hello on a `WebMessageListener` channel limited to its own origin and its main frame, and talks to the app on it (`MapMessage` in `page/Protocol.kt`).
  - The app sends its commands back on the same channel, to the frame that said hello (`MapCommand`): the page reads them as JSON, so nothing the app sends, text from other apps included, is ever run as code.
  - Jobs such as pictures and conversions carry a number that their answer returns. A job is let go once it's answered, given up on after a minute, or no longer wanted by whoever asked.
  - Commands sent before the page says hello wait for it, only as long as they matter (`WaitingCommands`): a page that didn't load takes none, and a map loaded or unloaded makes the commands about the one before it moot.
  - After every change the page sends the whole map: each node with its attributes, its custom ones included, and the color it's drawn with (Orbit's `getNodeColor`).
  - A map's numbers reach the app as numbers its types hold. Orbit takes any number for how many levels a map shows, an idea's priority and the version of a kind of idea (`maxDepth: 1.5`, `priority: 1e12`, `goal@99999999999`), and a message with one the app's `Int` can't hold would be lost whole: the map never ready, or a save with its edit. So the page sends each as the whole number nearest what it means, within an `Int`'s range (`web/whole-numbers.ts`: 1.5 levels show what 2 do, and a version past the range is still newer than any the app knows), and the app reads any number there (`MapWholeNumberSerializer`). The map's text keeps them as they were written.
  - Both sides are held to one set of examples: `web/protocol-examples.ts` has one of each command and message, held to the pages' own types with a key for every kind, and the pages' build writes them into `app/src/test/resources/page-protocol.json`. `ProtocolTest` checks that the app writes each command exactly so, reads each message with all it says, and knows the same kinds. A message the app still can't read is an error in the log of every build, by its kind alone.
  - The app changes a goal's numbers by merging them into the idea's custom attributes, and steps it with `addToGoal`, which adds to the number the page has, so quick steps all count.
  - An earlier version from the history is drawn with `preview`, and the page takes no change and saves nothing until the map as it is now comes back (`web/changing.ts`): an editable `preview` of it, a `restore` or a `load`. The app brings it back itself before any change it sends (`showNow`), and closes the history before what takes the map over (`closeHistory`), so a change always lands on the map as it is now. While a version is on show, the app keeps the map's state as it is now, and takes only whether it can be changed from what the page says.
  - A picture's, video's, sound's or page's caption, description or live photo's motion goes to the page alone (`mediaChange`, `motionChange`), and the page merges it into the node's own value (`web/media.ts`): every other field stays as it is, those Orbit reads and those it keeps for a newer version or another app, which it carries through edits of the same file. Only another file is a whole new value.
- **Study mode never writes the map** (`study/`). A session's cards are the ideas under where it began with ideas or a note under them (`cardsUnder`). `StudyControl` asks the page to hide the ideas under every one of them, with Orbit's `hideChildren`, so revealing one card never gives away another's, then flies to each card and reveals it with `revealChildren`: that's only how the page draws the map, so nothing is edited, saved or added to the history, and nothing reaches the folder maps are kept in or other devices. Each answer is kept at once in the map's `meta.json` (`MapMetadata.study`): a card's Leitner box, the day it's due and the day it was first studied, by idea id, following an idea's new id as the path does (`renameIdea`), and dropped for ideas the map no longer has as a session begins, once the map was read whole (`readOnly` is null: a map Orbit couldn't read all of, or a newer Orbit made, may show only some of its ideas, or give them other ids, and the page reports a map in the trash, or kept by a newer Orbit, in place of either). When new text gives an idea a new id, its card goes on under it as its latest answer on any device left it, kept as this device's own, since the other devices see only the new text, and find the card in this device's record in the folder. The page says an idea's new id in its `saved` (`renamed`), for text the app's editor sent (`updating`) and for text typed on the map, as the field for typing there closes on the idea (`web/renaming.ts`). Days are counted in the phone's time zone (`LocalDate.toEpochDay`). Answers are written one at a time, the latest only, so an older one never lands last. A page that loads the map again (its process stopped, say) gets the hidden ideas and the card back (`pageLoaded`). Behind a private map's lock (`LocalMapLocked`, from `PrivateMapGate`), a session holds still: it takes no keys or focus, keeps the screen on no longer and says nothing to TalkBack, and it's just where it was once unlocked. A studied map takes no drops (`takesDrops`), and its ideas don't drag out.
- **Orbit's own features, used as they're meant to be:**
  - `editable` with `longPressDelay` set to Android's long-press timeout;
  - `focus` to reopen a map where you left it;
  - `hideChildren` and `revealChildren` for study mode's cards;
  - node types with `render` hooks for people, progress rings and goals. Orbit draws again only the nodes an edit touched, so the app draws its rings again in place after each edit, for the Progress idea above a goal, moving to the new share;
  - `nodeTypeFor`, which draws ideas with to-dos or goals under them as Progress ideas without writing the kind into the map, on the map, the audience's screens and web pages alike;
  - `showActionButtons: false`, with the idea's buttons in the native toolbar instead, and `onAction` taking over the detail sheet for the native one;
  - `insets`, so the map fits around the toolbar, the rail, sheets and captions;
  - `baseUrl` serving each map's pictures from its folder;
  - themes and palettes from the frontmatter, and `serializeOrbitMarkdown` and `serializeOrbitYAML` for files.
- **Keys go where they belong:**
  - The map's page gets the keys first, and the app's shortcuts take what it leaves (`WebViewClient.onUnhandledKeyEvent`). So typing in an idea keeps Ctrl+Z for its text.
  - Shortcuts no text field uses, such as Ctrl+F, the views and Ctrl+/, are caught before anything else.
  - The page keeps its own focus when the view takes the keyboard's (`setNeedInitialFocus(false)`). The map takes the keyboard's focus back whenever nothing covers it, even after the layout moves it.
- **Native feel, on purpose:**
  - **The page is transparent:** Compose paints behind it, so there's never a white flash.
  - **No web tells:** no text selection, callouts or tap highlights.
  - **Haptics:** Orbit's spin haptics play native clock ticks, and long presses and new ideas get their own.
  - **Back:** predictive back on every screen.
  - **One of several choices** is Material 3 Expressive's connected buttons when there are a few (`ui/Choices`, `ConnectedChoice`), which become a list of radio buttons when their words don't fit on one line, and chips when there are many (`ChoiceChips`). TalkBack hears both as radio buttons in a group. Material's filter chips are checkboxes to it, so `ChoiceChips` sets the radio role outside the chip's own, which Compose applies last.
  - **The keyboard:** it comes up when a menu opens the field for typing, and the map makes room for it at once.
  - **Times and dates** follow the phone's 12- or 24-hour clock and its language (`ui/TimeWords`): Android's pattern for the fields wanted (`DateFormat.getBestDateTimePattern`), since java.time's localized styles ignore the clock setting. A pattern ICU had to pad (`├…┤`), or one without the fields asked for, gives way to a plain one, and a calendar or hour cycle in the language's tag changes nothing.
