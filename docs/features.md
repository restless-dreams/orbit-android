# What Orbit for Android does

Everything the app does, area by area. The website shows the highlights, with pictures: https://orbit.hyuchia.com/android.


## Your maps

- **Each map as Orbit draws it.** Home shows every map as a picture of how it opens, in its own style or the app's colors. Orbit draws the pictures off screen, and again as a map changes while it shows, as when pictures, videos or sounds its text points at arrive after it, from the folder maps are kept in or a zip. A map with to-dos or goals says how far along they are ("2 of 7 done", with a bar), counted as its outline counts them.
- **Finding maps:**
  - search titles, ideas, notes and tags, with how many maps match above them, which screen readers hear as it changes. A YAML map is searched for its ideas and notes, never for the words its structure uses, such as `children`, `text` or `status`, which no map would match on;
  - pin maps to the top;
  - sort by last edited, last opened, newest or title. The order sorts the pinned maps too, so it's beside them when every map shown is pinned.
- **Tags** group maps however you think of them, and a map can have several. They're kept in each map's file, as `tags:` in its frontmatter, where notes apps like Obsidian read them too, so they go wherever the map goes; they're read as Orbit reads YAML, so a comment after them isn't a tag, and a quoted tag keeps its commas, quotes and escapes. Home shows the tags in use as chips under the search: tapping one shows only its maps, and a map made meanwhile takes that tag. Each card shows its map's tags. Edit tags renames a tag, or takes it out of every map; one it couldn't change (Orbit can only show it, the storage couldn't take the change, or it kept changing meanwhile) keeps the tag, and the sheet says so right there, naming the map when it's one (never a private one), and why. Home keeps showing the old tag's maps while some still have it, rather than following the tag to its new name. A map's tags are picked or added from its card's menu, or from its own menu while it's open, with Undo there.
- **Card menus:** present or study a map, pin, rename, tag, duplicate, share or delete it, and its Private switch. A deleted map comes back with Undo, and from the trash for 30 days.
  - **Renamed, tagged and copied as Orbit reads the map:** these go through Orbit's own reading of the map, as its screen would change it, so a map in markdown keeps every line but the ones that change, and one in YAML keeps every idea and what it holds (it's written as markdown from then on, as its screen writes it). Renaming changes the map's own title when it has one, and its center's name when that's the map's name. A version is kept before a rename and before new tags. A map Orbit can only show, made by a newer Orbit or one it can't read all of, is left as it is, and Home says why; a tag renamed in every map stays on those.
- **What's due:** a map studied in study mode says on its card how many of its cards are due ("5 to study"), counting, as a session does, only the ideas that were still cards when the map was last open on this device; it moves on at midnight, and as the phone's date, time or time zone changes. A locked private map's card doesn't say.
- **Private maps:** the Private switch in a map's menu, on its card or while it's open, keeps it for its owner, and turns that off again. Screen readers hear a switch, with its state. It opens with the phone's own fingerprint, face or screen lock, through Android's prompt: Orbit never sees any of them. One unlock opens every private map, until Orbit locks them again.
  - **Locked,** a private map's card keeps its shape and place, with an empty orbit and a lock where its picture was, and "Private map" for its name: no title, tags or progress. Each one's orbit has a color and planets of its own, made from its id and never from what it holds, so locked maps tell apart at a glance. Home's search doesn't find it, its tags aren't among Home's, and a picked tag leaves it out. A tap asks for the screen lock, and every private card gives way to its map's, the lock opening as the map's picture grows in. Unlocked, a private map's card has a small lock beside its pin, and Home's bar a lock that locks them all at once.
  - **A cover of its owner's:** "Cover while locked", beside the Private switch, gives a private map an emoji and a short name, such as "📓 Journal", shown only while it's locked, in place of "Private map": on its card (where screen readers hear "Private map, Journal, locked"), its lock, its widget and pinned shortcut, the trash, the folder's notes, and when another version of it arrives. Nothing of the map itself shows. It goes where being private goes, into backups and Export all, and a copy that stays private keeps it, with "(copy)" as its title has.
  - **Hidden instead,** as Settings can choose: locked, private maps aren't on Home at all, nor in its searches, tags or count. The open lock in Home's bar unlocks them and shows them again; it's there whether any private map is or not, so it gives nothing away, and never says how many. They fade away as they lock on screen and back as they're unlocked, and a Home where every map is private looks as a new one does. Nothing else gives them away either: quick capture, the share sheet's list and the widget's list leave them out and offer the same open lock, there whether any private map is or not; the trash leaves them out, and emptying it empties only what it shows; Settings doesn't count them in the trash, the folder's notes, or what exporting and importing say; and another version of one arriving from another device or in a file is asked about as a new map, whose Open asks for the screen lock first. Off by default: locked cards keep their place.
  - **Opened while locked** (from a shortcut, a widget, or beside the list on a tablet), a private map shows its lock first and asks at once, and nothing of it loads until it's unlocked; at a cold start too, before Orbit has read which maps are private, and after Android closed Orbit and brings the map's screen back. A map that locks while it's open is covered whole, its sheets and menus included, and is just as it was once unlocked; a sound of it that was playing stops, and its small player goes. The share sheet Orbit opened goes as well, while nothing was chosen in it, and so does a print dialog that hasn't printed: nothing of a private map stays offered to other apps or a printer over the lock. A share, save or print still being made as the map locks, its zip with videos say, opens nothing once it's ready, and the file made for it is deleted; so does a picture, video or sound shared from it full screen, and a computer it's open on, or the browsers it's presented in, get none of what was being made for them. Under its lock, screen readers and the keyboard can't reach it: none of the map's shortcuts work, nor does presenting, studying or undoing. The lock fits any window: in half a phone, in split screen, it stands alone above smaller words. What's typed into a private map, in its planets and its fields, asks keyboards not to learn from it, so its words don't come back as suggestions elsewhere; so do its title, tags and cover typed on Home, and ideas quick capture adds to it.
  - **Locking again:** when Orbit has been off screen for longer than Settings says (right away, after 1 minute, which is the default, 5 or 15 minutes, counted on a clock that setting the phone's time can't move), when the screen turns off, and from Home's lock or Settings, or Lock now: in a private map's menu and its card's on Home, the lock in the map's top bar on tablets, and Ctrl+L. Locking now is as Orbit being away: what's being typed on the map is saved first, the map goes behind its lock at once, and everything that closes as private maps lock closes. A new start of Orbit starts locked. A private map being presented stops as it locks: browsers watching hear it ended, and a TV or second screen goes back to waiting, a TV at once, even while a map was still on its way to it, which then never shows, while the phone shows the lock; unlocked, Orbit offers to present it again rather than going on by itself.
  - **Everywhere else:** a private map's widget shows only a lock and "Private map", and opens to its lock, and so does the preview in the launcher's list of widgets (Android 15 and later), drawn again as a map is made private; a widget already showing a map shows its lock the moment it's made private; the widget's list and quick capture leave it nameless or out until unlocked, and quick capture can unlock from its list of maps. When the map quick capture was adding to, or added to last, is a private one that's locked, no map is chosen until one is picked or it's unlocked: ideas never quietly go to another map. It leaves the launcher's shortcuts, and one pinned to the home screen is renamed "Private map". Nothing outside the map says its name while it's locked: the trash shows "Private map" and when it was deleted, not how many ideas it has; sorted by title, Home puts locked cards after the others, by when they were made; another app that asks Orbit to open a map by its id gets a hidden private map only from Orbit's own shortcuts and widgets, which carry a mark of this install; the folder's notes in Settings count it as "a private map" (and its pictures and videos too big for it likewise), and a version of it arriving from another device or in a file is "another version of a private map", whose Update asks for the screen lock first (cancelled, Keep both is still there). Locking them takes away a message, or a rename, tags or send sheet on Home, that named one; and a message that comes once something's done doesn't name a private map that locked meanwhile: Undo for a map deleted from Home names it by its cover, if it has one, or as "a private map", and says nothing while they hide; named so, or about a map that isn't private, it names none of them, and stays as they lock, Undo and all; quick capture says its ideas were added, to no map named; and that a map is private now goes unsaid, as its card shows its lock. While something private is on screen, quick capture's and the widget's lists naming an unlocked private map included, as are Settings' folder notes and a dialog about another version of one, Recents gets no picture of Orbit: on Android 13 and later only that, and on Android 10 to 12 Orbit's window is kept from screenshots and screen casting too, which is the only way there, pictures and videos full screen included. Printing a private map shows it in Android's own print screen, which Orbit can't keep out of Recents: Recents may picture it there until the map locks.
  - **Honest about it:** private maps are locked in Orbit, not encrypted. Their files stay plain markdown, so a sync app, the folder they're kept in or a backup reads them as any other map, and Settings says so. Being private goes with a map's details, into backups and Export all, but not with the map itself: sent to another device, shared or synced through a folder, it arrives there as any other map. Details Orbit can't read, damaged ones say, count as private: such a map shows locked like any other private map, here, in backups and coming back from Export all, and its owner unlocks it and can make it not private. Export all and choosing a folder to keep maps in take every map out as it is, so while a private map is locked they ask for the screen lock first; while private maps hide, they ask whether any is there or not. A copy of a private map is private too, from the moment it's there: a duplicate, the folder's version kept beside it when both changed, a version from another device kept as well, a sync app's conflict copy of its file (Syncthing's `.sync-conflict-…`, or a "conflicted copy" from Dropbox or Nextcloud), and its file back in the folder after the map was deleted here. The folder maps are kept in updates a locked private map as any other, without asking for the screen lock first, on purpose: it's the owner's own sync, kept in step in the background, and asking would stall it until they unlocked. A version sent from another device or opened from a file does ask, since it comes from outside.
  - **Without a screen lock,** there's nothing to open private maps with: making a map private asks to set one first, in the phone's settings, and the map becomes private once there is one. Private maps on a phone whose screen lock was removed show unlocked, and Settings says how to lock them again.
- **Starting a map:**
  - a blank map;
  - one of eleven templates (project plan, study notes, talk outline, decision, SWOT, goals and more), each previewed live in its own style;
  - a markdown, YAML or zip file, from Open a file, or another app's Open with or Share, whatever type that app gives it (markdown is kept just as it's written, its lists too);
  - a map from another app, with its notes, links, pictures and folded branches: OPML, from outliners and most mind map apps, FreeMind and Freeplane (`.mm`), and XMind (`.xmind`, from XMind 8 on), where each sheet becomes a map of its own. They come from Open a file, Import maps in Settings, or another app's Open with or Share, and a note says what Orbit kept another way (XMind's floating topics, summaries, callouts and boundaries, extra links) or left out (icons past an idea's first, pictures the file doesn't hold, links to ideas it doesn't have);
  - text shared from any app, or opened as a plain text file (`.txt`): its first line goes in the middle, and the other lines around it, nested as they're indented, as pasting nests them (bullets and numbers go, to-dos stay to-dos, tabs count as four spaces). One line with everything else indented under it is the middle. With several lines at the top, the first one is, and the others keep their nesting around it, so a plain list makes the map it always has. A title shared with the text, such as an article's, names the map instead, and every line goes around it;
  - map files and text dragged onto the list of maps from another app beside Orbit, which come in as they would from Open a file or a share, several files at once too: those that are other versions of maps here are asked about one after another, as one file is ("2 of 3" above each question), then what the rest brought is said. A text file comes whole, up to the 8 MB a map's text may be, and a longer one is refused, saying so. A zone over the list says what letting go makes, quiet while the drag is elsewhere and lit up once it's over the list.
- **First launch:** one map, the welcome map, shows Orbit off: a planet for each thing it does, to try right there, with a picture, a video and a sound of its own, to-dos and a goal, a tiny deck to study, and a path ready to present as a quick tour. About in Settings opens it again, or brings it back. An update leaves the maps an earlier version started with, the samples among them, where they are.

## On the map

- **Getting around:**
  - tap a planet to fly into it;
  - spin the orbit, or pinch to zoom in and out;
  - switch to the overview.
- **Going back:** back and the swipe gesture go up a level, and the map shrinks under your finger. Breadcrumbs lead back up too.
- **Picking up where you left off:** a map reopens on the idea and view you left it in.
- **Names that fit:** a name too long for its planet wraps inside it, and a long word breaks with a hyphen where the app's language, English, Spanish or Portuguese, breaks it ("Pho-to-syn-the-sis"), rather than shrinking the name until it's hard to read: once the map is still, a word breaks once if that shows its name at least 2 px larger. A name that still doesn't fit a small planet shows on a pill by it, with its icon.
- **Adding ideas:**
  - tap the **+** in the orbit (or the floating +) and type right inside the new planet;
  - Enter adds the idea and opens the next one, so ideas keep coming.
- **Talking ideas onto the map:**
  - hold the floating **+** and talk: each pause, or each sentence where the phone's speech service punctuates, becomes a planet in the orbit in focus as you speak;
  - let go to finish, or slide up while holding to keep listening hands-free until Done; TalkBack's long press does the same. Leaving the app, starting a presentation or a study session, or recording a voice memo ends the talk and lets the microphone go. Locking a private map ends it at once, and nothing said after that reaches the map, even what a slow speech service says late;
  - once the talk's last ideas are saved, one Undo takes the whole talk back, and only the talk;
  - it listens through whatever speech service the phone has, not only Google's, in Orbit's language, which Settings can make other than the phone's. Where there's only another app's listening screen, that screen hears one go. Where there's no speech service at all, as on many phones without Google, the new idea's field opens for typing or the keyboard's microphone.
- **The long-press menu,** where you pressed, once you let go:
  - for a goal, its numbers first: −1 and +1 for a count, with the menu staying open for the next step, or an amount to add, take off or pay in a small dialog;
  - add ideas under or next to an idea, or rename it on the map;
  - give it a date, and a time if you like, in Material's date and time pickers, or change or take them off (see "Dates and reminders", below);
  - set its status, color or icon. Icons come from Android's emoji picker, with a search above it that finds emoji by name or keyword, in the app's language and in English, the emoji people use most first. A long press on a match offers its skin tones;
  - fold its ideas away, or move it earlier, later, in or out;
  - duplicate it, or delete it with Undo;
  - copy it with everything under it, as text other apps paste as an outline, or paste into it, with one Undo: text becomes ideas under it (a line each, nested as the lines are indented; a markdown list, or an idea copied in Orbit, keeps its shape, to-dos, colors and icons), an address on a line of its own an idea that opens it, and a picture its picture. An idea pasted into another map brings its pictures, videos and sounds, unless that map is private and locked: then it comes as its text alone. When they can't come, past the map's room or with no room left on the device, nothing is pasted, and a message says why. A picture the other map already has another of by the same name (two maps from zips can each have a photo.jpg) comes beside it under a name of its own: the pasted idea shows its own, and the map's ideas keep theirs. What's copied from a private map is marked sensitive, so Android doesn't show it as it's copied and keyboards keep it out of their clipboard history. Orbit only reads the clipboard when you paste, so Android's notice that it did comes with what you asked for, and an idea dragged within Orbit carries a mark of the run it was dragged in, so another app's drag can't pass for one.
- **Dragging an idea somewhere else:** a long press lifts it, and moving on drags it, its moons with it.
  - Onto another idea: it lights up, and the idea goes inside it, shrinking as it's about to. Moved under another idea, a message says where, with Undo.
  - Along the orbit: its place is held where it was, and the other planets make room as it passes them. Over a planet's rim it's still the orbit; near the middle, the planet takes it.
  - A light tick on each idea it could land in, and a tap when it lands. Undo takes it back like any change.
  - Out of Orbit's window, into another app beside it, it goes on as a drag that app can take: see below.
- **Drag and drop with other apps,** on a tablet, a Chromebook or a phone in split screen:
  - **Onto the map:** text, web addresses, pictures, videos, sounds and map files. The idea under the finger lights up as when an idea is moved, with a tick for each, and a pill over the map says what letting go does ("Add to Flights"), moving out of the finger's way. Over empty space it's the idea in focus.
  - **What a drop adds,** with one Undo: text becomes ideas under the idea, as pasting makes them, and an address on a line of its own an idea that opens it; one picture, video or sound becomes the idea's, a sound with its caption from its file's name as when it's added from the phone; several become ideas of their own under it, named by their files; a PDF, a spreadsheet or any file Orbit doesn't show joins the idea's files, with every file let go with it; map files open as maps, as Open a file opens them, whatever else comes with them, and the pill says so before they're let go. A text file comes whole, up to 512 KB, a list rather than a book: a longer one adds nothing, and the message says to drop it on your maps to make a map of it. A video's or a sound's copy says how far along it is, with a way to stop it, and the usual limits hold, with an M4A kept as the MP4 it is. Home takes no sounds: they aren't maps. Ideas dropped deeper than a planet, where they wouldn't show, take the map there.
  - **When the other app fails:** a file it no longer lets Orbit read, can't lend, no longer has or fails to give says why ("Orbit isn't allowed to read that file"), and never closes Orbit: of several pictures, videos, sounds or map files, the rest still come in, and text comes in whole or not at all.
  - **Out of Orbit:** an idea lifted on the map and dragged out of the window, or a row of the outline held and moved, goes to other apps as its outline, the text copying it gives, under a planet in its color with "+N" for the ideas that go with it. Let go on its own map instead, into another idea, it moves there, with Undo; the outline's sheet steps aside for it. A private map's ideas leave it only while it's unlocked, and one on its way out from the map goes back if private maps lock.
  - **What takes nothing:** a map that can only be looked at, a private map behind its lock, a presentation, a path being made and an earlier version on show. A drop still being read or copied in as one of those begins adds nothing, and says so. Nor does pasting, from the menu or with Ctrl+V, and Ctrl+X only copies. While an idea is typed into, its field takes text as any field does.
- **The toolbar** floats over the map: the outline, search, the overview and styles, then the idea in focus's own buttons (its picture or video, its sound, what it holds, and its editor), and **+**. On a map that can only be looked at, the idea's own buttons stay, all but its editor's, in the toolbar and in the bar at the top of a short screen, and Ctrl+E and the keys that change the map are gone, from the map and from the list of shortcuts; nothing opens the editor or the style there. On a narrow phone, a map with pictures or videos keeps Style and layout in its menu, so the toolbar stays the same as you fly around.
- **The editor** (the pencil in the toolbar) covers everything an idea can be:
  - its date, with a time if you like, in Material's pickers, or none; under it, when Orbit will remind you, or why it won't;
  - its text, icon, color, status and priority. A color is one of Orbit's, one the map's ideas already have (most used first), or any other from the color picker: hue, saturation and brightness, or its hex code, with the idea shown in it. The long-press menu offers the same colors;
  - its kind: milestone, question, decision, risk, source, person, progress, which draws a ring that fills as the ideas under it get done, or goal;
  - a goal's numbers, at the top for a goal: saving up to a target in a currency (the phone's, or any other), paying something off, or counting things, like 5 apples. The numbers are typed in the phone's language, on the decimal keyboard, and what isn't a number is said under its field, a decimal point twice (1.2.3) or marks that group no digits (1,2.5) too, rather than read as another number. A number or word typed a moment before the goal changes how it counts is saved first, for the way it was typed in, and the goal turns around from it; one typed a moment before the idea becomes another kind is saved before the change, never after it;
  - tags, a markdown note with a preview, whose checkboxes check right there as part of the note being written, and a link to a web page or to another idea;
  - a photo or a video from the Photo Picker, a YouTube, Vimeo or video address, and a web page. A video from the phone is copied into the map as it is, with its progress showing and a way to stop it, and a frame from it becomes its poster, which its planet shows;
  - a sound: a voice memo recorded right there, a sound file from the phone, or a sound's web address, with a caption (see "Sounds on ideas", below).

  The sheet stops halfway, and the map glides into the room above it as the sheet comes up, so each change shows on the map as you make it.
- **Goals** track a number toward a target. A goal's planet shows a ring like a Progress idea's, with its number big in the middle and its target under it ("of 5", "of $3K"), or "left" when it counts down; a goal reached closes its ring and shows a check. Numbers are written in the phone's language, money in its currency. A Progress idea counts each goal under it by how far along it is, and a goal reached gives a light tap. A goal left without a target follows the goals and to-dos under it instead, with a ring like a Progress idea's.
- **Progress rings by themselves:** an idea with a to-do (to do, in progress, blocked or done; other status words, like "primary key", are labels), or a goal with a target, right under it shows a Progress ring without being made one, while it has no kind and no picture or video of its own, and its emoji moves onto its name. The ring is only drawn: the map's file never gets a kind for it. Each to-do and goal counts once, for every idea above it: an idea whose ring follows the ones under it isn't counted itself. A map can turn these rings off in Style and layout, which writes `progressRings: false` in its frontmatter; turning them on again takes the line out.
- **Details:** an idea's note, picture, video, sound, links and files open in a native sheet, from the toolbar or by tapping them on the map. Its kind, status, priority, date and tags come first, as words after their emoji, dot or icon: read, not tapped, so they aren't chips, and screen readers hear them as words ("Status: Done"); the sheet's Edit changes them. A status reads the same everywhere, without the spaces around it: in the details, the editor, the long-press menu, the outline and the rings. On a map that can only be looked at, the details offer no Edit, no step to a goal and no "Write one". A goal's details show how far along it is, its numbers in words ("$1,250 of $3,000 · 42% · $1,750 to go"), and −/+ or a way to add an amount. An idea whose ring follows the ideas under it says how far along they are ("2 of 4 done · 63%"). Notes render natively with links: web links open in a tab, and links to other ideas fly there.
  - **Tables** in a note are Material tables: a tonal header row, a line between rows, and each column aligned as the table's row of dashes says. A table wider than the details scrolls sideways inside them, and one narrower takes their width. TalkBack hears it as a table, its header cells as headings.
  - **Checklists** (`- [ ]` and `- [x]` in a note, at any depth) have Material checkboxes, a checked item dimmed and struck through. On a map that can change, a press anywhere on an item checks or unchecks it, and only that line of the map's file changes. The box shows its new state at once, and once the change is saved, a message says so with Undo, which takes it back while nothing has changed since. On a phone the message comes up over the details. TalkBack hears each item as one checkbox named by its words. A tap on its picture shows it full screen, one on its video plays it, and its sound plays right there, with a slider to move it.
- **Dates and reminders:** an idea can have a date, and a time if you like, kept small: no repeats, no snoozing.
  - **In the map's file,** as `due: "2026-11-20 09:30"`, where other apps read it too, so it goes wherever the map goes. It's local time, with no time zone: 9:30 is 9:30 wherever you are. Orbit 0.4.0 keeps a date when it changes a map.
  - **On the map:** a small chip inside the planet ("Today", or its time today, "Tomorrow", "Fri", "Nov 20"), in the app's language and on the phone's 12- or 24-hour clock. Once past, it turns red, unless the idea is done. The details show it among the idea's facts, after a calendar ("Overdue: Mon, Nov 16" once past, in red), and the outline after each idea; TalkBack says it with the idea's name, and whether it's overdue.
  - **Reminders:** when the date comes, at its time or at 9:00 for a date alone, a notification reminds you, on every device that has the map, like a calendar; a tap opens the map at the idea. It comes within minutes, without Orbit asking for exact alarms, and at the latest as the phone wakes from a doze. A reminder missed while the phone was off comes as it starts again, but never one for a date set, or arriving, already past. Reminders follow dates changed anywhere: on the map, in the folder maps are kept in, sent from another device, imported or restored from a version; and they stay through restarts, time and time zone changes and updates. Done ideas don't remind, and neither do maps in the trash.
  - **Private maps:** a private map's reminder says only "A reminder from a private map", wherever it shows, the lock screen included, and its tap asks for the screen lock before the map opens. Any other reminder names the idea and its map, and only "A reminder" on a lock screen that hides what notifications say.
  - **Settings** turns reminders off on this device. Android 13 and later ask for notifications once, as the first date is set, saying why; Settings offers it again while they're off.
- **Sounds on ideas:** an idea can hold a sound, and its planet wears a small sound wave for it.
  - **Voice memos:** "Record a voice memo", in the editor or the long-press menu, opens a recorder under the map: a big button that records, pauses and goes on, the waveform of what you say as you say it, and its time. Finish to listen back, then save it or record again; it joins the idea with Undo. Closing with a take asks first, and leaving Orbit or a call pauses it. Listening back pauses for a call or another app's sound, and when headphones come out. A take outlasts a change of language or font size, ready to listen to and save, and one that couldn't be saved (the map out of room, say) can still be played and saved again. The microphone is only asked for, and only used, while you record. A memo is AAC in an `.mp4`, about 0.7 MB a minute, and stops where the map runs out of room.
  - **From the phone or the web:** a sound file (MP3, M4A, AAC, Ogg, Opus, WAV, FLAC or WebM) is copied into the map as it is, with its progress showing and a way to stop it, and its name, unless a recorder made it up, becomes its caption. A sound's web address plays from there.
  - **Playing:** from the toolbar, the long-press menu, the details or the editor. A small player comes up over the map, with the idea's art or picture, its name, its time and a line that waves while it plays, and the map makes room for it; the sound goes on as you fly around, and a tap on the player opens it full screen. Playing another sound stops the one before, even when the new one can't play, which then says so. It pauses when you leave Orbit, when another app plays, or when headphones come out.
  - **Full screen:** among the map's pictures and videos, the sound shows the idea's picture, or art in its color with its icon, whose rings turn while it plays, with play and pause, ten seconds back and on, a slider, its time and the sound off and on, and share. Play stays in the middle, and on a narrow phone or a split screen the buttons close up, then line up in one row, so none covers another.
- **Sketching on a planet:** "Sketch" in an idea's long-press menu or its editor turns its planet into a canvas, right where it is.
  - **The moment:** the map flies until the planet fills most of the screen, the rest of the map and its bars dim, and the planet becomes round paper in its own color. Done brings the map back the way its flights move, the planet shrinking into its place with the sketch as its picture; the way out in the corner leaves nothing behind, asking first when something was drawn.
  - **Drawing:** with a finger or a stylus, the ink a frame or two ahead of the touch where it's going, so it keeps up. A stylus draws thinner or wider as it presses, and wider laid flat; a finger draws a little thinner the faster it goes. While a stylus is near the screen, and a moment after, a palm resting on it draws nothing, and a stroke a palm began just before the stylus came down goes.
  - **The tools,** in a floating toolbar under the planet, or beside it on a phone on its side: the pen, whose second tap offers the map's own colors, black and white, and three widths; the eraser, which takes the strokes it passes over (as does a stylus's other end, or its button held); undo and redo; clear; and Done. With a keyboard: P and E, Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z, Ctrl+Enter, and Escape.
  - **Described:** as Done is tapped, a field asks what the sketch shows, which screen readers say and web pages and prints carry, as for any picture. It comes up above the keyboard, the planet staying where it is behind it; on a phone on its side, where the keyboard leaves little room, its title, field and buttons go in one row. The sketch then joins the idea in one change, which one Undo takes back.
  - **Drawn on further:** an idea whose picture is a sketch offers "Keep sketching" instead, and the canvas reopens with its strokes, to draw on, erase or clear. A picture of another kind is replaced only after asking, and Undo brings it back.
  - **A plain drawing everywhere:** each sketch is an SVG of its own in the map's folder, a new one each time, so earlier versions keep the one they had: its round paper and the outline of each stroke, a few kilobytes. It shows wherever pictures do, on the map, in the details, full screen, on Home's cards and widgets, in zips, web pages, PDFs and prints, in what's sent to other devices and in the folder maps are kept in. It holds nothing but the drawing, so the cleaner every picture going out passes through takes nothing from it: what leaves is the same drawing, written again with an XML declaration (`<?xml …?>`) at its top. Another device reopens it to draw on, and earlier versions of Orbit show it as any picture.
- **Files on ideas:** an idea can hold files of any kind: a PDF, a spreadsheet, a ticket, a document. Its planet wears a small paperclip at its upper left, with how many from two on, readable on every style.
  - **Adding them:** Add files in the editor (Android's document picker, any kind, several at once), "Add a file" in the long-press menu, files let go on an idea (a drop with a PDF, a spreadsheet or another file Orbit doesn't show, and every file let go with it), and files shared to Orbit from another app with "Attach to an Orbit idea": they join the idea in focus on the map that's open, or a new map named after the first. Each is copied into the map as it is, its progress showing with a way to stop it, and they join their idea together, with one Undo. A file is up to 250 MB and counts in the map's 1 GB, refused as a video or a sound is, saying why.
  - **Seeing them:** the details list them after the note, each with its kind's icon, its name and its kind and size ("PDF · 48 kB"), in the app's language; TalkBack hears each as its name, kind and size, and that a tap opens it in another app. One that isn't on this device, as one sent from an older version arrives without it, is quieter and says "Not on this device". The editor lists them the same way, each with a button that takes it off, with Undo.
  - **Opening one:** a tap opens it in an app that can, with a copy, never the map's own file. A picture, video or sound held as a file goes clean, as from the viewer; any other kind first asks, "Open “Boarding pass.pdf” in another app?", since Orbit can't take out what a file like it may say beyond what it shows, such as who made it, when and where: Cancel is the choice it starts on. A copy from a private map is deleted when private maps lock, and the question goes with the lock too. A file no app on the device opens says so, and one that isn't on the device (as one an older device in the folder didn't keep) says that.
  - **Where they go:** with the map, in its zip, sent to a device, in Export all, backups and the folder maps are kept in. Every way out to other people names them in its line ("2 files stay on this device: Orbit can't take out what may be hidden in them") and asks once, with the pictures it can't clean, whether they go as they are. A web page lists them by name, kind and size without carrying them, and says they stayed with the map; PDFs and prints name them too. The browsers a map is presented in never get them.
  - **Earlier versions:** Orbit 0.4.0 and earlier keep an idea's files, and every field each file says, when they change its map, and keep the files themselves on the device. They carry only their own kinds of file in zips, sending, backups and the folder, so in a shared folder this device's record lists the kinds of the ideas' files there, and 0.4.0 says which open only on a newer device; Settings here says the same of an older device beside it. Sending a map to one says which files stay here.
- **Pictures full screen:** the toolbar's picture button shows the idea's picture over everything, on the dark, fitted to the screen.
  - Pinch, double-tap where you want a closer look, or use the wheel or + and -, then pan and fling; 0 fits it again. Big photos stay sharp at any zoom, read a part at a time.
  - **Animated GIFs and WebPs** play, looping, and zoom like any picture. They play only while on screen, and start over each time they come back; the bar at the top pauses and plays them, and so do space and K. With Android's animations off ("Remove animations", in its accessibility settings), they show their first frame with a play button. The idea's details play them too, or, with animations off, show their first frame with a play badge. On the map, a planet shows an animation's first frame, as the map's pictures on Home and in widgets do, so the map never keeps drawing, nor moves with animations off.
  - **Live photos** play their motion once, without sound, as their page comes up, and settle back on the still; held, they play again with their sound, and the "Live" mark at the top plays them that way too, as do space and Enter (screen readers hear it as a button). Leaving the app stops the motion, sound and all, and the still is back. Zoomed in, they stay still. With Android's animations off, they play only when asked. The idea's details show the mark on its picture; on the map, planets stay still.
  - Swipe, or use the arrow keys, through the map's pictures, videos and sounds in the outline's order, with "3 of 10" at the top. A video's page shows its poster and a sound's page its length, and both wait for their play button; a sound playing pauses as another page comes up.
  - A tap hides and shows the controls: close and share at the top (the file itself, or a picture's web address), and the caption and description at the bottom. The description is what screen readers say. A picture is shared without what its file says beyond the picture, such as where and when it was taken: only which way up it is stays.
  - Back closes it, shrinking as you swipe, and so do Escape and a flick up or down when the picture isn't zoomed, with the map showing through as it goes.
- **Live photos,** a still with the few seconds a phone recorded around it, come in whole, however they were taken, wherever a picture comes in (the editor's picture, pasting and drops), and are kept as two plain files: the picture, as any picture is kept, and its motion beside it as an MP4.
  - **Android phones** keep the motion inside the picture's file, each its own way, and Orbit reads it by what's in the file: Google's Motion Photos (JPEG, HEIC and AVIF, and older Pixels' MicroVideo), Samsung's and Huawei's and Honor's. The picture loses the video it carried, never anything of the picture.
  - **iPhones** keep two files, the still (HEIC or JPEG) and a QuickTime movie: dropped together, they're joined by the id they share, else by their name, else by being taken within a few seconds of each other, never when each has an id of its own, and a picture and a live photo's movie that nothing ties together are joined if you say so. A movie that keeps its sound uncompressed comes whole, however QuickTime describes it. The movie is kept as an MP4, its video as it was and its sound made AAC, so browsers play it too.
  - **Within the limits:** the picture and its motion count together, 250 MB at most and the map's 1 GB in all; a motion past them is left out, and the picture comes alone, with a note.
  - **Everywhere maps go:** the motion goes with its picture in zips, sending, Export all, backups and the folder maps are kept in, and in shares without where and when it was taken, as videos go. Wherever a way out says what goes with the map, a live photo is named as one, its motion part of it: "With 2 pictures and a live photo inside". Earlier releases show the picture as any other, and keep and carry its motion, except that 0.3.0 and earlier drop it when they change the picture's caption or description there.
- **HEIC and HEIF pictures** that arrive through the folder maps are kept in, or a zip, as iPhones and some Samsungs take them, show on the map as a JPEG made for the page, which Android's WebView doesn't draw them, and as that JPEG in web pages and prints, which browsers other than Safari don't draw either: the file stays as it is. Pictures from the phone are made JPEGs as they come in, as always.
- **Big videos made smaller:** a video over about 50 MB, chosen in the editor or let go on the map, comes with an offer: "Make this video smaller? It's 180 MB. Made smaller on this device, at 1080p, it comes to about 62 MB", with "Make it smaller" and "Keep it as it is". Several let go together are asked about once. Made smaller, it's H.264 and AAC in an MP4, at most 1080 pixels on its shorter side at a bitrate for its size and frames, the way up it was recorded, its sound and its frames as they were, an HDR video made SDR: the editor, or the drop's pill, says how far along it is, from how big to about how big, with Stop, and once it's in the editor says what it came to ("Made the video smaller: 61.4 MB instead of 180 MB"). Stopped, or failing, it leaves nothing. It's offered only where it comes out well under what it was, with an encoder in the phone's hardware, which every phone that records video has: a phone without one keeps the video as it is, and says so. A video too big for the map as it is (past 250 MB, or past the map's room) is offered only smaller, when that fits; one that wouldn't fit either way is refused, saying why. It's AndroidX Media3's Transformer, open source, in both builds.
- **Videos play in the app:** from the toolbar's play button, the details or the editor, or a video's page among the pictures.
  - Videos in the map and video files on the web play at once, fitted to the screen whichever way the phone turns, with play and pause, ten seconds back and on, the time, a slider and the sound. The screen stays on while they play, and they pause when you leave the app. The controls step aside while a video plays, as a presentation's do, and stay while TalkBack is on.
  - **Open in another app** hands a video in the map to the phone's video app as a copy without where and when it was recorded and on what, made as the button waits; the map's own file stays in Orbit. One Orbit can't clean, as a video cut short, opens only once the person chooses to, after it says what would go with it, and a copy there's no room for says so. A video on the web opens at its address.
  - YouTube and Vimeo videos play in their own players inside the app, YouTube's in its privacy-enhanced mode, with their own controls and fullscreen, and a button to watch them on their site or in their app.
  - Keys: space plays and pauses, the arrows go back and on, and Escape closes.
- **Outline:** the whole map as a list, with each idea's color, its date (red once overdue), progress for every branch, the share of each ring that follows the ideas under it, checkboxes to tick to-dos off (a status that's a label, like "primary key", reads with the tags instead; on a map that can only be looked at, they show whether each is done and take no tap, and a row held opens no editor), and each goal's numbers ("2/5", "$1.3K of $3K") with a small ring. It is also how a screen reader or keyboard gets around.
- **Search:** finds ideas by text, tags or notes, shows the path to each, and flies to the one you pick.
- **Style and layout:**
  - Orbit's styles, each previewed with your map: paper and night, flat, Material, liquid glass and glass (each light and dark), cosmos, blueprint, ink and high contrast;
  - "App colors", the default: Orbit's Material style grown from your wallpaper's color, so the sun and every branch take tones in harmony with it;
  - color sets, each with every color whole in its row on any phone, down to a 320 dp window and with larger text: the swatches come closer together, then get smaller;
  - whether the map opens on one idea or the whole map;
  - orbit rings, progress rings on ideas with to-dos or goals, a slow drift, and how many levels show.

  The screen around the map takes on its style.
- **Undo and redo** for every change, up to 100 back; a very big map goes back as far as about 8 MB of text holds, and always at least one. On a phone, Redo shows in the top bar once something has been undone, so the map's name has room. The message a big change leaves (an idea removed with the ideas under it, a version restored) has Undo too, and swipes away to either side.
- **Version history:** "Version history" in a map's menu lists the map as it was: a version as each visit begins, one for every ten minutes of editing and one before each big change (removing an idea with ideas under it, editing the text by hand, restoring, a version from another device, a file or the synced folder), the last day's (the newest 50, then one for every ten minutes), then one an hour for a week, one a day for three months, and one a week after that, the one kept before a big change over the others of its stretch, up to the newest 200. A busy day never takes the older ones' places, and the newest 20 always stay. A file as it came in from elsewhere is never thinned out. Versions are named by the clock, and while it disagrees with the network's time (Android 13 and later), none is thinned out.
  - **When, why and what changed:** versions sit on a timeline under their days (Today, Yesterday, a weekday, a date). Each says when ("Just now", "12 minutes ago", then its time, in the phone's 12- or 24-hour clock), why it was kept ("Before you removed “Food” and 3 ideas under it", "When you opened it"), and what changed from the one before it: ideas added, removed, renamed and moved, by name when they're few ("Renamed “Hotels” to “Where to stay”") and counted when they're many. The map as it is now comes first, with what changed since the latest version, and the list keeps up while it's open: an idea dragged on the map beside it, or a change from elsewhere, shows at once. A YAML map's changes read the same, ideas written in brackets included, and one that gains a second idea at its top, which Orbit puts under a center named by its title, has that idea added. A version that was already the map as it was before a big change says why. Versions kept before Orbit kept reasons show their time and changes only.
  - **Look, then restore:** choosing one shows it on the map to look around in, its badge turning into a cookie. Nothing changes it there, so it's never written over the map: changing the map while it's shown (its tags, its name, a goal's step, undo) brings back the map as it is now first, with the history open on it, and presenting, the path, Edit as text, talking and studying close the history. Restore makes it the map again, with Undo, and the map as it was joins the history too. Looking around a version doesn't change where the map reopens.
  - **Quick on long histories:** what each version changed is worked out as its row comes on screen, so long histories and maps of thousands of ideas open at once, and TalkBack reads each row as one: when, its day, why and what changed.
- **Edit as text:** the map's orbit-md, colored as Orbit reads it, with the map redrawing above as you type and Orbit's notes about the text below. It also shows as YAML, Orbit's own form of the map, which says that an edit there writes the markdown anew; switching back without one gives the markdown back just as it was written. What's typed is kept however soon the editor or the map closes after it, and Undo and Redo wait for it, so they step back from it, never over it. Text typed on the map as it was before it changed outside the editor (an edit on the map beside it, or a change from elsewhere, even as the text switches between markdown and YAML) is never saved over that change: it goes into the map's history, with a message.
- **Export and share:**
  - a web page: one HTML file with Orbit, the map, its pictures, its live photos' motions and its sounds inside (up to 100 MB of pictures, 25 MB of motions and 25 MB of sounds), which flies in any browser, offline, and presents along the map's path with captions, keys and swipes (videos from the phone stay out of it, and their posters show). What doesn't fit stays out, and Export says so: a picture that stays out leaves its idea without it, and a live photo whose motion stays out shows its still. Printed from the browser, it's laid out as the PDF is. Its `generator` says which builds of the app and of Orbit made it, as "Orbit for Android 0.4.0 (Orbit 0.3.0, 00c0099)";
  - a PDF, or the map printed: its title and its picture (the current view, the whole map or the map as it opens), then its outline with every idea's details, link, note (its tables at the page's width, its checklists' boxes drawn as they are), picture, video and sound (by its caption, with its address when it's on the web), files (by name, kind and size), and how far its to-dos and goals have come. Paper is white, so dark styles print in their light version and the app's colors as they are in light. Android's print dialog prints it or saves it as a PDF, with its own Save as PDF even on phones without Google's apps, on the paper it picks for the phone's region or any other;
  - a PNG of the current view, the whole map or the map as it opens (on its center, or on the whole map for a map set to open on it), with or without its background;
  - an SVG;
  - markdown, YAML, OPML (the outline, for outliners and other mind map apps, which reads back into Orbit as it was), or a zip with the map's pictures, videos and sounds. Markdown, YAML and OPML can also be copied. A map written in YAML exports as its own YAML, as it's written, and as the markdown Orbit writes of it.

  A map at its limits, a gigabyte of pictures, videos and sounds, exports as a web page or a zip, and opens on a computer, without the phone running out of memory: a zip with all of it, or a page with all it carries inside, about 200 MB, is written a piece at a time into a file of its own, which is saved or shared from there, and a computer's page is made once for each version of the map. A picture goes a piece at a time too, however big it is, and so does one shared from the full-screen viewer. A phone without room for the file says so before any of it is written. A zip past what Orbit 0.4.0 and earlier open (about 100 MB, or a file over 64 MB) says so beside it, in Export and the send sheet: "Older versions of Orbit can't open a zip this big: update Orbit wherever it's opened".

  Each way out says what goes with the map, counted: "With 3 pictures and a video · 12.4 MB" for the zip, "With 3 pictures and an audio file inside" and what stays on this device for the web page (its videos, pictures past its 100 MB, sounds past its 25 MB, and live photos' motions past theirs, those chosen to go as they are among them), the pictures the PDF prints, and "Just the text" for markdown, YAML and OPML. Sharing a map from its card on Home says the same before Android's share sheet opens, when the map has pictures, videos or audio, and offers just its text instead: a markdown file, or for a map written in YAML, the YAML file it is. What goes is what the map shows: a web address in it that happens to say `media/…` brings none of the map's files along.
- **What leaves without saying:** pictures, videos and sounds go to other people without what their files say beyond what they show or play, such as where and when they were taken or recorded and on what, and a sound's tags: in shared zips, maps sent to a device, web pages, SVGs, PDFs and prints, the browsers a map is presented or opened in, a picture, video or sound shared from the full-screen viewer, and a video opened in another app; one Orbit can't take that out of, as a picture or video cut short, a file damaged anywhere but at its end, or one it doesn't recognize, such as an idea's PDF, stays on the phone unless the person chooses otherwise. As a way out starts at the phone (sharing or exporting a map as a zip, web page, PDF or SVG, sending it, opening it on a computer, presenting it in browsers or on a TV), Orbit asks once, naming the files and what would go with them, whether to leave them out, as it does unless told otherwise, or share them as they are; the answer holds until that way out ends. Each way out's line names them rather than counting them, “A video stays on this device: Orbit can't take out where it was recorded.”, and says once they go as they are. The full-screen viewer asks the same of the one file it shares, and the video player of the video it opens in another app. A browser is never asked, and never gets them. Pictures keep their pixels, color profile and which way up they are, and an animated PNG's or WebP's frames, with none of the chunks other apps keep their own in, such as content credentials; HEIF and AVIF pictures have their EXIF and XMP written over where they are, an animated AVIF's movie is cleaned as a video is, and they leave without the video a motion photo keeps inside them, a phone's own trailer and other apps' boxes; GIFs keep their frames, timing and looping without comments or other apps' blocks such as XMP; SVG drawings keep only what draws them, the text diagrams lay out included, without their metadata, comments or an editor's own elements and attributes, nor scripts, links or anything they'd run or fetch from elsewhere, and the pictures inside them go clean too; and BMPs keep their picture and color profile, without what an app keeps around them. Every video and sound keeps every frame where it is, as what it says beyond them is written over where it is:
  - MP4 and QuickTime videos, and voice memos and other sounds in MP4: the boxes cameras and phones write their place, date and make into become free space, in the movie, its tracks, their media and its fragments, and so does what free space held; their headers' dates are zero, and the names cameras give themselves in a track's handlers and a video's description say nothing;
  - WebM videos and sounds: their tags, chapters, attachments, title, date, and their files' and tracks' names become empty space, and the apps that made them are named with nothing;
  - MP3s and AAC: their ID3 tags (title, artist, cover, chapters, comments) say nothing, and the ID3v1, APE and Lyrics3 tags after them stay out;
  - FLAC: its tags, pictures, cue sheet and other apps' blocks become padding;
  - Ogg (Vorbis, Opus, Speex and FLAC): their comments are emptied, a cover among them;
  - WAV: the chunks that make the sound stay (its format, samples, cue points, loops and instrument), and every other one, a Broadcast Wave's originator and when it was recorded, its tags, iXML, XMP and ID3 among them, becomes empty space.

  What an encoder writes into the sound or the picture itself, as its name in an MP3's first frame, stays, and a video keeps all of its tracks, so a GPS track an action camera or a drone records beside the picture goes with it, as the privacy policy says. A sound cut short at its end, as a recording that stopped or a download that didn't finish, goes clean too, as far as its last whole frame, page or sample: the unfinished bit at its end, which no player can play, stays out, tags it was cut in among it, and the file says where it ends now. A voice memo or a video in MP4 cut short stays on the phone, as it can't be played without all of it. The map's own files stay as they are, and so does Export all, a backup.
- **Present:** see [Presenting](#presenting).

## Presenting

- **One tap away:** Present has a button of its own on every map, starting at the idea in focus, and each map's menu on the home screen presents it from the top.
- **Full screen:** one idea at a time, in reading order or along your path, with its note as the caption, under the map or beside it on a screen wider than tall. A note's tables and checklists show there as in the details, and on the audience's screens at the captions' size; nothing checks while presenting. The controls step aside after a moment, and a tap brings them back; they stay while TalkBack is on, which can't tap them back, and for longer when Android's accessibility settings ask for more time to act. Turn the captions off for the map alone.
- **Your own path:** "Presentation path" in a map's menu starts at the top of the map: tap the ideas in the order you'll present them, and each joins a numbered strip along the bottom. Flying back up to an idea already on the path doesn't add it again, so going from branch to branch stays simple, and "Add again" comes back to one on purpose. Tap a step to fly to it, long-press it to move it, or take it off with its ×. Without a path, maps present in reading order, as they do once a path has only one of its ideas left on the map. An idea renamed later, on the map or in its editor, keeps its steps, and its rehearsed time, as does the map's center when the map is renamed from Home.
- **Rehearse:** run through the presentation with a clock on each idea. Afterwards Orbit shows how long it took and where the time went, idea by idea. Presenting, the controls then show whether you're on pace, behind or ahead, and the presenter's view counts down each idea's rehearsed time. Rehearse a part on its own and only its times change. A map Orbit can't fully read keeps the times of the ideas it doesn't show, for when it's fixed or Orbit is updated.
- **Like a clicker:** swipe across the screen, or use arrow keys, page keys, volume keys or a presentation clicker; a key held down steps once. It can also move on by itself, at the pace chosen in Settings, or eight seconds an idea when only a tap moves it on; paused and played again, it keeps that pace. The screen stays on.
- **On a second screen:** a TV on HDMI or USB-C, or a wireless display, shows the audience the map and its captions. The phone becomes the presenter's view:
  - the map as the audience sees it;
  - the idea's whole note, and what comes next;
  - a clock, your pace against the last rehearsal, and big buttons; on a phone, where the audience is watching has a line of its own under them, so it always reads whole.

  Unplug the screen and the presentation carries on, on the phone.
- **In any browser in the room:** "Show in a browser" serves the presentation from the phone over the local Wi-Fi, or its hotspot, with no server and no cost. A laptop on a projector, or the phones in the room, open a short address with a six-digit code, or scan its QR code, and follow every step; the presenter view counts who's watching. It runs only while presenting with it on. Its sheet, with the address, says that whoever joins gets the map with its pictures and videos, counted; its sounds play from the phone, and no browser gets their files. Nor any other file of the map's folder, such as a picture taken off the map and kept for its earlier versions: a browser gets only what the map shows.
  - **Who's watching:** people type their name as they join, or come in as Guest 1, Guest 2 and so on, and the phone lists them with when each joined. Remove someone and their browser says so, and they can't join again.
  - **Who may join:** a 4-digit PIN to say aloud, besides the code (it's never in the address or the QR code), a limit on how many at once, and a switch that stops anyone new joining once everyone's in. The next presentation starts with the same names, PIN and limit choices. A device that types five wrong PINs waits before it can try again, longer each time, and after twenty wrong PINs from every device together, new devices wait too, so guessing from address after address gets nowhere; the presenter's phone says so, and a new PIN lets everyone in again.
  - **A phone that sleeps** during the talk picks up where the presentation is when it wakes, however long it slept, without joining again, even once no one new may join. After a minute asleep its place is free for someone else, so in a presentation that has filled up meanwhile, its page says it's full and comes back in as soon as there's room.
- **On a TV, with Google Cast (the play build):** the TV runs the audience's page, and Orbit draws the map there, pictures included; the phone is the presenter's view. The receiver is Orbit's own, at orbit.hyuchia.com/cast (see [Casting to TVs](../CONTRIBUTING.md#casting-to-tvs)). Google Cast starts only once you present, and from then until Orbit closes it looks for TVs while Orbit is on screen. After a presentation, the map's toolbar shows the TV it's still connected to, to stop casting. Built without its ID, the presentation's cast button opens Android's own screen casting, which shows the phone's screen on the TV.
- **Pictures and videos, big:** when the idea in focus has a picture or a video, the presenter's view offers "Show the picture" and "Play the video". The audience's screens show it over the map, with its title and caption, and the preview shows what they see.
  - **A live photo** plays its motion once as the audience's screens show it, muted, as Orbit's viewer does, and the presenter's view has "Play the live photo again" for every screen while it shows.
  - **A video** plays with its sound on a second screen and a TV, and the phone has play and pause, "Start over" and its time ("0:42 / 3:10"). A browser that won't play sound before it's touched shows "Tap for sound", and one that joins halfway starts where the others are. A video file kept on the phone can't reach a TV, which shows its poster with its title and caption instead, and the phone says so.
  - **Back to the map:** its button, a step or Esc. A presentation that moves on by itself waits while a picture or video shows, and gives the idea its whole time again after.
  - **On the phone alone,** they open full screen over the presentation; a step key or the clicker closes them and steps.
- **Sounds play from the phone:** when the idea in focus has a sound, a button plays it from the presenter's phone, under its picture or art in the presenter's view, which says so, and at the top on the phone alone; the presentation goes on, and stepping on stops it.

## Studying

- **Flashcards from a map:** "Study" in a map's menu begins at the idea in focus, and on its card on Home at the top. Every idea under it with ideas or a note of its own is a card.
- **A card at a time:** the map flies to the card with the ideas under it hidden, their places faint in its orbit, each in its idea's color, and says what to recall. A tap on the map, Space or Show reveals them: they grow out of the card to their places, each place fading as its idea lands on it, and its note shows with the answers, its tables and checklists as in the details (a session checks nothing).
- **Again or Knew it:** with the buttons, the keys (1 and 2, or the arrows) or a swipe toward the answer's button. Each button says when the card comes back. Again brings it back a few cards later; Knew it sends it on to its next box: 1, 3, 7, 16, 35, 80, then 180 days. Undo takes the last answer back.
- **Sessions:** the cards due come first, the most overdue first, then the day's new ones, in outline order. Revealing a card never gives away another's: the ideas under every card wait hidden until it comes. A summary closes the session, with the whole branch back on the map: how many cards, how many on the first try, and when the next ones come back, with "Keep going" for new cards left for another day.
- **New cards a day:** 20 unless the learner chooses 5, 10, 30 or 50, in Settings or right where a session runs out of new cards (its summary, and "All caught up"). Chosen there, "Keep going" brings in what the day then has room for, or another day's worth. Cards first studied today on any device count toward it.
- **Nothing due:** "All caught up" says when the next cards come back, and "Study anyway" goes through every card: knowing one early leaves it where it was, and missing one starts it over. With new cards still waiting, it offers "Keep going" and how many a day first.
- **Sounds:** a revealed card whose idea has a sound offers to play it, under its note: a word said aloud, for a language's cards. A session stops any sound playing as it starts, and each card's as the next one comes, and the small player stays out of it.
- **With private maps and drops:** a private map is studied once it's unlocked, and a session on one that locks waits behind the lock with the map, coming back where it was once unlocked. While a map is studied it takes no drops, and none of its ideas can be dragged out.
- **Never in the map:** how the cards are going is kept in the map's details, never its text or its history; the hidden ideas are only how the map is drawn. A map Orbit can't fully read is studied for the ideas it shows, and keeps the progress of the others for when it's fixed or Orbit is updated.
- **No answer lost:** each answer is kept the moment it's given, a card at a time, even as the map closes; Keep going and Study anyway go on from every answer before them, and leaving the folder keeps both this device's answers and the others'. An idea that stops being a card keeps its progress for when it's one again.
- **The map changing meanwhile:** a card removed on another device while it's studied leaves the session at once, and the next one comes; it can't be answered.
- **Renamed ideas keep their cards:** an idea renamed, on the map or in its editor, keeps how its card is going, as its latest answer on any device left it, and so does the map's center when the map is renamed from Home.
- **Across devices, through the folder:** with the maps kept in a folder, each device's answers go there too, in its own hidden records (one per map, named after its file and kept under the device's random id, saying the ideas' ids, how each card goes, and up to eight names the map's file had before), and it reads the others'. A card goes as its latest answer on any device left it, so studying on the phone counts on the tablet, and two devices studying at once never clash. A map renamed in the folder keeps its answers on every device, and so does an idea renamed on any of them. Choosing a folder brings the answers along; leaving it keeps every device's on this one. Releases before this one never see the records; a record a newer Orbit wrote on this device, which this one would misread, is left as it is; and progress another version of Orbit wrote in a way this one doesn't expect never stops a session.
- **Everywhere:** phones and tablets, either way up (a phone on its side keeps its height for the map, with the card's panel beside it), keyboards, light and dark, English, Spanish and Portuguese. With a keyboard, Tab goes through the session's buttons and Space or Enter presses the one it's on, while 1, 2, the arrows and Escape still answer and stop. TalkBack hears how many ideas a card hides, then their names as they show.
- **From a map's card, always:** Study and Present on a map's card start, even on a tablet whose map is already open beside the list, whatever it was doing.

## Between your devices

- **Send to a device:** from a map's menu or its card. Your other devices with Orbit open on the same Wi-Fi show up by name, with their address, found with Android's own network service discovery, so it works without Google services. One tap sends the map, encrypted end to end, so no one else on the Wi-Fi can read it on the way. The other device asks before taking it, then opens it where you left off, presentation path included, and the sender sees it opened: once the codes match, only that device can say so, never another on the Wi-Fi. The sheet says it sends a copy, with the map's pictures, videos and sounds counted (a sound is what an idea plays as one, so a voice memo's `.mp4` counts as audio), and the other device's question counts them the same way, and, while the maps aren't kept in a folder, that only a synced folder keeps maps in step between devices.
- **A check code:** before the map goes, both devices show four digits, and the map leaves only once you say they match. When they don't, another device on the Wi-Fi answered in its place: cancel, and nothing of the map leaves the phone. The other device shows the code with the map's sender, and asks again once the map arrives.
- **Older versions:** a device with Orbit 0.2.0 or earlier sends maps before checking codes, so the two can't send maps to each other: each says which device to update, at most once every ten minutes, so a device offering again and again can't keep the phone's message up. An older Orbit since then still takes maps, and the dialog with the code says first what won't open there: the files of kinds it doesn't keep, which stay on this device (Orbit 0.3.0 has no sounds, and keeps only voice memos, unplayed), and a map it can only show, being written for a newer format than it reads. The map still goes once you say the codes match, and what such a device says of it, opened or not, is taken at its word. Orbit 0.4.0 and earlier take maps of up to 64 MB: a bigger map goes no further than saying hello, so its code never shows there, and the sender reads "It has an older Orbit, which can't take a map this big (180 MB). Update Orbit there, then send it again." This version takes maps of up to the gigabyte a map holds, and says so as it answers.
- **Maps keep their identity:** a map sent back and forth updates in place instead of piling up copies. An open map catches up at once, undo brings its earlier version back, and the version it replaced stays in the map's history. Its own pictures, videos and sounds stay as they are, with all their files say, and the ones it hasn't come in before its new text. "Keep both" makes a copy instead.
- **Other ways:**
  - share it as a file, through Quick Share, Huawei Share, Bluetooth, email or a chat, which opens straight into Orbit, updating or adding; a zip of every map, from Export all, brings in all its maps;
  - open it on a computer: a Chromebook or laptop opens a short address in its browser to explore, present or download the map, which the send sheet's next step, with the address and a way back, says goes with its pictures and videos. The phone lists who opened it, and can ask for a PIN, limit how many at once, stop anyone new, or remove someone;
  - type the other device's address, shown in Settings, where a network keeps devices from finding each other.
- **Your choice:** Settings can stop nearby devices from offering maps, and choose the name they see: the device's own until you change it.
- **Only the room:** the phone listens only while Orbit is open and there's a reason to (taking maps, a map open on a computer, presenting in browsers), only on the Wi-Fi, hotspot, cable or USB tethering, never over mobile data, only to other devices there, never to another app on the phone itself, and only at the address it shows, so a website can't reach it under a name of its own. Codes have six digits, and a device that types five wrong ones waits before it can try again, longer each time; browsers already in are never kept waiting. After a hundred wrong codes from every device together, new devices wait too, up to an hour, so guessing from address after address gets nowhere; a device on the Wi-Fi can use that to keep newcomers out for a while, and anyone in a room on a Wi-Fi they don't trust can be kept out the same way, which is the price of codes that can't be guessed. A crowd, or a slow or stuck browser, never holds up the rest of the app, and a device that sends a byte at a time is cut off. A map arriving has to start coming within three minutes of the codes showing, and to have room on the phone before any of it comes: it opens as it arrives, straight from the connection, into its own files, so it needs room for itself once; one that runs out of room leaves nothing behind, and one cut short on its way can come again whole while it has time. The browsers in a room share one zip of the map, made once for each version of it, only once there's room for all of it. Whatever the phone was still doing for a device stops as it stops listening.
- **Keep maps in a folder:** Settings can keep every map as a markdown file in a folder you choose: on the phone, on an SD card, or a sync app's or cloud drive's folder. Android's own folder picker chooses it, so it works without Google services.
  - **Both ways:** an edit here is written to the map's file once it settles, and a file changed there, by a sync app such as Syncthing, on a computer or on another device, changes its map. An open map catches up at once, and its earlier text stays in its history.
  - **When:** as Orbit opens and closes, every two minutes while it's open, and on "Check the folder now". A map, or a step, the folder's app fails on is tried again by the next sync while the others go on, and a problem that lasts more than one sync shows on Home too, with "Try again" and a way to Settings. So do pictures, videos or sounds that go on failing to arrive from the folder for ten minutes, and Orbit's own record of the maps deleted here when it can't be read: the folder then waits, rather than bring a deleted map back. Choosing the same folder again, as after restoring a phone, keeps Orbit's access to it past a restart.
  - **New and deleted:** a file new in the folder becomes a map. Deleting a map deletes its file, and the other way round, unless the map is open or changed since; a map deleted while the maps were kept in another folder takes its file there once they're kept there again. A file renamed there keeps its map. When more than three maps' files leave the folder at once (moved into a subfolder, say, or lost by a sync app), Home asks first: they go to the trash, or stay and are written back. Maps the folder sends to the trash show on Home too, with the way to the trash, and stay there until that's read, however long it takes; while private maps hide, "Got it" leaves theirs for when they show.
  - **Nothing lost:** a map changed both here and there is kept twice, the folder's as "… (changed elsewhere)", named as Orbit names a map, markdown or YAML (one a newer Orbit made is kept just as it came, and only its file's name says it's a copy), once: if a sync stops right after, the next one writes this map's text rather than keeping both again. A folder with none of the maps' files left, emptied or swapped for another, gets them written again rather than every map deleted, once a sync ten minutes later agrees, so a folder still filling (a restored phone's, as its sync app catches up) isn't written over with older text. A map whose details came from another phone's backup waits a day, rather than ten minutes, for its file to come back before it follows it out. A folder with markdown files already asks before taking them in, and maps it has already, from another device, are followed rather than copied.
  - **Pictures, videos and sounds** go in a `media` folder beside the files, where the maps point at them. A copy there that a sync cut short is written again, and one still arriving there isn't taken until it's whole. One that reaches the folder after its map's text is fetched by a later sync. Each is copied a piece at a time, and only once this device has room for it and some to spare, both ways when the folder is on its storage: until then it waits, its map with it when it's going to the folder, and the sync says "A picture, video or sound needs more room than this device has free". The folder has one `media` for every map, so when another map's file already has a picture's name there (as two maps from zips each with their "photo.jpg", of one size or not: what they hold tells them apart), the picture gets a name of its own ("photo-2.jpg"), in the folder and in its map. One there too big for a map (see [Your maps are safe](#your-maps-are-safe)) stays only in the folder, and Settings says which, until it's replaced there or its map has room for it. A device with Orbit 0.4.0 or earlier keeps less of a map (50 MB a file, 90 MB a map), and each device's record in the folder says how much it keeps, so Settings names, beside such a device, the maps it keeps only part of: "Keeps only part of “Trip” and “Concert”: files over 50 MB, and what's past 90 MB a map, stay in the folder there. Update Orbit there to keep them all". A picture the folder's app renames as it's put there (some keep names in lower case, or number a name that's taken) keeps its map pointing at it, and a map with a file's very text but another picture by the same name gives its own a name of its own, so every device shows the same.
  - **Devices with other versions:** each device keeps a small record of itself in the folder's hidden `.orbit/devices/`: a random id made for its install, which names the record and is never backed up, so a new phone is a new device, its name, its Orbit, the newest map format it reads, the kinds of file it carries, and when it last synced (written again once a day at most while nothing else in it changes). No release reads that folder for maps. Settings lists the devices that use the folder with their versions, and what one with another Orbit can't open: files of a kind it doesn't carry, such as sounds (".mp3 and .ogg") for one from before them, while voice memos, `.mp4`s, go to every version, or maps saved for a newer format, which it only shows. A map Orbit writes there for a newer format than one of them reads is in Settings' notes, as one that device can only show. When that's so for files the folder holds, Home says so once for each new version, asking to update the older device. Devices show once they sync with this version of Orbit or a later one, and a record not written in three months is of a device that stopped using the folder. A device's own record that a newer Orbit wrote, which this one would misread, is left as it is.

## Your maps are safe

A map holds someone's thinking, so Orbit never loses one, or any part of one:

- **Every write is whole.** Maps, their details, their versions and pictures are written beside the old file, reach the disk, and only then take its place, so a crash or a dead battery never leaves half a map.
- **The last edit stays as a map closes.** Leaving a map, with Back or its arrow, a tablet's close or another map opened beside the list, keeps what was being typed on it, in its fields and in Edit as text, YAML included, and a save still being written lands before the last one, so the last idea or word typed is the map's. The version kept before it says why, as any save's would: the visit's first edit, a big change, the text edited by hand.
- **Nothing empties a map by mistake.** Text with no ideas in it, markdown or YAML (a YAML map whose nodes are all empty, say), never replaces a map that has some, and a save made before the map changed elsewhere (another device, the folder, ideas caught on the go) never writes over that change, even one the map's page made just before the change reached it (each save says which text it was made on), nor one waiting behind a slow write, in whatever order they come: it goes into the history instead, as the map closes too, and Undo brings it back, or, for that last one, a message with the way to the history. A map that changes elsewhere as it opens is drawn as that change left it.
- **Maps Orbit can't fully read are only shown.** When Orbit can't read part of a map's text, or a newer version of Orbit made it, the map opens read-only with a note saying why, and its file stays exactly as it is until the text is fixed or Orbit is updated. Undo, versions brought back, and changes from elsewhere are drawn as they are, never rewritten. An idea a newer Orbit wrote as a newer kind of Progress or goal shows as a plain idea, its name inside, and keeps what it was written with.
- **Versions at the right moments:** as each visit to a map begins, every ten minutes of editing, and before big changes: an idea removed with the ones under it, a version brought back, the text edited by hand, a version from elsewhere arriving. A file that comes in as a new map (an import, a share, a new file in the folder) is kept as it came, for good, before Orbit ever writes it, OPML and FreeMind files included; an XMind workbook, a zip that can hold several maps, isn't. A newer version of a map that arrives later (from the folder, another device or a file) becomes the map, with the map as it was kept in its history first, and both are thinned out as they age, like any version.
- **A trash:** deleted maps wait 30 days in Settings, Trash, history and all, to be restored or deleted for good, a map whose file Orbit can't read among them, listed by its name on the phone. A map deleted while it's open becomes read-only, with a way to restore it. A clock that's wrong never takes one early: a map goes for good only once it's been seen past its 30 days at two launches a day apart, and never while the clock disagrees with the network's time; one deleted with the clock ahead, or behind Orbit's install, gets its 30 days again from when that's seen. One the folder sent there waits for its note to be read, and so does one whose details can't be read, which may say so. Deleting for good, one map or the whole trash, says a map went only once it has: one whose files don't all delete, on storage that fails, stays in the trash, its text the last to go, private if it was, and the trash says it couldn't delete it.
- **Only files nothing points at are cleared:** as a map closes, a picture, video, sound or idea's file Orbit named in its folder that neither the map nor any of its versions points at, by its name or with the name percent-encoded as a link may write it, is deleted; never one added in the last ten minutes, one put there some other way, or any while a version, or the history itself, can't be read. The versions are read one at a time, and only when the map itself leaves a file unused.
- **The folder never deletes on a hunch.** A map only follows its file out of the folder once two syncs at least ten minutes apart both miss it, and then into the trash. A file that can't be read is left for the next sync, writes to the folder are read back before they count, a file this device's write left cut short is written again, and one emptied there gets the map's ideas back. A file that's shorter than its map, as deleting the map's last ideas on another device leaves it, is taken once it has stayed as it is for half a minute, so one still arriving isn't. Nor is a file that stops partway through a character, or isn't the size the folder said (an empty one too), until it's whole or has stayed that way. A file changed since the folder was listed isn't written over or deleted: it's looked at again right before, by its very text before a deletion, and wherever the folder's app gives no times, so another device's edit is kept twice or comes back as a map. A write of this device's it couldn't read back is still its own, never kept twice or taken for a new map. And a markdown file too big to be a map (over 8 MB) stays only in the folder, as Settings says, even one whose size the folder's app can't tell.
- **Pictures, videos, sounds and files within limits:** a video, a sound or an idea's file from the phone may be up to 250 MB, and a map's pictures, videos, sounds and files up to 1 GB together (sizes from 1,000 MB on read in GB). Pictures from the phone are kept at most 2048 pixels on their longer side, as JPEG or PNG; an animated GIF or WebP is kept as it is, up to 250 MB, so it still moves (a bigger one is kept still, as other pictures are). So a map always fits in its zip and comes back from it, and goes straight to a nearby device: each way takes it a piece at a time, never whole in memory, and only once there's room for it. A big video is offered smaller first (see "Big videos made smaller"). Larger ones are refused, saying why, and nothing of them stays. Ones in the folder maps are kept in get the same room, and those past it stay only there. Orbit 0.4.0 and earlier keep 50 MB a file and 90 MB a map: in a shared folder they keep what's past that only in the folder, as ever, and Settings here says, beside such a device, which maps it keeps only part of.
- **Sounds in earlier versions:** Orbit 0.3.0 and earlier show a map with sounds as it is, and keep its sounds as they are when they change it. A voice memo, and an M4A from the phone, is an `.mp4`, which they carry in zips, backups, the folder and sending too; other sound files go along from this version on.
