# Everywhere

- **Phones and tablets, either way up:**
  - **Tablets and foldables:** your maps sit beside the open map, whichever way up, which is the one you opened last when the app starts; a small tablet upright, without room for both, shows the map on its own.
    Upright, beside the list, the map's top bar leaves out Redo until something's undone, and has the lock in the map's menu, as on a phone.
    The outline, editor, details, search and styles open beside the map, and while they're open, or the map's text or a presentation is, the list steps aside for the map.
    Back closes what's on top, one thing at a time, as on a phone: a panel, then the map, leaving your maps beside room for the next one; only then does it leave Orbit.
  - **A phone on its side:** the map's tools stand in a rail at the side, the breadcrumbs and the idea's buttons move into the top bar, and panels open beside the map, the search's rising over the top bar as the keyboard comes up, so its field stays in view.
    Home fits its title and search into one bar, so the maps get the room; the search is as wide as the bar leaves it beside the title, and in a window too narrow for both, as beside another app's or in larger words, it stays above the maps.
    Turned upright again at the top of the list, Home shows its search above the maps, as it was.
  - **Turning the screen with a map's search or outline open,** a phone's or a tablet's, either way: the panel moves between over the map and beside it, and the search keeps what's typed, what it found and the place in its list, the outline its folds and its place.
  - **The map always fits what's left:**
    Orbit fits each view into the room the toolbar, the rail, a sheet or the captions leave, and glides when that changes.
    What opens beside the map leaves it a phone's width at least: the map's text narrows to make that room, a panel without it opens over the map, and Style and layout leaves the toolbar for the map's menu when the map beside a panel is too narrow for it.
  - **Narrow windows and larger words,** as beside another app's: the sketch's description, in one row on a phone on its side, lets its title step aside where the field would be narrow, then puts Done under Keep drawing, and narrower still gives the field the row with the buttons under it where the room above the keyboard is tall enough, else lets the field narrow beside them, its label on one line, so there's always room to type, the buttons keep their words whole and nothing goes under the keyboard.
- **Keyboards and mice,** on tablets and Chromebooks:
  - **Building a map from the keyboard:**
    Tab adds an idea under the one with keyboard focus, Shift+Enter one beside it, F2 renames it, Delete deletes it, with Undo, and Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V copy, cut and paste into it.
    While typing, Enter brings the next idea, Tab goes under the one just typed, and Shift+Tab comes back up.
    Arrows move between ideas, Enter flies in, and Escape goes back.
  - **Shortcuts:**
    - Ctrl+Z undoes, Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y redoes (on a map that can change), and Ctrl+F searches.
    - Ctrl+1, 2 and 3 switch between the orbit, the whole map and the outline.
    - Ctrl+E edits the idea in focus, F5 or Ctrl+Enter presents, and Ctrl+Shift+S studies.
    - On the list, Ctrl+N starts a map, Ctrl+Shift+N one from a template, Ctrl+O opens a file, Ctrl+F searches the maps, and Ctrl+, opens the settings.
    - Ctrl+/ lists the ones the screen has, as does Android's own list (Meta+/), and so does Keyboard shortcuts in a map's menu.
    - They work with a sheet or a dialog open too, a map's panels as sheets included, and Android's list has them there; a picture or video full screen, and a private map's lock, keep the keys to themselves.
  - **Escape** closes dialogs, sheets and panels, and otherwise goes back, as Back does.
  - **Tab** goes through a screen's buttons in order, Home's + right after its bar, and what has focus has a ring around it.
    A dialog opened from the keyboard has its answer focused, so Enter gives it; where the answer takes something away (maps for good, a tag from every map), that's Cancel.
  - **The mouse:** a button shows its name and keys as the pointer rests on it.
    A right click opens an idea's or a map's menu where it was clicked, and the wheel zooms the map, or a picture full screen, around the pointer.
  - **Pictures and videos:** the arrows go through the pictures, + and - zoom and 0 fits; in a video, a sound or an animated picture, space or K plays and pauses, J and L go ten seconds back and on, the arrows too (five seconds in YouTube's and Vimeo's players, as on their sites), and M turns the sound off and on.
    A button Tab is on takes Space and Enter, as it takes a tap, and a video's controls stay while it's on one of them.
    Escape closes them.
- **Screen readers:** section titles are headings to jump between; a map's card says its name first, then that it's pinned or private, and its menu's actions are its actions too; each button names what it acts on ("Rename “Work”"); and while the + menu is open, it's all they have.
- **Material You,** light and dark, or Orbit's own colors.
  You pick in Settings, along with haptics, snapping the orbit to a planet, the style for new maps and how long presentations wait.
  Light or dark set against the system holds everywhere Orbit draws: screens change without a flash of the other, the bars' icons follow, and on Android 13 and later so does the splash as Orbit starts.
  A map whose style is always light or dark keeps the bars' icons its own as the system's theme changes under it, and leaves the app's as the app is then.
- **In English, Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese,** as the phone is, or as Settings picks on Android 13 and later: every screen, the map's own controls and what screen readers hear on it, the pages browsers and TVs show, the welcome map, the templates, the privacy policy and the icon search's emoji names.
  A phone in another Portuguese, as in Portugal, gets Brazil's.
  Long names break where each language breaks its words.
- **Launcher shortcuts:** a new map, and the maps you opened last.
  They follow the maps: renamed with them, wherever a map is renamed; turned off, saying so, when a map is deleted, here or in the folder maps are kept in; and on again when it's restored.
- **Updates without a store:**
  Orbit downloaded from its website or GitHub asks GitHub once a day whether a new version is out.
  The home screen then says so, with a button to its download page and Not now, which waits for the next one; About shows what the last look found and looks again when tapped.
  Looks never overlap, so two windows open at once ask once, and a look the phone can't store, as when it's full, still shows what it found.
  Settings can turn it off.
  The builds from Google Play, F-Droid and AppGallery leave it to their store.
- **Without Google services,** on Huawei phones and others, everything works:
  - **Maps draw** with the phone's own WebView, Android System WebView or Huawei's, from Chromium 99 on.
    An older one gets a notice saying what to update, with a button to its page in the phone's app store (Google Play, AppGallery or another) when there's a store that opens it.
    Your maps stay safe meanwhile.
    When the page that draws a map stops, as Android frees memory, or doesn't load at all, the map's place says which, that the map is safe, and Load it again draws it in a new page; Orbit never hands a map, a thumbnail or a picture to a page that didn't load.
    A sketch under way waits, whole, and the map's place says it comes back with the map.
    A page that loads but never starts, or never says the map is drawn, counts as one that didn't load: it has a minute and a half from its start, a quarter of one once it has loaded, and a minute and a half to draw the map it's sent, counting only while Orbit is in sight, so the map never spins for good, and a map it draws after all, once given up on, never shows.
  - **Sending maps** finds devices with Android's own network service discovery, and keeping maps in a folder uses Android's own folder picker.
  - **Presenting:** without Google Cast, the cast button opens Android's own screen casting where the phone has it, and presenting in browsers works on any network.
  - **Speaking ideas:** without a speech service, a held + opens the new idea for typing or the keyboard's microphone.
  - **Crashes:** a report of what went wrong (Orbit's and Android's versions and where in the code, nothing from your maps, not even a map's name of one or two letters) stays on the phone, and the next launch offers to share it or delete it.
    Quoted words go from the report whole, in any kind of quotes, even with apostrophes or line breaks of their own.
    Each crash keeps a report of its own, even two in the same moment or one after the phone's clock was set back, and one that won't delete says so, and stays offered until it does.
    What Android itself writes of the crash, in the phone's log and its own crash reports, which may leave the phone when it shares usage and diagnostics, names no map either.
- **Your data:** maps are plain files on this device, backed up with your phone (see below).
  Orbit has no account and no cloud: they reach other devices only when you send or share them, or keep them in a folder that a sync app or cloud drive keeps in step.
  Home says so once, with a way to choose a folder, and Settings, at the top of Your maps, says where they're kept now: on this device, or on it and in a named folder.
