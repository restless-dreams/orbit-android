# Presenting

- **One tap away:**
  Present has a button of its own on every map, starting at the idea in focus, and each map's menu on the home screen presents it from the top.
- **Full screen:** one idea at a time, in reading order or along your path, with its note as the caption, under the map or beside it on a screen wider than tall.
  A note's tables and checklists show there as in the details, and on the audience's screens at the captions' size; nothing checks while presenting.
  The controls step aside after a moment, and a tap brings them back; they stay while TalkBack is on, which can't tap them back, and for longer when Android's accessibility settings ask for more time to act.
  With a keyboard, Tab goes through the controls, never into the map, and Space or Enter presses the one it's on; the controls stay while it's on one of them, and the other keys still step.
  Once they've stepped aside, Tab brings them back on the first of them, and Shift+Tab on the last.
  Turn the captions off for the map alone.
- **Your own path:**
  "Presentation path" in a map's menu starts at the top of the map: tap the ideas in the order you'll present them, and each joins a numbered strip along the bottom.
  Flying back up to an idea already on the path doesn't add it again, so going from branch to branch stays simple, and "Add again" comes back to one on purpose.
  Tap a step to fly to it, long-press it to move it, or take it off with its ×.
  Without a path, maps present in reading order, as they do once a path has only one of its ideas left on the map.
  An idea renamed later, on the map or in its editor, keeps its steps, and its rehearsed time, as does the map's center when the map is renamed from Home.
  Undo takes them back with its old name, and redo on again.
- **Rehearse:** run through the presentation with a clock on each idea.
  Afterwards Orbit shows how long it took and where the time went, idea by idea.
  Presenting, the controls then show whether you're on pace, behind or ahead, and the presenter's view counts down each idea's rehearsed time.
  Rehearse a part on its own and only its times change.
  A map Orbit can't fully read keeps the times of the ideas it doesn't show, for when it's fixed or Orbit is updated.
- **Like a clicker:** swipe across the screen, the caption too (a long one still scrolls up and down, and its links still go to their ideas), or use arrow keys, page keys, volume keys or a presentation clicker; a key held down steps once.
  It can also move on by itself, at the pace chosen in Settings, or eight seconds an idea when only a tap moves it on; paused and played again, it keeps that pace.
  The screen stays on.
- **On a second screen:** a TV on HDMI or USB-C, or a wireless display, shows the audience the map and its captions.
  The phone becomes the presenter's view:
  - the map as the audience sees it;
  - the idea's whole note, and what comes next;
  - a clock, your pace against the last rehearsal, and big buttons; on a phone, where the audience is watching has a line of its own under them, so it always reads whole.
    In a narrow window, as in a split screen, how far along you are takes a line of its own above the buttons, and Tab and Space or Enter press them as on the phone alone.

  Unplug the screen and the presentation carries on, on the phone.
  When the map stops on the second screen, as Android frees memory, or doesn't load there, the presenter's view says so under its bar, with Load it again, which shows the map there again at the idea on screen.
- **When the map's page stops** on the presenter's phone, as Android does to free memory, the presentation says so in the map's place, with Load again, and goes on from the idea on screen; the audience's screens keep the map meanwhile.
  A second screen that stopped with it loads again with it.
- **A map that changes while it's presented,** as a change from another device arrives, or an idea typed just as the presentation started is saved, reaches every audience as it is now, at the idea on screen.
  An idea renamed on this device, or the map renamed from Home, keeps its place in the presentation under its new name.
  One renamed on another device, which says nothing of the rename, is followed to the new idea at its place: the same parent, at the same position among its siblings.
  When the idea on screen is gone, and no new idea took its place, the presentation goes on from the step that did.
- **In any browser in the room:**
  "Show in a browser" serves the presentation from the phone over the local Wi-Fi, or its hotspot, with no server and no cost.
  A laptop on a projector, or the phones in the room, open a short address with a six-digit code, or scan its QR code, and follow every step; the presenter view counts who's watching.
  Phones in the room can pinch to zoom into the map and its captions: the map there takes no touches of its own.
  It runs only while presenting with it on.
  Its sheet, with the address, says that whoever joins gets the map with its pictures and videos, counted; its sounds play from the phone, and no browser gets their files.
  Nor any other file of the map's folder, such as a picture taken off the map and kept for its earlier versions: a browser gets only what the map shows.
  Once the presentation ends, nothing more of it goes, not even a picture a browser asked for just before, as an iPhone's photo still being made into a picture every browser draws, nor once a new presentation starts.
  A file the presenter chose to show as it is goes as a copy of that very file, made the first time a browser asks for it, in room it leaves on the phone, and deleted as the presentation ends or the map locks, or as Orbit starts again after it stopped meanwhile; one put in its place since stays on the phone, unless it can go clean.
  However many browsers ask for a big video at once, its copy is made once, and the others wait for it.
  - **Who's watching:** people type their name as they join, or come in as Guest 1, Guest 2 and so on, and the phone lists them with when each joined.
    Remove someone and their browser says so, and they can't join again.
  - **Who may join:** a 4-digit PIN to say aloud, besides the code (it's never in the address or the QR code), a limit on how many at once, and a switch that stops anyone new joining once everyone's in.
    The next presentation starts with the same names, PIN and limit choices.
    A device that types five wrong PINs waits before it can try again, longer each time, and after twenty wrong PINs from every device together, new devices wait too, so guessing from address after address gets nowhere; the presenter's phone says so, and a new PIN lets everyone in again.
  - **A phone that sleeps** during the talk picks up where the presentation is when it wakes, however long it slept, without joining again, even once no one new may join.
    After a minute asleep its place is free for someone else, so in a presentation that has filled up meanwhile, its page says it's full and comes back in as soon as there's room.
  - **A connection that drops** leaves the map on screen as the presenter left it, and the page says over it that it's looking for the presenter until it's back, then catches up.
  - **The room stays** as long as the presentation: changing the phone's language or text size meanwhile keeps its address, its code and everyone in it.
- **On a TV, with Google Cast (the play build):** the TV runs the audience's page, and Orbit draws the map there, pictures included; the phone is the presenter's view.
  The receiver is Orbit's own, at orbit.hyuchia.com/cast (see [Casting to TVs](../../CONTRIBUTING.md#casting-to-tvs)).
  Google Cast starts only once you present, and from then until Orbit closes it looks for TVs while Orbit is on screen.
  After a presentation, the map's toolbar shows the TV it's still connected to, to stop casting.
  When something the phone sends doesn't reach the TV, as on a Wi-Fi that drops it, the presenter's view says so under its bar, with Send it again, which shows the TV the presentation as it is now.
  Built without its ID, the presentation's cast button opens Android's own screen casting, which shows the phone's screen on the TV.
- **Pictures and videos, big:** when the idea in focus has a picture or a video, the presenter's view offers "Show the picture" and "Play the video".
  The audience's screens show it over the map, with its title and caption, and the preview shows what they see.
  - **A live photo** plays its motion once as the audience's screens show it, muted, as Orbit's viewer does, and the presenter's view has "Play the live photo again" for every screen while it shows.
  - **A video** plays with its sound on a second screen and a TV, and the phone has play and pause, "Start over" and its time ("0:42 / 3:10").
    Played at its end, or paused right there, it starts over on every screen.
    A browser that won't play sound before it's touched shows "Tap for sound", which starts the sound where the others are, after the video was played again from its end too, and one that joins halfway starts where the others are.
    A video file kept on the phone can't reach a TV, which shows its poster with its title and caption instead, and the phone says so.
  - **Back to the map:** its button, a step or Esc, a step back at the first idea or on at the last included.
    A presentation that moves on by itself waits while a picture or video shows, and gives the idea its whole time again after.
  - **On the phone alone,** they open full screen over the presentation; a step key or the clicker closes them and steps.
- **Sounds play from the phone:** when the idea in focus has a sound, a button plays it from the presenter's phone, at the foot of the stage in the presenter's view, which says so, an idea with only a sound included, and at the top on the phone alone; the presentation goes on, and stepping on stops it.
