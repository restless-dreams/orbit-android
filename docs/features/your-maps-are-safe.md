# Your maps are safe

A map holds someone's thinking, so Orbit never loses one, or any part of one:

- **Every write is whole.**
  Maps, their details, their versions and pictures are written beside the old file, reach the disk, and only then take its place, so a crash or a dead battery never leaves half a map.
- **The last edit stays as a map closes.**
  Leaving a map, with Back or its arrow, a tablet's close or another map opened beside the list, keeps what was being typed on it, in its fields and in Edit as text, YAML included, and a save still being written lands before the last one, so the last idea or word typed is the map's, even with an earlier version, one a newer Orbit made, on show.
  Opened again at once, before that's written, the map shows it as soon as it is, and the next edit lands on it.
  The version kept before it says why, as any save's would: the visit's first edit, a big change, the text edited by hand.
- **Nothing empties a map by mistake.**
  Text with no ideas in it, markdown or YAML (a YAML map whose nodes are all empty, say), never replaces a map that has some, and a save made before the map changed elsewhere (another device, the folder, ideas caught on the go) never writes over that change, even one the map's page made just before the change reached it (each save says which text it was made on), nor one waiting behind a slow write, in whatever order they come: it goes into the history instead, as the map closes too, and Undo brings it back, or, for that last one, a message with the way to the history.
  A map that changes elsewhere as it opens is drawn as that change left it.
  An idea being typed on the map as a change from elsewhere arrives, a new one or one renamed, goes on being typed into the change when the change leaves it where it was; when the change moves it or takes it away, or is a version a newer Orbit made or one Orbit can't read all of, what was typed goes into the map's history, with a message, never lost as its field closes.
  Undo or Redo pressed as an idea is typed keeps the typing first, as a change of its own: Undo takes it back, and Redo brings it again.
- **Maps Orbit can't fully read are only shown.**
  When Orbit can't read part of a map's text, or a newer version of Orbit made it, the map opens read-only with a note saying why, and its file stays exactly as it is until the text is fixed or Orbit is updated.
  Undo, versions brought back, and changes from elsewhere are drawn as they are, never rewritten.
  An idea a newer Orbit wrote as a newer kind of Progress or goal shows as a plain idea, its name inside, and keeps what it was written with.
- **Versions at the right moments:** as each visit to a map begins, every ten minutes of editing, and before big changes: an idea removed with the ones under it, a version brought back, the text edited by hand, a version from elsewhere arriving.
  A newest version that can't be read doesn't stop the next one from being kept.
  A file that comes in as a new map (an import, a share, a new file in the folder) is kept as it came, for good, before Orbit ever writes it, OPML and FreeMind files included; an XMind workbook, a zip that can hold several maps, isn't.
  A newer version of a map that arrives later (from the folder, another device or a file) becomes the map, with the map as it was kept in its history first, and both are thinned out as they age, like any version.
- **A trash:** deleted maps wait 30 days in Settings, Trash, history and all, to be restored or deleted for good (the buttons beside each map where the window has room for them in the font's size, else under it, Restore on a line of its own where both don't fit), a map whose file Orbit can't read among them, listed by its name on the phone.
  A map deleted while it's open becomes read-only, with a way to restore it.
  What was being typed on it as it went is in its history, which comes back with it, and a message says so.
  A clock that's wrong never takes one early: a map goes for good only once it's been seen past its 30 days at two launches a day apart, and never while the clock disagrees with the network's time; one deleted with the clock ahead, or behind Orbit's install, gets its 30 days again from when that's seen.
  One the folder sent there waits for its note to be read, and so does one whose details can't be read, which may say so.
  Deleting for good, one map or the whole trash, says a map went only once it has, its details and history included: one whose files don't all delete, on storage that fails, stays in the trash, its text the last to go, private if it was, and the trash says it couldn't delete it.
  What a deletion for good that Orbit stopped in the middle of left behind goes at the next start.
  A map goes to the trash with its history, or not at all: one that can't, or can't come back from it, stays where it was, and Home, or the map's own screen, says so.
- **Only files nothing points at are cleared:** as a map closes, a picture, video, sound or idea's file Orbit named in its folder that neither the map nor any of its versions points at, by its name or with the name percent-encoded as a link may write it, is deleted; never one added in the last ten minutes, one put there some other way, or any while a version, or the history itself, can't be read.
  The versions are read one at a time, and only when the map itself leaves a file unused.
- **The folder never deletes on a hunch.**
  A map only follows its file out of the folder once two syncs at least ten minutes apart both miss it, and then into the trash.
  A file that can't be read is left for the next sync, writes to the folder are read back before they count, a file this device's write left cut short is written again, and one emptied there gets the map's ideas back.
  A file that's shorter than its map, as deleting the map's last ideas on another device leaves it, is taken once it has stayed as it is for half a minute, so one still arriving isn't.
  Nor is a file that stops partway through a character, or isn't the size the folder said (an empty one too), until it's whole or has stayed that way.
  A file changed since the folder was listed isn't written over or deleted: it's looked at again right before, by its very text before a deletion, and wherever the folder's app gives no times, so another device's edit is kept twice or comes back as a map.
  A write of this device's it couldn't read back is still its own, never kept twice or taken for a new map.
  And a markdown file too big to be a map (over 8 MB) stays only in the folder, as Settings says, even one whose size the folder's app can't tell; while private maps are locked, Settings doesn't name one that is, or may be, a private map's.
- **Pictures, videos, sounds and files within limits:** a video, a sound or an idea's file from the phone may be up to 250 MB, and a map's pictures, videos, sounds and files up to 1 GB together (sizes from 1,000 MB on read in GB).
  Pictures from the phone are kept at most 2048 pixels on their longer side, as JPEG or PNG; an animated GIF or WebP is kept as it is, up to 250 MB, so it still moves (a bigger one is kept still, as other pictures are).
  So a map always fits in its zip and comes back from it, and goes straight to a nearby device: each way takes it a piece at a time, never whole in memory, and only once there's room for it.
  A big video is offered smaller first (see "Big videos made smaller").
  Larger ones are refused, saying why, and nothing of them stays.
  Several coming in at once, a batch chosen together, a drop, or a video still being copied as a file is added, share that room: each counts from the moment its copy starts, so together they never take a map past 1 GB, and the ones that wouldn't fit are refused the same way.
  Ones in the folder maps are kept in get the same room, and those past it stay only there; so do those in a zip of every map, whose maps take what fits and leave the rest in the zip, Import maps saying how many.
  Orbit 0.4.0 and earlier keep 50 MB a file and 90 MB a map: in a shared folder they keep what's past that only in the folder, as ever, and Settings here says, beside such a device, which maps it keeps only part of.
- **Sounds in earlier versions:**
  Orbit 0.3.0 and earlier show a map with sounds as it is, and keep its sounds as they are when they change it.
  A voice memo, and an M4A from the phone, is an `.mp4`, which they carry in zips, backups, the folder and sending too; other sound files go along from this version on.
- **Upgrades and going back:** the app's files carry their layout's version, and an update moves older files to its layout after keeping a copy of every map's text, a version at a time, each whole before it leaves where it was: a move cut short never loses one, and a version by a name the history has for another text moves in beside it.
  A map that can't be moved to the new layout leaves the move to be made again at the next launch, never taken for done.
  Details a newer version wrote stay in a map's `meta.json` when an older one changes it, those inside its other details too, and files a newer version laid out in a way an older one can't change safely are only shown by it, as are files whose record of their layout it can't read.
  A setting a newer version chose stays as it was while an older one changes others, a value it can't read reading as the default; and a backup comes back onto any version, an older one too.
- **Every map in one zip:**
  Export all maps writes every map, private ones too, with its details, pictures, videos, sounds, files and versions, or no zip at all.
  It looks first that every map's files can be read, before the file chosen for the zip is opened, so a backup it would write over is left as it was when one can't, and it says which map (a private one by its cover, and none while private maps hide).
  Each map goes as it was at one moment, its files opened as its text is read, so one deleted, edited or with versions thinned out while the zip is written goes whole, as it was; in a map with more than 256 files, one of the rest that's gone by its turn stops it, rather than be left out.
  Whatever stops it once it's writing, what it had written goes, or is left empty where the app that keeps it won't delete it, and what's there never reads as a whole zip.
  A file chosen to write over that can't be opened is left as it was too.
  Import maps leaves a map that's here already as it is, filling in what it's missing: the one with its identity, or, for one given an identity apart, as two phones' welcome maps are, one with the same text; never one that's another of the zip's maps, or that came in from it.
  A private map's files and versions never go into a map that isn't private: into the one with its identity, open here, only the files its text points at come, which it shows anyway, and its versions stay in the zip, as Import maps says.
  What Export all and Import maps say counts private maps as they are when Settings says it, which can be well after it began: once they've locked and hide, neither counts them among the maps exported, here already, or that files came into.
  A map that changed since comes in beside the one here, private with its cover when that one is, or the zip's; a private copy made open afterwards isn't taken for it again, so importing that zip once more brings it back private beside the open one, rather than put a private map's text into an open one.
  While a private map is locked, Import maps finds it by its identity alone, as its owner's own backups carry it, never by its text: a zip of text someone guessed comes in as a map of its own, as it would on a phone without that map, so what the import says and the list of maps tell nothing of it.
  Such a copy holds only what the zip held, open as it was there.
  An import that has to stop partway, as when the device's storage fills up, says why, then exactly what came in, each map whole and listed, and the files that came into maps here; importing the same file again brings in the rest, leaving what came as it is.
- **Backups:**
  Android's backup keeps the maps and settings, and Settings says what it leaves out, pointing to Export all maps.
  Private maps go into it only when it's encrypted end to end, as Google's is on a phone with a screen lock, or goes straight to a new phone: a cloud backup without a screen lock, or `adb backup`, leaves out their text, details, pictures, videos, sounds, history and place in the trash, and takes every other map as ever.
  It goes by each map as its files are written: one made private while the backup is made stays out from then on, one whose details can't be read stays out too, and a map still being made or copied never goes.
  Such a backup carries how many private maps it left out, a count and nothing else, and after a restore from it Home says so once: with a synced folder, they come back from it as ordinary maps, to make private again, and without one, Import maps brings them back from an Export all, still private.
  Settings' line on Android's backup, and the private maps' note when the phone has no screen lock, say beforehand that it leaves them out.
  A backup to the cloud stops altogether past 25 MB, so it takes the maps' text first, the most recently changed map first, then their pictures, sounds, videos and ideas' files smallest first, as many as fit, and a big video never stops it; past 25 MB of text alone, the maps changed longest ago are left out, and Settings says how many.
  Moving to a new phone takes them all, private ones included; from a phone with Android 12 or later, it takes the maps' history, the trash and what the folder maps are kept in still has to delete too, which a move from Android 10 or 11 leaves out, as a cloud backup does.
  A widget's choice of map stays on its phone, whose widget ids another phone gives other widgets.
  A restore writes each file beside its place, and it takes its place only once all of it came, the folder's deletions too, so one cut short never leaves half a file.
  Settings, Export all maps writes every map, pictures, videos, sounds, ideas' files and history included, exactly as they are, into one zip to keep anywhere, and Import maps brings them back on any device, leaving maps already there as they are: every map, whatever letters and numbers its title has (CO₂, m², ½, ① or Arabic and Devanagari digits), and a folder of the zip with no map in it is said to be left out.
  A map already there takes the pictures, videos, sounds and versions it's missing from the zip, as after a cloud backup that left them out, and keeps the ones it has; why each version was kept comes as the zip says it, what a newer version wrote of it too.
  Details a version couldn't read, kept aside beside a map's, go out and come back with it, and so do a zip's own that this version can't read.
  Either shows on its row while it runs, goes on if Settings closes, and says how it went when it's done, or when Settings opens again; a backup that couldn't be written whole is taken away rather than kept half-done.
  Uninstalling offers to keep the maps.
