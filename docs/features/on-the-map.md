# On the map

- **Getting around:**
  - tap a planet to fly into it;
  - spin the orbit, or pinch to zoom in and out;
  - switch to the overview.
- **Going back:** back and the swipe gesture go up a level, and the map shrinks under your finger.
  Breadcrumbs lead back up too.
- **Picking up where you left off:** a map reopens on the idea and view you left it in.
- **Names that fit:** a name too long for its planet wraps inside it, and a long word breaks with a hyphen where the app's language, English, Spanish or Portuguese, breaks it ("Pho-to-syn-the-sis"), rather than shrinking the name until it's hard to read: once the map is still, a word breaks once if that shows its name at least 2 px larger.
  A name that still doesn't fit a small planet shows on a pill by it, with its icon.
- **Adding ideas:**
  - tap the **+** in the orbit (or the floating +) and type right inside the new planet;
  - Enter adds the idea and opens the next one, so ideas keep coming.
- **Talking ideas onto the map:**
  - hold the floating **+** and talk: each pause, or each sentence where the phone's speech service punctuates, becomes a planet in the orbit in focus as you speak;
  - let go to finish, or slide up while holding to keep listening hands-free until Done; TalkBack's long press does the same.
    Leaving the app, starting a presentation or a study session, or recording a voice memo ends the talk and lets the microphone go.
    The map's screen made again mid-talk, as for a new language or font size, ends the talk too, and its Undo is offered once the screen is back; closing the map ends it with its ideas kept.
    Locking a private map ends it at once, and nothing said after that reaches the map, even what a slow speech service says late;
  - once the talk's last ideas are saved, one Undo takes the whole talk back, and only the talk;
  - it listens through whatever speech service the phone has, not only Google's, in Orbit's language, which Settings can make other than the phone's.
    Where there's only another app's listening screen, that screen hears one go.
    Where there's no speech service at all, as on many phones without Google, the new idea's field opens for typing or the keyboard's microphone.
- **The long-press menu,** where you pressed, once you let go:
  - for a goal, its numbers first: −1 and +1 for a count, with the menu staying open for the next step, or an amount to add, take off or pay in a small dialog;
  - add ideas under or next to an idea, or rename it on the map;
  - give it a date, and a time if you like, in Material's date and time pickers, or change or take them off (see "Dates and reminders", below);
  - set its status, color or icon.
    Icons come from Android's emoji picker, with a search above it that finds emoji by name or keyword, in the app's language and in English, the emoji people use most first.
    A long press on a match offers its skin tones;
  - fold its ideas away, or move it earlier, later, in or out;
  - duplicate it, or delete it with Undo;
  - copy it with everything under it, as text other apps paste as an outline, or paste into it, with one Undo: text becomes ideas under it (a line each, nested as the lines are indented; a markdown list, or an idea copied in Orbit, keeps its shape, to-dos, colors and icons; markdown with one top heading, at any level, written with `#`, or underlined with `===` or `---` when more markdown follows (a list, or another heading), comes as that idea with what's under it, as Orbit reads it, so a line in a note's code block that looks like a heading doesn't count, and with several, as each of them; a line that only underlines or rules off plain text's lines, as `=====` or `-----`, is no idea), an address on a line of its own an idea that opens it, and a picture its picture.
    An idea pasted into another map brings its pictures, videos and sounds, unless that map is private and locked: then it comes as its text alone.
    That's asked again once they're copied, right before the paste lands: a private map that locks meanwhile, the one they came from or the one they go into, keeps them out, and the copies go again.
    When they can't come, past the map's room or with no room left on the device, nothing is pasted, and a message says why.
    A picture the other map already has another of by the same name (two maps from zips can each have a photo.jpg) comes beside it under a name of its own: the pasted idea shows its own, and the map's ideas keep theirs.
    What's copied from a private map is marked sensitive, so Android doesn't show it as it's copied and keyboards keep it out of their clipboard history; one that locks while the copy is made puts nothing on the clipboard, however quickly it's unlocked again.
    Orbit only reads the clipboard when you paste, so Android's notice that it did comes with what you asked for, and an idea dragged within Orbit carries a mark of the run it was dragged in, so another app's drag can't pass for one.
- **Dragging an idea somewhere else:** a long press lifts it, and moving on drags it, its moons with it.
  - Onto another idea: it lights up, and the idea goes inside it, shrinking as it's about to.
    Moved under another idea, a message says where, with Undo.
  - Along the orbit: its place is held where it was, and the other planets make room as it passes them.
    Over a planet's rim it's still the orbit; near the middle, the planet takes it.
  - A light tick on each idea it could land in, and a tap when it lands.
    Undo takes it back like any change.
  - Out of Orbit's window, into another app beside it, it goes on as a drag that app can take: see below.
- **Drag and drop with other apps,** on a tablet, a Chromebook or a phone in split screen:
  - **Onto the map:** text, web addresses, pictures, videos, sounds and map files.
    The idea under the finger lights up as when an idea is moved, with a tick for each, and a pill over the map says what letting go does ("Add to Flights"), moving out of the finger's way.
    Over empty space it's the idea in focus.
  - **What a drop adds,** with one Undo: text becomes ideas under the idea, as pasting makes them, and an address on a line of its own an idea that opens it; one picture, video or sound becomes the idea's, a sound with its caption from its file's name as when it's added from the phone; several become ideas of their own under it, named by their files; a PDF, a spreadsheet or any file Orbit doesn't show joins the idea's files, with every file let go with it; map files open as maps, as Open a file opens them, whatever else comes with them, and the pill says so before they're let go.
    A text file comes whole, up to 512 KB, a list rather than a book: a longer one adds nothing, and the message says to drop it on your maps to make a map of it.
    A video's or a sound's copy says how far along it is, with a way to stop it, and the usual limits hold, with an M4A kept as the MP4 it is.
    Stopped partway, nothing of the drop is added, and what had come in already takes none of the map's room.
    Home takes no sounds: they aren't maps.
    One that takes a while lands on its idea however its text changes meanwhile, and only "Added" once it's there; its idea deleted meanwhile, it says so, and what it copied goes.
    Ideas dropped deeper than a planet, where they wouldn't show, take the map there.
  - **When the other app fails:** a file it no longer lets Orbit read, can't lend, no longer has or fails to give says why ("Orbit isn't allowed to read that file"), and never closes Orbit: of several pictures, videos, sounds or map files, the rest still come in, and text comes in whole or not at all.
  - **Out of Orbit:** an idea lifted on the map and dragged out of the window, or a row of the outline held and moved, goes to other apps as its outline, the text copying it gives, under a planet in its color with "+N" for the ideas that go with it.
    An outline, dragged or copied, nests up to 32 levels, and lists ideas deeper than that at the 32nd, so a very deep branch is never more text than its ideas.
    Let go on its own map instead, into another idea, it moves there, with Undo; the outline's sheet steps aside for it.
    A private map's ideas leave it only while it's unlocked, and one on its way out from the map goes back if private maps lock.
  - **What takes nothing:** a map that can only be looked at, a private map behind its lock, a presentation, a path being made and an earlier version on show.
    A drop still being read or copied in as one of those begins adds nothing, and says so.
    Nor does pasting, from the menu or with Ctrl+V, and Ctrl+X only copies.
    While an idea is typed into, its field takes text as any field does.
- **The toolbar** floats over the map: the outline, search, the overview and styles, then the idea in focus's own buttons (its picture or video, its sound, what it holds, and its editor), and **+**.
  On a map that can only be looked at, the idea's own buttons stay, all but its editor's, in the toolbar and in the bar at the top of a short screen, and Ctrl+E and the keys that change the map are gone, from the map and from the list of shortcuts; nothing opens the editor or the style there.
  On a narrow phone, a map with pictures or videos keeps Style and layout in its menu, so the toolbar stays the same as you fly around.
- **The editor** (the pencil in the toolbar) covers everything an idea can be:
  - its date, with a time if you like, in Material's pickers, or none; under it, when Orbit will remind you, or why it won't;
  - its text, icon, color, status and priority.
    A color is one of Orbit's, one the map's ideas already have (most used first), or any other from the color picker: hue, saturation and brightness, or its hex code, with the idea shown in it.
    The long-press menu offers the same colors;
  - its kind: milestone, question, decision, risk, source, person, progress, which draws a ring that fills as the ideas under it get done, or goal.
    Another kind picked is the kind it becomes, even for an idea written for a newer version of its old kind, which the map draws as no kind until then.
    An idea written for a newer version of a kind shows that kind as chosen, and picking it again leaves the idea as the newer Orbit wrote it.
    TalkBack hears the kinds, like the statuses, as radio buttons, one of them chosen;
  - a goal's numbers, at the top for a goal: saving up to a target in a currency (the phone's, or any other), paying something off, or counting things, like 5 apples.
    The numbers are typed in the phone's language, on the decimal keyboard, and what isn't a number is said under its field, a decimal point twice (1.2.3) or marks that group no digits (1,2.5) too, rather than read as another number.
    Spaces and apostrophes group digits as commas and points do (1 250, 1'250), and where they group none (1 2) the number is said to be none, rather than read as 12.
    A number or word typed a moment before the goal changes how it counts is saved first, for the way it was typed in, and the goal turns around from it; one typed a moment before the idea becomes another kind is saved before the change, never after it, and one typed as a new name gives the idea a new id stays, and saves on it.
    Only what's typed saves: a number with more decimals than a field shows stays as it's written until it's changed;
  - tags, a markdown note with a preview, whose checkboxes check right there as part of the note being written, and a link to a web page or to another idea;
  - a photo or a video from the Photo Picker, a YouTube, Vimeo or video address, and a web page.
    A video from the phone is copied into the map as it is, with its progress showing and a way to stop it, and a frame from it becomes its poster, which its planet shows;
  - a sound: a voice memo recorded right there, a sound file from the phone, or a sound's web address, with a caption (see "Sounds on ideas", below).

  The sheet stops halfway, and the map glides into the room above it as the sheet comes up, so each change shows on the map as you make it.
- **Goals** track a number toward a target.
  A goal's planet shows a ring like a Progress idea's, with its number big in the middle and its target under it ("of 5", "of $3K"), or "left" when it counts down; a goal reached closes its ring and shows a check.
  Numbers are written in the phone's language, money in its currency.
  A Progress idea counts each goal under it by how far along it is, and a goal reached gives a light tap.
  A goal left without a target follows the goals and to-dos under it instead, with a ring like a Progress idea's.
- **Progress rings by themselves:** an idea with a to-do (to do, in progress, blocked or done; other status words, like "primary key", are labels), or a goal with a target, right under it shows a Progress ring without being made one, while it has no kind and no picture or video of its own, and its emoji moves onto its name.
  The ring is only drawn: the map's file never gets a kind for it.
  Each to-do and goal counts once, for every idea above it: an idea whose ring follows the ones under it isn't counted itself.
  A map can turn these rings off in Style and layout, which writes `progressRings: false` in its frontmatter; turning them on again takes the line out.
- **Details:** an idea's note, picture, video, sound, links and files open in a native sheet, from the toolbar or by tapping them on the map.
  Its kind, status, priority, date and tags come first, as words after their emoji, dot or icon: read, not tapped, so they aren't chips, and screen readers hear them as words ("Status: Done"); the sheet's Edit changes them.
  A status reads the same everywhere, without the spaces around it: in the details, the editor, the long-press menu, the outline and the rings.
  On a map that can only be looked at, the details offer no Edit, no step to a goal and no "Write one".
  A goal's details show how far along it is, its numbers in words ("$1,250 of $3,000 · 42% · $1,750 to go"), and −/+ or a way to add an amount.
  An idea whose ring follows the ideas under it says how far along they are ("2 of 4 done · 63%").
  Notes render natively with links: web links open in a tab, and links to other ideas fly there.
  - **Tables** in a note are Material tables: a tonal header row, a line between rows, and each column aligned as the table's row of dashes says.
    A table wider than the details scrolls sideways inside them, and one narrower takes their width.
    TalkBack hears it as a table, its header cells as headings.
  - **Checklists** (`- [ ]` and `- [x]` in a note, at any depth) have Material checkboxes, a checked item dimmed and struck through.
    On a map that can change, a press anywhere on an item checks or unchecks it, and only that line of the map's file changes.
    The box shows its new state at once, and once the change is saved, a message says so with Undo, which takes it back while nothing has changed since.
    On a phone the message comes up over the details, in the sheet itself, so a keyboard's Tab reaches its Undo after the sheet's own buttons and TalkBack finds it with them.
    TalkBack hears each item as one checkbox named by its words.
    A tap on its picture shows it full screen, one on its video plays it, and its sound plays right there, with a slider to move it.
- **Dates and reminders:** an idea can have a date, and a time if you like, kept small: no repeats, no snoozing.
  - **In the map's file,** as `due: "2026-11-20 09:30"`, where other apps read it too, so it goes wherever the map goes.
    It's local time, with no time zone: 9:30 is 9:30 wherever you are.
    Orbit 0.4.0 keeps a date when it changes a map.
  - **On the map:** a small chip inside the planet ("Today", or its time today, "Tomorrow", "Fri", "Nov 20"), in the app's language and on the phone's 12- or 24-hour clock.
    Once past, it turns red, unless the idea is done.
    The details show it among the idea's facts, after a calendar ("Overdue: Mon, Nov 16" once past, in red), and the outline after each idea; TalkBack says it with the idea's name, and whether it's overdue.
  - **Reminders:** when the date comes, at its time or at 9:00 for a date alone, a notification reminds you, on every device that has the map, like a calendar; a tap opens the map at the idea.
    It comes within minutes, without Orbit asking for exact alarms, and at the latest as the phone wakes from a doze.
    A reminder missed while the phone was off comes as it starts again, but never one for a date set, or arriving, already past.
    Reminders follow dates changed anywhere: on the map, in the folder maps are kept in, sent from another device, imported or restored from a version; and they stay through restarts, time and time zone changes and updates.
    Each comes once, however far you travel: one that came doesn't come again where its time is still ahead, and one whose time passed on the way comes as the phone lands.
    A tap opens the very idea, even among ideas of the same name and date, and even once Android stopped Orbit as the screen lock was asked for.
    Done ideas don't remind, and neither do maps in the trash.
  - **Private maps:** a private map's reminder says only "A reminder from a private map", wherever it shows, the lock screen included, and its tap asks for the screen lock before the map opens.
    One already showing as its map is made private says only that from then on, in its place.
    Any other reminder names the idea and its map, and only "A reminder" on a lock screen that hides what notifications say.
  - **Settings** turns reminders off on this device.
    Android 13 and later ask for notifications once, as the first date is set, saying why; Settings offers it again while they're off.
- **Sounds on ideas:** an idea can hold a sound, and its planet wears a small sound wave for it.
  - **Voice memos:**
    "Record a voice memo", in the editor or the long-press menu, opens a recorder under the map: a big button that records, pauses and goes on, the waveform of what you say as you say it, and its time.
    Finish to listen back, then save it or record again; it joins the idea with Undo.
    Closing with a take asks first, and leaving Orbit or a call pauses it.
    During a call, or while another app keeps the phone's sound, it doesn't start or go on, and says why; a take already recorded stays.
    If the phone's recorder stops partway, even as it pauses or goes on, it says so, and what it recorded so far stays, ready to listen to and save; a take goes only when you throw it away or record over it.
    Listening back pauses for a call or another app's sound, and when headphones come out.
    A take outlasts a change of language or font size, ready to listen to and save, and one that couldn't be saved (the map out of room, say) can still be played and saved again.
    The microphone is only asked for, and only used, while you record.
    A memo is AAC in an `.mp4`, about 0.7 MB a minute, and stops where the map runs out of room.
  - **From the phone or the web:** a sound file (MP3, M4A, AAC, Ogg, Opus, WAV, FLAC or WebM) is copied into the map as it is, with its progress showing and a way to stop it, and its name, unless a recorder made it up, becomes its caption.
    A sound's web address plays from there.
  - **Playing:** from the toolbar, the long-press menu, the details or the editor.
    A small player comes up over the map, with the idea's art or picture, its name, its time and a line that waves while it plays, and the map makes room for it; the sound goes on as you fly around, and a tap on the player opens it full screen.
    Playing another sound stops the one before, even when the new one can't play, which then says so.
    It pauses when you leave Orbit, when another app plays, or when headphones come out.
  - **Full screen:** among the map's pictures and videos, the sound shows the idea's picture, or art in its color with its icon, whose rings turn while it plays, with play and pause, ten seconds back and on, a slider, its time and the sound off and on, and share.
    Play stays in the middle, and on a narrow phone or a split screen the buttons close up, then line up in one row; narrower still, beside another app's, the sound's switch goes under the others, which shrink as they must, never below a touch's 48 dp, so none covers another or leaves the window.
    On a phone on its side, the art takes only what the buttons leave beside it, and in a window too narrow for both the buttons have the room.
- **Sketching on a planet:**
  "Sketch" in an idea's long-press menu or its editor turns its planet into a canvas, right where it is.
  - **The moment:** the map flies until the planet fills most of the screen, the rest of the map and its bars dim, and the planet becomes round paper in its own color.
    Done brings the map back the way its flights move, the planet shrinking into its place with the sketch as its picture; the way out in the corner leaves nothing behind, asking first when something was drawn, a stroke still being drawn included.
  - **Drawing:** with a finger or a stylus, the ink a frame or two ahead of the touch where it's going, so it keeps up.
    A stylus draws thinner or wider as it presses, and wider laid flat; a finger draws a little thinner the faster it goes.
    While a stylus is near the screen, and a moment after, a palm resting on it draws nothing, and a stroke a palm began just before the stylus came down goes.
  - **The tools,** in a floating toolbar under the planet, or beside it on a phone on its side: the pen, whose second tap offers the map's own colors, black and white, and three widths; the eraser, which takes the strokes it passes over (as does a stylus's other end, or its button held); undo and redo; clear; and Done.
    With a keyboard:
    P and E, Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z, Ctrl+Enter, and Escape.
  - **Described:** as Done is tapped, a stroke still being drawn is kept as it is, and a field asks what the sketch shows, which screen readers say and web pages and prints carry, as for any picture.
    It comes up above the keyboard, the planet staying where it is behind it; on a phone on its side, where the keyboard leaves little room, its title, field and buttons go in one row.
    The sketch then joins the idea in one change, which one Undo takes back.
    One the map can't show stays off the idea, which keeps the picture it had, and the canvas stays, its drawing whole, to try again.
    So does one the map doesn't take, as when its page stopped: the canvas lifts only once the sketch is on the idea, and on its way to the map's file.
  - **Never lost to a page that stops:** when Android stops the page that draws the map, as it does to free memory, the sketch waits, whole, its description too, and the map's place says it comes back with the map.
    Load it again, and the planet comes up close again with the drawing on it; an idea gone meanwhile ends the sketch, saying so.
    Back asks first, as on the planet, before strokes go.
    Left empty, the field gives the sketch no description, as a picture can have none, and the editor's "Describe it, for screen readers" gives it one later.
    With nothing on the paper, Done asks nothing: a new sketch leaves nothing behind, and one whose strokes were all erased or cleared comes off its idea, saying so, with Undo.
  - **Drawn on further:** an idea whose picture is a sketch offers "Keep sketching" instead, and the canvas reopens with its strokes, to draw on, erase or clear.
    Done without a change keeps the same file, and changes only its description, if that changed.
    A picture of another kind is replaced only after asking, and Undo brings it back; so is one that landed on the idea while it was drawn, a picture chosen before that took a while to copy in.
  - **A plain drawing everywhere:** each sketch is an SVG of its own in the map's folder, a new one each time, so earlier versions keep the one they had: its round paper and the outline of each stroke, a few kilobytes.
    It shows wherever pictures do: on the map, in the details, full screen, on Home's cards and widgets, in PDFs and prints, and in pictures of the map.
    It goes as the SVG it is in zips, in what's sent to other devices, in a presentation shown in browsers in the room (Show) and shared from full screen; it holds nothing but the drawing, so the cleaner every picture going out passes through takes nothing from it: what leaves is the same drawing, written again with an XML declaration (`<?xml …?>`) at its top.
    Export all and the folder maps are kept in keep the file exactly as it is.
    A web page of the map (the one browsers in the room are served too), Orbit's own Print and PDF from the Export panel, and a TV over Google Cast take it as the PNG it draws, 1024 pixels across at most, since Orbit puts no SVG inside a page: an SVG can do more than draw.
    Another device reopens it to draw on, and earlier versions of Orbit show it as any picture.
- **Files on ideas:** an idea can hold files of any kind: a PDF, a spreadsheet, a ticket, a document.
  Its planet wears a small paperclip at its upper left, with how many from two on, readable on every style.
  - **Adding them:**
    Add files in the editor (Android's document picker, any kind, several at once), "Add a file" in the long-press menu, files let go on an idea (a drop with a PDF, a spreadsheet or another file Orbit doesn't show, and every file let go with it), and files shared to Orbit from another app with "Attach to an Orbit idea": they join the idea in focus on the map that's open, or a new map named after the first.
    Text shared there with no file, as a browser shares a page, joins the idea in focus as ideas under it, as pasting it there would, with Undo, or with no map open becomes a new map, as text shared to Orbit does.
    Shared as Orbit comes back, before the map is ready, either waits for it.
    Each is copied into the map as it is, its progress showing with a way to stop it, and they join their idea together, with one Undo.
    A new map made for them that they can't join after all, on a full device say, goes to the trash with them, and Orbit says why.
    A file is up to 250 MB and counts in the map's 1 GB, refused as a video or a sound is, saying why.
  - **Seeing them:** the details list them after the note, each with its kind's icon, its name and its kind and size ("PDF · 48 kB"), in the app's language; TalkBack hears each as its name, kind and size, and that a tap opens it in another app.
    One that isn't on this device, as one sent from an older version arrives without it, is quieter and says "Not on this device".
    It says so only while that's so: when the file comes, from the folder maps are kept in or a zip, its row changes at once, and a tap that finds a file gone says so in its row too.
    The editor lists them the same way, each with a button that takes it off, with Undo.
  - **Opening one:** a tap opens it in an app that can, with a copy, never the map's own file.
    A picture, video or sound held as a file goes clean, as from the viewer; any other kind first asks, "Open “Boarding pass.pdf” in another app?", since Orbit can't take out what a file like it may say beyond what it shows, such as who made it, when and where:
    Cancel is the choice it starts on.
    A copy from a private map is deleted when private maps lock, and the question goes with the lock too.
    A file no app on the device opens says so, and one that isn't on the device (as one an older device in the folder didn't keep) says that.
  - **Where they go:** with the map, in its zip, sent to a device, in Export all, backups and the folder maps are kept in.
    Every way out to other people names them in its line ("2 files stay on this device: Orbit can't take out what may be hidden in them") and asks once, with the pictures it can't clean, whether they go as they are.
    A web page lists them by name, kind and size without carrying them, and says they stayed with the map; PDFs and prints name them too.
    The browsers a map is presented in never get them.
  - **Earlier versions:**
    Orbit 0.4.0 and earlier keep an idea's files, and every field each file says, when they change its map, and keep the files themselves on the device.
    They carry only their own kinds of file in zips, sending, backups and the folder, so in a shared folder this device's record lists the kinds of the ideas' files there, and 0.4.0 says which open only on a newer device; Settings here says the same of an older device beside it.
    Sending a map to one says which files stay here.
- **Pictures full screen:** the toolbar's picture button shows the idea's picture over everything, on the dark, fitted to the screen.
  - Pinch, double-tap where you want a closer look, or use the wheel or + and -, then pan and fling; 0 fits it again.
    Big photos stay sharp at any zoom, read a part at a time.
  - **Animated GIFs and WebPs** play, looping, and zoom like any picture.
    They play only while on screen, and start over each time they come back; the bar at the top pauses and plays them, and so do space and K. With Android's animations off ("Remove animations", in its accessibility settings), they show their first frame with a play button.
    The idea's details play them too, or, with animations off, show their first frame with a play badge.
    On the map, a planet shows an animation's first frame, as the map's pictures on Home and in widgets do, so the map never keeps drawing, nor moves with animations off.
  - **Live photos** play their motion once, without sound, as their page comes up, and settle back on the still; held, they play again with their sound, and the "Live" mark at the top plays them that way too, as do space and Enter (screen readers hear it as a button).
    Leaving the app stops the motion, sound and all, and the still is back, even while it's held; so do headphones coming out while it plays with its sound.
    Zoomed in, they stay still, and zooming in on one playing stops it there.
    With Android's animations off, they play only when asked.
    The idea's details show the mark on its picture; on the map, planets stay still.
  - Swipe, or use the arrow keys, through the map's pictures, videos and sounds in the outline's order, with "3 of 10" at the top.
    A video's page shows its poster and a sound's page its length, and both wait for their play button, or space, Enter or K; a sound playing pauses as another page comes up.
  - A tap hides and shows the controls: close and share at the top (the file itself, or a picture's web address), and the caption and description at the bottom.
    The description is what screen readers say.
    A picture is shared without what its file says beyond the picture, such as where and when it was taken: only which way up it is stays.
    One Orbit can't clean asks first whether it goes as it is, naming it as the one it is, whatever page is on screen by then; a copy there's no room for says so.
  - Back closes it, shrinking as you swipe, and so do Escape and a flick up or down when the picture isn't zoomed, with the map showing through as it goes.
- **Live photos,** a still with the few seconds a phone recorded around it, come in whole, however they were taken, wherever a picture comes in (the editor's picture, pasting and drops), and are kept as two plain files: the picture, as any picture is kept, and its motion beside it as an MP4.
  - **Android phones** keep the motion inside the picture's file, each its own way, and Orbit reads it by what's in the file:
    Google's Motion Photos (JPEG, HEIC and AVIF, and older Pixels' MicroVideo), Samsung's and Huawei's and Honor's.
    The picture loses the video it carried, never anything of the picture.
  - **iPhones** keep two files, the still (HEIC or JPEG) and a QuickTime movie: dropped together, they're joined by the id they share, else by their name, else by being taken within a few seconds of each other, never when each has an id of its own, and a picture and a live photo's movie that nothing ties together are joined if you say so.
    A movie that keeps its sound uncompressed comes whole, however QuickTime describes it, and one whose sound was cut short partway through a frame comes as far as its last whole frame.
    The movie is kept as an MP4, its video as it was and its sound made AAC, so browsers play it too.
  - **Within the limits:** the picture and its motion count together, 250 MB at most and the map's 1 GB in all; a motion past them is left out, and the picture comes alone, with a note.
  - **The latest picture wins:** a picture or an iPhone's movie that takes a while to copy in, from a cloud drive say, never lands over a picture chosen, sketched or taken off after it: it stays out, and its copy goes.
    A caption or description typed meanwhile keeps it coming.
    A movie goes only onto the still it was chosen for.
    One that lands while its idea is sketched on is asked about as Done is tapped, and the sketch takes its place only if you say so, with Undo.
  - **On the idea it was for:** a picture, video, sound or files that take a while to copy in follow their idea as its text changes, never landing on another idea that took its old name; one whose idea was deleted meanwhile says so ("The picture wasn't added: its idea is gone"), its copy goes, and nothing says it was added.
  - **Everywhere maps go:** the motion goes with its picture in zips, sending, Export all, backups and the folder maps are kept in, and in shares without where and when it was taken, as videos go.
    Wherever a way out says what goes with the map, a live photo is named as one, its motion part of it:
    "With 2 pictures and a live photo inside".
    Earlier releases show the picture as any other, and keep and carry its motion, except that 0.3.0 and earlier drop it when they change the picture's caption or description there.
- **HEIC and HEIF pictures** that arrive through the folder maps are kept in, or a zip, as iPhones and some Samsungs take them, show on the map as a JPEG made for the page, which Android's WebView doesn't draw them, and as that JPEG in web pages, prints, SVG and PNG pictures of the map, on a second screen and in the browsers a map is presented in, which browsers other than Safari don't draw either: the file stays as it is.
  Pictures from the phone are made JPEGs as they come in, as always.
- **Big videos made smaller:** a video over about 50 MB, chosen in the editor or let go on the map, comes with an offer:
  "Make this video smaller?
  It's 180 MB.
  Made smaller on this device, at 1080p, it comes to about 62 MB", with "Make it smaller" and "Keep it as it is".
  Several let go together are asked about once.
  Made smaller, it's H.264 and AAC in an MP4, at most 1080 pixels on its shorter side at a bitrate for its size and frames, the way up it was recorded, its sound and its frames as they were, an HDR video made SDR: the editor, or the drop's pill, says how far along it is, from how big to about how big, with Stop, and once it's in the editor says what it came to ("Made the video smaller: 61.4 MB instead of 180 MB").
  Stopped, or failing, it leaves nothing.
  It's offered only where it comes out well under what it was, with an encoder in the phone's hardware, which every phone that records video has: a phone without one keeps the video as it is, and says so.
  A video too big for the map as it is (past 250 MB, or past the map's room) is offered only smaller, when that fits; one that wouldn't fit either way is refused, saying why.
  It's AndroidX Media3's Transformer, open source, in both builds.
- **Videos play in the app:** from the toolbar's play button, the details or the editor, or a video's page among the pictures.
  - Videos in the map and video files on the web play at once, fitted to the screen whichever way the phone turns, with play and pause, ten seconds back and on, the time, a slider and the sound.
    The screen stays on while they play, and they pause when you leave the app.
    The controls step aside while a video plays, as a presentation's do, and stay while TalkBack is on, or while a keyboard's Tab is on one of them.
    Gone, they come back with Tab, on the first of them, or Shift+Tab, on the last, as with a tap.
    In a narrow window, as narrow as Android makes one, back, play and on get smaller, never smaller than a touch.
  - **Open in another app** hands a video in the map to the phone's video app as a copy without where and when it was recorded and on what, made as the button waits; the map's own file stays in Orbit.
    A copy from a private map is deleted when private maps lock, as an idea's file's is.
    One Orbit can't clean, as a video cut short, opens only once the person chooses to, after it says what would go with it, and a copy there's no room for says so.
    A video on the web opens at its address.
  - YouTube and Vimeo videos play in their own players inside the app, YouTube's in its privacy-enhanced mode, with their own controls and fullscreen, and a button to watch them on their site or in their app.
  - Keys: space plays and pauses, the arrows go back and on, and Escape closes.
- **Outline:** the whole map as a list, with each idea's color, its date (red once overdue), progress for every branch, the share of each ring that follows the ideas under it, checkboxes to tick to-dos off (a status that's a label, like "primary key", reads with the tags instead; on a map that can only be looked at, they show whether each is done and take no tap, and a row held opens no editor), and each goal's numbers ("2/5", "$1.3K of $3K") with a small ring.
  It is also how a screen reader or keyboard gets around.
- **Search:** finds ideas by text, tags or notes, shows the path to each, and flies to the one you pick.
- **Style and layout:**
  - Orbit's styles, each previewed with your map: paper and night, flat, Material, liquid glass and glass (each light and dark), cosmos, blueprint, ink and high contrast;
  - "App colors", the default:
    Orbit's Material style grown from your wallpaper's color, so the sun and every branch take tones in harmony with it;
  - color sets, each with every color whole in its row on any phone, down to a 320 dp window and with larger text: the swatches come closer together, then get smaller;
  - whether the map opens on one idea or the whole map;
  - orbit rings, progress rings on ideas with to-dos or goals, a slow drift, and how many levels show.

  The screen around the map takes on its style.
- **Undo and redo** for every change, up to 100 back; a very big map goes back as far as about 8 MB of text holds, and always at least one.
  On a phone, Redo shows in the top bar once something has been undone, so the map's name has room.
  A map that can only be looked at offers neither, nor takes their keys.
  The message a big change leaves (an idea removed with the ideas under it, a version restored) has Undo too, and swipes away to either side.
- **Version history:**
  "Version history" in a map's menu lists the map as it was: a version as each visit begins, one for every ten minutes of editing and one before each big change (removing an idea with ideas under it, editing the text by hand, restoring, a version from another device, a file or the synced folder), the last day's (the newest 50, then one for every ten minutes), then one an hour for a week, one a day for three months, and one a week after that, the one kept before a big change over the others of its stretch, up to the newest 200.
  A busy day never takes the older ones' places, and the newest 20 always stay.
  A file as it came in from elsewhere is never thinned out.
  Versions are named by the clock, and while it disagrees with the network's time (Android 13 and later), none is thinned out.
  - **When, why and what changed:** versions sit on a timeline under their days (Today, Yesterday, a weekday, a date).
    Each says when ("Just now", "12 minutes ago", then its time, in the phone's 12- or 24-hour clock), why it was kept ("Before you removed “Food” and 3 ideas under it", "When you opened it"), and what changed from the one before it: ideas added, removed, renamed and moved, by name when they're few ("Renamed “Hotels” to “Where to stay”") and counted when they're many.
    The map as it is now comes first, with what changed since the latest version, and the list keeps up while it's open: an idea dragged on the map beside it, or a change from elsewhere, shows at once.
    A YAML map's changes read the same, ideas written in brackets included, and one that gains a second idea at its top, which Orbit puts under a center named by its title, has that idea added.
    A version that was already the map as it was before a big change says why.
    Versions kept before Orbit kept reasons show their time and changes only.
  - **Look, then restore:** choosing one shows it on the map to look around in, its badge turning into a cookie.
    Nothing changes it there, so it's never written over the map: changing the map while it's shown (its tags, its name, a goal's step, undo) brings back the map as it is now first, with the history open on it, and presenting, the path, Edit as text, talking and studying close the history.
    Restore makes it the map again, with Undo, and the map as it was joins the history too; Edit as text, open beside it, shows it, and what was typed there a moment before joins the history with the map as it was.
    Restore tapped again, or on a version that's the map already, closes the history and changes nothing more, so one Undo takes the restore back.
    Typing in Edit as text, open beside it, brings back the map as it is now, as any change does, and the typing is drawn on it.
    Looking around a version doesn't change where the map reopens.
  - **Quick on long histories:** what each version changed is worked out as its row comes on screen, so long histories and maps of thousands of ideas open at once, and TalkBack reads each row as one: when, its day, why and what changed.
- **Edit as text:** the map's orbit-md, colored as Orbit reads it, with the map redrawing above as you type and Orbit's notes about the text below.
  It also shows as YAML, Orbit's own form of the map, which says that an edit there writes the markdown anew; switching back without one gives the markdown back just as it was written.
  A note about a line puts the cursor at that line's start, whatever ends the text's lines (Windows' carriage returns included).
  What's typed is kept however soon the editor or the map closes after it, and Undo and Redo wait for it, so they step back from it, never over it, as they do for an idea being typed on the map.
  Text typed on the map as it was before it changed outside the editor (an edit on the map beside it, or a change from elsewhere, even as the text switches between markdown and YAML) is never saved over that change: it goes into the map's history, with a message.
- **Export and share:**
  - a web page: one HTML file with Orbit, the map, its pictures, its live photos' motions and its sounds inside (up to 100 MB of pictures, 25 MB of motions and 25 MB of sounds), which flies in any browser, offline, and presents along the map's path with captions, keys and swipes (videos from the phone stay out of it, and their posters show); Tab reaches its presentation's buttons, which stay while the keyboard is on them, and Enter or Space presses the one it's on.
    What doesn't fit stays out, and Export says so: a picture that stays out leaves its idea without it, and a live photo whose motion stays out shows its still.
    Printed from the browser, it's laid out as the PDF is, with the pictures inside it, whether it was opened from a website or from the saved file itself.
    Its `generator` says which builds of the app and of Orbit made it, as "Orbit for Android 0.4.0 (Orbit 0.3.0, 00c0099)";
  - a PDF, or the map printed: its title and its picture (the current view, the whole map or the map as it opens), then its outline with every idea's details, link, note (its tables at the page's width, its checklists' boxes drawn as they are), picture, video and sound (by its caption, with its address when it's on the web), files (by name, kind and size), and how far its to-dos and goals have come.
    A picture that stays on the phone (one Orbit can't clean, not chosen to go as it is, or one past the page's room) is left off the paper, with its caption, as the map leaves it off its planet, rather than printed as a broken picture; a video's poster that stays leaves the video's name.
    Paper is white, so dark styles print in their light version and the app's colors as they are in light.
    Android's print dialog prints it or saves it as a PDF, with its own Save as PDF even on phones without Google's apps, on the paper it picks for the phone's region or any other.
    Pictures from the web, and a YouTube video's poster, are fetched for it over HTTPS; one that can't be loaded in time, as on a phone offline, is left out, and Orbit says how many were (not counting the map's own pictures kept on the phone, or past what fits, which were left out on purpose); a big map's layout takes none of its pictures' time;
  - a PNG of the current view, the whole map or the map as it opens (on its center, or on the whole map for a map set to open on it), with or without its background;
  - an SVG;
  - markdown, YAML, OPML (the outline, for outliners and other mind map apps, which reads back into Orbit as it was), or a zip with the map's pictures, videos, sounds and ideas' files.
    Markdown, YAML and OPML can also be copied.
    A map written in YAML exports as its own YAML, as it's written, and as the markdown Orbit writes of it.

  A map at its limits, a gigabyte of pictures, videos, sounds and files, exports as a web page or a zip, and opens on a computer, without the phone running out of memory: a zip with all of it, or a page with all it carries inside, about 200 MB, is written a piece at a time into a file of its own, which is saved or shared from there, and a computer's page is made once for each version of the map.
  A picture goes a piece at a time too, however big it is, and so does one shared from the full-screen viewer.
  A phone without room for the file says so before any of it is written.
  A zip past what Orbit 0.4.0 and earlier open (about 100 MB, or a file over 64 MB) says so beside it, in Export and the send sheet:
  "Older versions of Orbit can't open a zip this big: update Orbit wherever it's opened".

  Each way out says what goes with the map, counted:
  "With 3 pictures and a video · 12.4 MB" for the zip, "With 3 pictures and an audio file inside" and what stays on this device for the web page (its videos, pictures past its 100 MB, sounds past its 25 MB, and live photos' motions past theirs, those chosen to go as they are among them), the pictures the PDF prints, and "Just the text" for markdown, YAML and OPML.
  Sharing a map from its card on Home says the same before Android's share sheet opens, when the map has pictures, videos or audio, and offers just its text instead: a markdown file, or for a map written in YAML, the YAML file it is.
  What goes is what the map shows: a web address in it that happens to say `media/…` brings none of the map's files along, and nor does a name written in code, which the map shows as words (the map keeps such a file, in its folder, backups and Export all).
- **What leaves without saying:** pictures, videos and sounds go to other people without what their files say beyond what they show or play, such as where and when they were taken or recorded and on what, and a sound's tags: in shared zips, maps sent to a device, web pages, SVGs, PDFs and prints, the browsers a map is presented or opened in, a picture, video or sound shared from the full-screen viewer, and a video opened in another app; one Orbit can't take that out of, as a picture or video cut short, a file damaged anywhere but at its end, or one it doesn't recognize, such as an idea's PDF, stays on the phone unless the person chooses otherwise.
  As a way out starts at the phone (sharing or exporting a map as a zip, web page, PDF or SVG, sending it, opening it on a computer, presenting it in browsers or on a TV), Orbit asks once, naming the files and what would go with them, whether to leave them out, as it does unless told otherwise, or share them as they are; the answer holds until that way out ends, for those files alone: an SVG carries as they are only the pictures chosen so, never one left out or no one was asked about.
  Each way out's line names them rather than counting them, “A video stays on this device:
  Orbit can't take out where it was recorded.”, and says once they go as they are.
  The full-screen viewer asks the same of the one file it shares, and the video player of the video it opens in another app.
  An answer is about that very file, as it was when Orbit asked: another put in its place under its name since, as a newer version a synced folder brings, wasn't asked about.
  If the new one can go clean, it goes clean; if not, a way out that can ask stops before anything goes and asks again, its question saying first "It changed after you answered, so Orbit asks again."; browsers watching a presentation, a TV and a computer the map is open on, which can't be asked, never get it.
  What's checked is the file Orbit reads, as it opens it and again after its last byte, so nothing of a file that changed meanwhile leaves the phone.
  A file that leads elsewhere through a link in the map's folder isn't the map's, and no way out takes it.
  A browser is never asked, and never gets them: a presentation's browsers get a copy of each file chosen to go as it is, made the first time one asks for it, which goes as the presentation ends.
  Pictures keep their pixels, color profile and which way up they are, and an animated PNG's or WebP's frames, with none of the chunks other apps keep their own in, such as content credentials, nor a JPEG's thumbnails, which can show what an edit took out; HEIF and AVIF pictures have their EXIF, XMP and every other item that only says things about them written over where they are, whatever those are named, an animated AVIF's movie is cleaned as a video is, and they leave without the video a motion photo keeps inside them, a phone's own trailer and other apps' boxes; GIFs keep their frames, timing and looping without comments or other apps' blocks such as XMP; SVG drawings keep only what draws them, the text diagrams lay out included, without their metadata, comments or an editor's own elements and attributes, nor scripts, links or anything they'd run or fetch from elsewhere, and the pictures inside them go clean too; and BMPs keep their picture and color profile, without what an app keeps around them.
  What a HEIF or AVIF picture's items are named, which an app may make anything, as where it was taken, says nothing either.
  Nor do the app that wrote one, a description, name or tags someone gave it, or when it was made, changed or taken, which its properties may say.
  Every video and sound keeps every frame where it is, as what it says beyond them is written over where it is:
  - MP4 and QuickTime videos, and voice memos and other sounds in MP4: the boxes cameras and phones write their place, date and make into become free space, in the movie, its tracks, their media and its fragments, and so does what free space held; their headers' dates are zero, and the names cameras give themselves in a track's handlers and a video's description say nothing;
  - WebM videos and sounds: their tags, chapters, attachments, title, date, and their files' and tracks' names become empty space, and the apps that made them are named with nothing; cut short, their index and cues say nothing of what was cut away;
  - MP3s and AAC: their ID3 tags (title, artist, cover, chapters, comments) say nothing, and the ID3v1, APE and Lyrics3 tags after them stay out;
  - FLAC: its tags, pictures, cue sheet and other apps' blocks become padding, and one with anything between its frames, which players skip, stays on the phone;
  - Ogg (Vorbis, Opus, Speex and FLAC): their comments are emptied, a cover among them;
  - WAV: the chunks that make the sound stay (its format, samples, cue points, loops and instrument), and every other one, a Broadcast Wave's originator and when it was recorded, its tags, iXML, XMP and ID3 among them, becomes empty space, as does the byte of padding after a chunk of an odd size.

  What an encoder writes into the sound or the picture itself, as its name in an MP3's first frame, stays, and a video keeps all of its tracks, so a GPS track an action camera or a drone records beside the picture goes with it, as the privacy policy says.
  A sound cut short at its end, as a recording that stopped or a download that didn't finish, goes clean too, as far as its last whole frame, page or sample: the unfinished bit at its end, which no player can play, stays out, tags it was cut in among it, and the file says where it ends now.
  A voice memo, a video in MP4 or a HEIF or AVIF picture cut short stays on the phone, as it can't be played or shown without all of it.
  The map's own files stay as they are, and so does Export all, a backup.
