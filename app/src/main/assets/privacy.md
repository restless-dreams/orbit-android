# Privacy policy

*Last changed on November 5, 2026.*

**Orbit keeps your maps on your phone.** It has no accounts, no ads and no tracking. It doesn't send your maps, or anything about you, to us or to anyone else. Apart from a daily look for new versions in Orbit from its website (see Updates), and the anonymous information about casting that Google's Cast library sends in Orbit from Google Play (see Casting to a TV), what leaves your phone is only what you send, as below.

## What stays on your phone

- **Your maps,** with their pictures, their sounds, their versions and the trash, in Orbit's own storage, which other apps can't read. Orbit has no account and no cloud, so your maps are only on this device: they reach others only as below, when you keep them in a folder, send, share or show them, or when your phone backs them up.
- **Voice memos** you record for an idea. Orbit uses the microphone, once you allow it, only while you record one, never in the background, and the memo stays in its map, like its pictures.
- **Your settings.**
- **Crash reports,** if Orbit ever closes unexpectedly. They say what went wrong and which versions of Orbit and Android you have, never what's in your maps, and they leave your phone only if you share one.

## What leaves your phone

- **A folder you keep maps in.** Orbit writes your maps there as markdown files, with their pictures, videos and sounds exactly as they are. Where they go from there is up to the app that holds the folder: your phone's storage, a memory card, or a sync or cloud app. Orbit also keeps a small record of this device there, in a hidden folder named .orbit, so your other devices with Orbit can tell what it opens: a random id Orbit makes for this install, which also names the record, the name you choose in Settings, its kind (phone, tablet or computer), its version of Orbit, the kinds of file it carries and how big they may be, and when it last synced. The id stays the same while Orbit is installed, unless you clear its storage, and it isn't backed up, so a new phone gets a new one. When you study a map, Orbit also keeps there how each of its cards is going, so your other devices pick up where you left off: a record for each map you study, kept under this install's id and named after the map's file. It says which of the map's ideas you've studied, how well you know each, when you first studied it, when it comes back and when you last answered it, and the names the map's file had before, up to eight, so your answers follow the map when its file is renamed.
- **Sharing and exporting.** Maps, pictures, sounds and files you share go to the app you pick. A map goes with its own pictures, videos and sounds: before you share one, Orbit says what goes with it, and you can share just its text. Pictures, videos and sounds leave without what their files say beyond what they show or play, such as where and when they were taken or recorded and on what, and a sound's tags, such as its title, artist and cover, whether they go in a map's zip, a web page, a PDF or a print, to another device, to browsers, on their own from the full-screen viewer, or to another app you open a video in. A video keeps all of its tracks, though, so a GPS track some cameras record beside the picture, as action cameras and drones do, goes with it, and the name of the app that encoded a sound or a video goes with it where that app wrote it into the sound or the picture itself. A sound cut short at its end, as a download that stopped, leaves clean too, without the unfinished bit at its end, which can't be played anyway. That's not so for a voice memo, or another sound in MP4 such as an M4A: cut short, it can't be played at all, as an MP4 video can't. A file Orbit can't take that out of, as a picture, an MP4 video or a voice memo cut short, or one it doesn't recognize, stays on your phone: wherever you share, send, show or present a map, or share a picture, video or sound from the full-screen viewer, or open a video in another app, Orbit says so first, and shares it as it is only if you choose to. Browsers that join are never asked, and never get it. Export all maps, a backup of your own, keeps everything exactly as it is. Files you add to ideas, such as PDFs and spreadsheets, are files Orbit can't clean: wherever a map goes to other people, Orbit names them, and they go as they are only if you choose to. Opening one in another app asks first, unless it's a picture, video or sound Orbit can clean, and hands that app a copy, never the map's own file; a copy from a private map is deleted when private maps lock. A web page of a map lists its files without them.
- **Nearby devices and screens.** While Orbit is open, it runs a small server on your Wi-Fi and announces itself there with the name you choose in Settings (your device's name until you change it), so your other devices can find it and send you maps. You choose whether to take each one, and Settings can turn this off. It runs too when you send a map, present in a browser or open a map on a computer. Maps sent between your devices are encrypted end to end, so others on the Wi-Fi can't read them on the way. Before a map goes, both devices show a code, and it leaves your phone only once you've checked that they match, so it goes to the device you meant and to no other. None of this goes through the internet, and other apps on your phone can't reach Orbit's server. Someone on the same Wi-Fi who has the code on your screen can see what you're showing, the pictures and videos you show them included, and Orbit stops the server when you're done.
- **Casting to a TV, in Orbit from Google Play.** Google's Cast library starts only when you present a map. From then until Orbit closes, it looks for TVs on your Wi-Fi while Orbit is on screen, so you can present on one. When you present on a TV with Google Cast, the map is sent to the TV. The library also sends Google anonymous information about casting, such as when TVs are found and presentations start and end, with your phone's and Orbit's versions, to keep Cast working well. Nobody can turn that off; [Google's privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy) covers it. Orbit from F-Droid, AppGallery or a download has no Google code, and shows presentations on TVs through your phone's own screen casting instead.
- **Talking ideas onto a map.** What you say goes to your phone's speech recognition, which may send it to its maker (such as Google) to turn it into words. Orbit only gets the words.
- **Pictures, videos and sounds from the web in a map.** Your phone gets them from their sites when it draws a map that has them, in your list of maps or a widget too, and when you open or play them, and the sites see that it asked.
- **YouTube and Vimeo videos** play inside Orbit, in YouTube's and Vimeo's own players: YouTube's in its privacy-enhanced mode, so what you watch there doesn't shape what YouTube shows you, and Vimeo's told not to track, with no third-party cookies in either. They see what your phone plays, and that it's played in Orbit, as YouTube asks apps to say. A YouTube video's preview picture comes from YouTube whenever a map that has it is drawn, so YouTube sees which videos your maps have. YouTube's player is part of YouTube's API Services, so [YouTube's terms of service](https://www.youtube.com/t/terms) and [Google's privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy) apply to it, and [Vimeo's privacy policy](https://vimeo.com/privacy) to Vimeo's. Vimeo protects its videos, so its player may ask your phone's protected-content system, as a browser's would, and nothing else of your phone.
- **Links** open in your browser.
- **Backups.** If your phone backs up its apps (Android's backup, or moving to a new phone), your maps and settings are part of it, under your phone's backup settings. Private maps go into it only when it's encrypted end to end, as Android's backup to Google is on a phone with a screen lock, or when you move to a new phone; after restoring one that left them out, Orbit says so. Your maps' versions and the trash are left out of online backups, as are pictures, sounds and videos that don't fit in them. Moving from a phone with Android 12 or later to a new one takes everything: the versions and the trash too, and what Orbit still has to delete from a folder you keep maps in. From a phone with Android 10 or 11, the move leaves those out, as an online backup does.

## Updates

When you download Orbit from its website or from GitHub, no app store updates it, so it looks for new versions itself: once a day, it asks GitHub for its latest release. GitHub sees that a phone asked, and from which internet address, as any website would. Orbit sends nothing about you, your phone or your maps, and you can turn it off in Settings. Orbit from Google Play, F-Droid or AppGallery never asks: its store updates it.

## Keeping it safe

- **Your maps and settings** are in Orbit's own storage, which other apps can't read.
- **Private maps** open with your phone's own fingerprint, face or screen lock. Android checks them: Orbit never sees your fingerprint, face, PIN, pattern or password, only whether it was you. Private maps are locked in Orbit, not encrypted, so in a folder you keep maps in, or in a backup, they're plain files like any other map.
- **Reminders** of ideas' dates are notifications Orbit posts on your phone itself, once you allow them: nothing leaves your phone for them. A private map's reminder says only that it's from a private map, and opens the map behind its lock.
- **Maps sent between your devices** are encrypted end to end, and leave your phone only once you've checked that both devices show the same code.
- **Presenting in a browser or opening a map on a computer** isn't encrypted, because browsers can't check a phone's identity: anyone on the same Wi-Fi who has the code on your screen, or who can read the Wi-Fi's traffic, can see what you show. Someone who can read that traffic can also see the PIN as it's typed and join with it, or change what those browsers show, so present this way on Wi-Fi you trust. Orbit's server only answers devices on your own network, and it stops when you're done.
- **What Google's Cast library sends** is encrypted.

## How long things are kept

- **On your phone,** everything stays until you delete it. Deleted maps wait in the trash for 30 days, then they're gone for good, with their history. Uninstalling Orbit deletes its storage, unless you choose to keep it when Android asks. Maps in a folder of yours stay in that folder, and so do this device's records there.
- **Nothing of yours reaches us,** so there's nothing we keep or could delete.
- **Google** keeps the anonymous casting information only for a short time, then combines it into overall figures.

## Children

Orbit collects nothing from anyone, children included, and the anonymous casting information Google's Cast library sends doesn't identify anyone.

## Changes

If this changes, the new version comes with an update of Orbit, with a new date at the top.
